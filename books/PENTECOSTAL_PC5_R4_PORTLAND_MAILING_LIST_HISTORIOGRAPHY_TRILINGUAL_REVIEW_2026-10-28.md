## Documentary findings and confidence

1. **High confidence, narrow fact:** The May 1908 Los Angeles issue, Vol. II No. 13, printed p. 2, directs future correspondence for the paper to the Apostolic Faith Campmeeting in Portland. Portland's successor organization acknowledges that the first Portland-issued paper followed in July 1908. Source: Apostolic Faith Church, “Florence Crawford's Testimony,” https://www.apostolicfaith.org/doctrinal-and-historical/florence-crawfords-testimony . The institutional page describes the original issue and reproduces a picture; a full-resolution original page has not been independently collated for this handoff.
2. **Contested, not an established fact:** The CTS paragraph currently says Clara Lum took the **national and international mailing lists**, then asserts Seymour **lost his means** of reaching the wider movement. The Portland Apostolic Faith Church's institutional history instead says Azusa transferred **two of twenty-two copies** of its mailing list and assigned the paper's publication to Crawford. Amos Morgan's `Azusa Street Mission Time Line` independently argues that more complete copies remained in Los Angeles than went to Portland, and cautions against the familiar theft account: https://azusabooks.org/time.shtml (especially “Bumps in the Road” and “My Defense”). This is evidence of a genuine historiographical dispute, **not** proof that Portland's account is the final truth. Conversely, popular accounts do allege a damaging loss of contact and contested control: https://www.azusastreet.org/The_Newspaper.htm . Do not describe one interpretation as settled documentary fact.
3. **Chronology and motive remain uncertain:** The Portland source places the move/transfer in 1908. Morgan places Seymour's later visit to Portland around 1911, while other narratives place a request for lists nearer the split. Both marriage dates and causal claims vary among secondary accounts. CTS's statement that Seymour married Jennie Evans Moore **in May 1908** is appropriately general; it should not convert the speculative claim that the marriage caused the publishing split into a proven fact.
4. **Independent cross-check:** Assemblies of God (USA) official history confirms the April 1914 organizing meeting in Hot Springs, Arkansas; CTS R4 question 32's historical location is independently supported: https://ag.org/About/About-the-AG/History/ . This does not validate the private answer bank.

## Proposed replacement for R4 section 5's disputed narrative (faculty copy, not live text)

**EN**

> The May 1908 Los Angeles issue directed readers to Portland, Oregon, for subsequent issues of *The Apostolic Faith*. Florence Crawford was leading a growing Pentecostal work there, and Clara Lum, an important contributor to the Los Angeles paper, moved to Portland. Publication continued there under Crawford's leadership. Historians disagree about how the mailing lists and publishing responsibility were transferred. Portland's institutional history says that two of twenty-two complete mailing lists were transferred with authorization; other histories describe the move as depriving Seymour of important contacts. The surviving accounts do not establish here that all national and international lists were taken, or that a single motive explains the separation. Seymour married Jennie Evans Moore in May 1908, but the claim that the marriage caused the break is disputed. The change in the paper's publishing center is documented; responsibility for the conflict and its precise consequences require more careful source comparison.

> El número de mayo de 1908 publicado en Los Ángeles indicó a sus lectores que dirigieran la correspondencia de los números siguientes de *The Apostolic Faith* a Portland, Oregón. La publicación continuó allí bajo el liderazgo de Crawford. Los historiadores discrepan sobre cómo se transfirieron las listas de correo y la responsabilidad editorial. La historia institucional de Portland afirma que se entregaron, con autorización, dos de veintidós copias completas de las listas; otros relatos describen el traslado como una pérdida de contactos importantes para Seymour. Los testimonios disponibles no establecen aquí que se llevaran todas las listas nacionales e internacionales ni que un solo motivo explique la separación. Seymour se casó con Jennie Evans Moore en mayo de 1908, pero se discute que el matrimonio causara la ruptura. El cambio de centro editorial está documentado; la responsabilidad del conflicto y sus consecuencias precisas requieren una comparación más cuidadosa de las fuentes.
