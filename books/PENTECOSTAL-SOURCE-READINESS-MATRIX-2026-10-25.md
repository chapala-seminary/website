## Cross-course risks that require one coordinated gate

1. **One newspaper corpus is being asked to support three courses.** A-C1, A-C3 and A-C5 draw from overlapping issues of *The Apostolic Faith*. Separate course titles do not create separate academic content. Before any examination bank is written, map every assigned passage by issue, page, heading, start/end text and topic so students are not tested repeatedly on the same material.
2. **Seymour's death does not clear every contribution.** The issues contain named letters, signed reports, quoted testimony and unsigned editorial material. A parent-article byline check is insufficient; embedded passages need their own provenance and rights disposition.
3. **Raw corpora are not student readings.** A-C3 has 138,451 source tokens, A-C4 has 230,771 and A-C5 has 89,505. These totals describe archival staging, not approved workloads. Fail closed if an import still identifies a file as OCR, source corpus, issue transcript or storage chunk.
4. **Modern reproductions are not substitutes for originals.** Readable PDFs and modern edited books help locate passages, but final wording, page references, attribution and omissions must be checked against original facsimiles. Do not translate OCR errors or assess names, dates, doctrine or quotations that have not been collated.
5. **Historical claims require explicit framing.** Healing, languages, visions, numerical growth, racial integration, mission success and “no medicine” language are claims made by historical participants or editors. They must not be presented as verified medical, linguistic or statistical findings. CTS materials must reject unsafe medical inference and contextualize race, gender, disability, class and mission power.

## Minimum source-manifest fields before coursebook drafting

For each textbook passage and each of five readings, record:

- course code and course purpose;
- parent work, edition, issue/date and original archive URL;
- exact original page span and start/end wording;
- contributor, speaker and embedded quoted contributors;
- contributor death-date or permission evidence and Mexico/US disposition;
- modern-transcription or OCR source, plus every material correction;
- final student word count and estimated printed pages;
- overlap with the other four Pentecostal electives;
- allowable uses: link, quotation, full reproduction, translation and assessment;
- checksum of the approved English source and later ES/FR artifacts.

No item should proceed to translation or a 40-question bank while any required field is unresolved.

## Release condition

A course may move from archival staging to academic review only when it has one identified textbook or transparently CTS-authored coursebook, five approved substantial readings, page-level provenance, manageable student workload, rights disposition for every contribution, corrected English source text, nonduplication mapping, historical safeguards and a private assessment plan. Production release still requires Wayne's explicit academic and rights approval.
