## Textbook

**John Wesley, _A Plain Account of Christian Perfection, as Believed and Taught by the Reverend Mr. John Wesley, from the Year 1725 to the Year 1777_.**

- Author: John Wesley (1703–1791).
- Revision represented: 1777.
- Edition represented by the transcription: _The Works of John Wesley_, ed. Thomas Jackson, 1872, vol. 11, pp. 366–446.
- Full-text source: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/w/wesley/perfection/cache/perfection.txt
- Committed selection: the complete author text beginning “1. What I purpose…” through the dated conclusion of “Brief Thoughts on Christian Perfection,” excluding CCEL metadata, indexes, generated file links, and the modern CCEL/Wesley Center credit line.
- Approximate committed length: 33,777 words, including historical editorial matter embedded in the 1872 text.

## Five readings

1. **Formation of Wesley's doctrine (sections 1–6).**
   - Start: “1. What I purpose in the following papers…”
   - End immediately before: “7. In the same sentiment did my brother and I remain…”
   - Focus: the 1725–1738 development of Wesley's thought through Taylor, à Kempis, Law, Scripture, and early preaching.

2. **The first tract and early controversy (sections 10–12).**
   - Start: “10. The first tract I ever wrote expressly on this subject…”
   - End immediately before: “13. Not long after, I think in the spring, 1741…”
   - Focus: Wesley's first explicit account, objections, scriptural argument, and correspondence.

3. **Hymns, conference, and doctrinal definition (sections 16–18).**
   - Start: “16. The hymns concerning it in this volume are too numerous…”
   - End immediately before: “19. At the Conference in the year 1759…”
   - Focus: hymnody, the 1744 Conference, questions and answers, and the doctrine's relation to justification and sanctification.

4. **The 1759 Conference examination (section 19).**
   - Start: “19. At the Conference in the year 1759…”
   - End immediately before: “20. In the year 1762, there was a great increase…”
   - Focus: Wesley's extended catechetical definition of perfection, sin, love, growth, assurance, and pastoral cautions.

5. **Wesley's mature summary (sections 26–28).**
   - Start: “26. In the year 1764, upon a review of the whole subject…”
   - End immediately before: “BRIEF THOUGHTS ON CHRISTIAN PERFECTION.”
   - Focus: propositions, final synthesis, and the doctrine's relation to justification, sanctification, and love.

## Content and academic warnings

- This is a primary theological source, not a neutral survey. Course materials should distinguish Wesley's claims from later Wesleyan, Methodist, Nazarene, and holiness interpretations.
- Preserve Wesley's distinctions among justification, sanctification, Christian perfection, infirmity, temptation, and sin; do not reduce “perfection” to sinless impeccability.
- Historical correspondence and reported experiences should be labeled as Wesley's evidence and interpretation, not independently verified fact.
- Retain archaic language where needed for fidelity, but explain obsolete terms in the CTS digest.
