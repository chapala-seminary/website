## Academic and operational decisions

1. **Critical chronology / attribution:** Q08 concerns a **1899** resignation. The assigned CTS digest does teach it, but Armitage's cited source was published in **1887** and Armitage died in **1896**. Q08 must be labeled **later CTS historical commentary**, not attributed to Armitage. A general publication-year chronology lint in source-to-exam QA could flag this automatically across all twenty electives; it would not replace faculty fact-checking.
2. **Numerical OCR:** Q37's current CTS digest states the Massachusetts vote as **187 to 168**, but the proposed Armitage OCR has a corrupted number. Preserve the assigned digest while a faculty editor collates the original printed page and independent historical record; do not infer an accepted answer from this public audit.
3. **Student fairness:** All forty topics are findable in the current English assigned digests, so the 20 proposed-excerpt gaps **do not establish a present reading-test defect**. Replacing the digests with staged OCR or independent-author readings would require a new 40-item mapping, faculty approval, native-language review and private-bank revalidation. Preserve existing credits.
4. **Five readings versus five authors:** These are five CTS historical digests drawn mainly from **one Armitage book**. Five cards do not mean five independent historical authors. Decide whether the approved requirement intends five units of one work or five independent scholarly voices; either approach can be legitimate if honestly described and assessed.
5. **French and Swahili:** A public question translation alone does not guarantee students can read all five assigned digests in that language. Test the actual live room with synthetic French/Swahili students, confirm substantive reading body, instructions, answers and durable credit. If unavailable, correct the availability claim only after owner/faculty policy review.
6. **Private assessment:** Authorized key custodian, not this public audit, must verify all forty accepted variants, translation aliases, 20-of-40 draw, 18-pass rule, retries, synthetic exact-course receipts and idempotent saved credit. Do not publish answer-bearing data or change student records.

## Original-source identity and Mexican copyright screening — preliminary, NOT clearance

Thomas Armitage, *A History of the Baptists: Traced by Their Vital Principles and Practices, from the Time of Our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ to the Year 1886* (New York: Bryan, Taylor & Co., **1887**), is identified by Open Library, edition `OL23378233M`, Internet Archive `ahistorybaptist00armigoog` (978 pages): https://openlibrary.org/books/OL23378233M/A_history_of_the_Baptists ; https://archive.org/details/ahistorybaptist00armigoog . The Library of Congress separately catalogs a **different 1893 abridged edition** (605 pages): https://www.loc.gov/item/unk82042786/ . **Do not substitute 1893 pagination or wording for the assigned 1887 edition.** Original scan/page collation of the selected 1887 passages remains incomplete.

Armitage died in **1896**. Mexico's current federal copyright law (Cámara de Diputados consolidation, last reform **15 January 2026**), article 29, states the general patrimonial term of the author's life plus **100 years**: https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFDA.pdf . His own 1887 expression is therefore a strong **preliminary public-domain candidate** on the ordinary term calculation; this is **not** blanket clearance for a scan's contractual notices, modern annotations, inserted quotations, separately authored introductions, CTS digests, illustrations, machine-assisted translations or future edition changes. Identify and clear each separately, including moral rights/attribution and any applicable exceptions. The original 1887 edition's 978-page identity was independently checked; no page-by-page transcription collation or lawyer's opinion was performed.
