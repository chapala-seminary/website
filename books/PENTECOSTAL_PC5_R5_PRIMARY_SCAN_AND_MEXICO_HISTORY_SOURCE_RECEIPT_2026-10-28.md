## Newly collated original issue, not merely a modern transcription

**Primary facsimile:** *The Apostolic Faith* (Los Angeles), vol. I no. 12, **January 1908**, **printed page 2**, bottom of the first column, a short unsigned editorial correction of the prior Portland revival report. Flower Pentecostal Heritage Center's four-page original-issue scan:  
https://archives.ifphc.org/DigitalPublications/USA/Independent/Apostolic%20Faith%20%28Azusa%20Street%29/Unregistered/1908/FPHC/1908_01.pdf

The actual page image, not only extracted OCR, was inspected. It confirms the existence and heading of the correction, the retraction of an earlier reported baptism count, the statement that no count record was kept, the qualification of the earlier healing claims, and the instruction not to overstate subsequent reports. These facts support the *historical-report-reading* purpose of PC5 R5 and its associated public-question range, **without certifying the private accepted answers**.

## Separate twentieth-century Mexican-history source

PC5 R5 §6 mentions **Romana Carbajal de Valenzuela** and a return to Chihuahua in **1914**. The **Iglesia Apostólica de la Fe en Cristo Jesús (IAFCJ)** itself recounts her work as part of its **Oneness/Apostolic Pentecostal** origins; it identifies her family mission at Villa Aldama, Chihuahua, and the 1914 beginning. This is **not** evidence that she uniquely founded *all* Pentecostalism in Mexico. The current CTS wording “an early Pentecostal work” is appropriately cautious, but a faculty source note should identify the denominational scope instead of citing unnamed “standard histories.”

- IAFCJ institutional history: https://publicaciones.iafcj.org/article/nosotros
- IAFCJ historical biography: https://historia.iafcj.org/Romana_Carbajal_de_Valenzuela

**Proposed EN clarification for academic review:** “One early Mexican Oneness Pentecostal community traces its beginnings to Romana Carbajal de Valenzuela, who returned to Villa Aldama, Chihuahua, in 1914, according to the IAFCJ's own history.”  
**ES:** “Según la historia de la propia IAFCJ, una de las primeras comunidades pentecostales unicitarias de México remonta sus orígenes a Romana Carbajal de Valenzuela, quien regresó a Villa Aldama, Chihuahua, en 1914.”  
**FR:** “Selon l'histoire publiée par l'IAFCJ elle-même, l'une des premières communautés pentecôtistes unicitaires du Mexique fait remonter ses origines à Romana Carbajal de Valenzuela, revenue à Villa Aldama, dans le Chihuahua, en 1914.”

These are editorial proposals, **not** approved replacements. Modern IAFCJ prose is used for attribution and paraphrase only; no wholesale republication or translation is licensed.
