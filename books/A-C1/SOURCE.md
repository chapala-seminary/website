## Five readings

1. **“Pentecost Has Come”** — No. 1 (September 1906), page 1. Start at the heading “Pentecost Has Come.” End immediately before “BRO. SEYMOUR'S CALL.”
   - URL: https://azusabooks.org/af/LA01.shtml
   - Focus: the Los Angeles revival's setting, early meetings, and the paper's own account of expansion.

2. **“Bible Pentecost” / “Gracious Pentecostal Showers Continue to Fall”** — No. 3 (November 1906), page 1. Start at “Bible Pentecost.” End immediately before the first group of short standalone reports following the main opening article (after the paragraph ending “pray for those who are seeking”).
   - URL: https://azusabooks.org/af/LA03.shtml
   - Focus: worship practice, class and racial inclusion, lack of formal organization, and the role of testimony.

3. **“Seven Months of Pentecostal Showers. Jesus, Our Projector and Great Shepherd.”** — No. 4 (December 1906), page 1. Start at that heading. End immediately before “PENTECOST AMONG THE YOUNG PEOPLE.”
   - URL: https://azusabooks.org/af/LA04.shtml
   - Focus: Bonnie Brae/Azusa chronology, Seymour and Lucy Farrow, the break with Parham's leadership, and early mission structure.

4. **“Beginning of World Wide Revival”** — No. 5 (January 1907), page 1. Start at the issue title. End immediately before “PENTECOST RESTORED.”
   - URL: https://azusabooks.org/af/LA05.shtml
   - Focus: retrospective on 1906, international expansion, interracial language, inter-mission cooperation, and early organizational practice.

5. **“Pentecost Both Sides the Ocean”** — No. 6 (February–March 1907), page 1. Start at the issue title. End immediately before “PENTECOST IN OTHER LANDS.”
   - URL: https://azusabooks.org/af/LA06.shtml
   - Focus: transnational networks, newspapers, named workers, missionary mobility, and the spread of local missions.

## Content and editorial warnings

- Treat healings, supernatural languages, visions, and numerical claims as claims made by participants or editors, not independently verified medical, linguistic, or statistical findings.
- No. 5 says mission members “never take medicine.” CTS must explicitly reject using this historical claim as medical advice and must not reproduce it without critical context.
- Retain historically important references to race, disability, nationality, and missions, but explain dated or offensive terminology.
- Distinguish primary-source testimony from later historical conclusions.
