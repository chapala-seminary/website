## Verified issue in the current handoff

The `textbook.md` file contains a signed mission report ending **“A.G. Garr and Wife.”** (No. 2, report from Danville; the paragraph begins “When we came here from Los Angeles…”). Alfred Goodrich Garr died in **1944**. The same reader includes a quoted statement introduced **“Sister Florence Crawford says…”** (No. 2); Florence Louise Crawford died in **1936**. A separate paragraph ends **“Mrs. Anna Hall.”**; her death date has not been checked. These are not all anonymous words of William J. Seymour.

Under Mexico's **Ley Federal del Derecho de Autor**, Art. 15 says publication in a newspaper does not remove the author's protection. Art. 29(I) generally gives identified authors protection during life plus 100 years; Art. 29(II) also provides a publication-based term, but must not automatically be applied to identifiable authors. Consequently **the entire periodical is not established to be free for Mexican reproduction merely because the issues date to 1906**, or because Seymour died in 1922. The signed Garr text presents a concrete unresolved case; if the life-plus-100 rule applies, the term would run beyond 2026.

Official law: https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFDA.pdf (Arts. 15 and 29). Cross-check: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/es/legislation/details/20225
