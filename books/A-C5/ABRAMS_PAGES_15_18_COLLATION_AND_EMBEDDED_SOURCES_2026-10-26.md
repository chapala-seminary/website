## Material boundary correction

The earlier handoff stated that Daniel Awrey’s “Life Sketches” begins on printed p. 19. Image inspection of printed p. 18 shows a more important boundary: Abrams’s article ends in the **upper portion of the right column on printed p. 18**, and Awrey’s separately authored article begins **lower on that same printed page**. Therefore:

- the safe Abrams unit is a bounded article segment spanning part of printed p. 13 through part of printed p. 18;
- printed p. 18 must be cropped or transcribed only through Abrams’s closing appeal;
- no whole-page extraction of printed p. 18 is acceptable;
- the Awrey title, byline, and text must be excluded from the Abrams reading.

This correction supersedes any instruction that treated printed p. 18 as wholly Abrams or that placed the Awrey boundary only at p. 19.

## Embedded-source ledger

1. **Habakkuk 3:17 (printed p. 15):** extended Scripture quotation inside Abrams’s account. Edition wording and publication rights must be tracked separately from Abrams.
2. **Pandita Ramabai speech and salary notice (pp. 15–16):** Abrams reports Ramabai’s words and a printed notice. This is evidentiary material attributed to Ramabai, not neutral narration; locate any surviving Ramabai report/leaflet if the wording is to be reproduced.
3. **Unnamed Parsi woman’s testimony (p. 16):** a distinct embedded voice. Use paraphrase or a narrowly bounded quotation only after fidelity and rights review; do not assign authorship to Abrams.
4. **“Lead me gently all the way” (p. 17):** reported song line associated in the article with an unnamed man. Attribution remains unresolved in this pass; omit from a student source text unless identified.
5. **“Am I a soldier of the cross” (p. 17):** the stanza matches the first stanza of Isaac Watts’s hymn, not an original Abrams composition. Watts (1674–1748) is the textual author; modern musical settings, arrangements, and editions require separate treatment.
6. **Revelation 13 and 15 (p. 17):** Abrams quotes or closely follows biblical text. Record the historical edition wording and keep it outside an Abrams-only word count.
7. **Daniel Awrey, “Life Sketches” (p. 18):** a new article begins on the same page; it is not an embedded Abrams quotation and must be excluded entirely.

## Fidelity and academic risks

- **OCR is not a transcript.** Visible errors include “Cod” for “God,” broken proper names, corrupted line endings, and column-order collapse.
- **Historical claims need framing.** Abrams reports famine, illness, conversion, caste exclusion, supernatural guidance, and eschatological interpretation. These are historical testimony and theology, not independently verified facts.
- **Indian agency must remain visible.** The article repeatedly makes Pandita Ramabai a leader and decision-maker. Editing should not flatten the account into an American missionary-centered narrative.
- **Financial claims are not CTS policy.** The article criticizes unsupported missionaries and also describes “faith” support. CTS must not imply a promise of money, devices, printing, connectivity, or institutional maintenance to modern students or partner churches.
- **One work is one reading voice.** This article may serve as one bounded R4 candidate; it does not satisfy the five-independent-reading requirement.

## Recommended bounded R4 design

If Wayne approves academic use and rights counsel/clearance permits it, use a short passage set rather than all six page images:

- Abrams-authored narrative on local leadership and institutional responsibility;
- a brief contextual paraphrase of the Ramabai and Parsi testimony rather than long embedded quotation;
- Abrams’s concluding appeal on responsibility and prayer;
- explicit brackets or notes identifying every Scripture, hymn, and third-party voice.

Exclude the preceding article/poem on p. 13, the unidentified song line, the Watts stanza unless separately documented, and all of Awrey’s “Life Sketches.” Provide a new exact word count only after a clean diplomatic transcript is built from the images.
