## Priority faculty findings

1. **Potential assessment-source mismatch is widespread if the staged journal extracts replace CTS digests**: 20/40 prompt stems are outside or not directly evidenced by those narrower excerpts. R5 is especially exposed (5/8 concern 1740–43 while its staged text ends 1 January 1740). This is a *conditional source-alignment risk*, not a finding that today's published digest-based test is unfair.
2. **Prompt 10 needs substantive wording review**: The staged 1736 journal says the infant Mary Welch was **ill** when immersed. The beta stem's explanation about the Prayer Book rule for **healthy children** may be historically true as a general rubric, but should not be made to sound like a description of this specific child. Review the original Prayer Book rubric and journal facsimile; do not silently change an assessed item or past grade.
3. **Prompt 29 has edition-sensitive dating**: Open Library records a **1739 Bristol** printing of *Free Grace* (https://openlibrary.org/books/OL25009602M/Free_grace), while Wellcome records a **1740 London** printing (https://wellcomecollection.org/works/c9unzq36), and the Wesley Center labels a later collected sermon as preached in 1740 (https://wesley.nnu.edu/john-wesley/the-sermons-of-john-wesley-1872-edition/sermon-128-free-grace/). These do not by themselves disprove the 1739 stem. Verify the specific Bristol original and distinguish sermon delivery, first printing, and reprints before approving a fact or translation.
4. **Historical source vs commentary**: Prompts 3, 15 and 28 test contextual claims not found in the selected journal passages. Supply a cited contextual reading or re-scope questions **after faculty decision**; never infer the original primary source contains them.
5. **J* language precision**: Prompts 6, 9, 10, 30, 33 have partial primary-source support but modernized names, contextual additions or location details requiring source/translation collation. Prompt 35 also needs a polarity check (Wesley versus opponents' view of degrees of faith).
