## Preliminary Mexico rights gate — not legal approval

The current Mexican LFDA (last reform 14 May 2026) gives an identified author's patrimonial rights for life plus 100 years (Art. 29), and separately protects original expression in derivative works and translations (Arts. 78–79).

- **Durham-authored articles:** the archive identifies William H. Durham (1873–1912) and his authorship/editorial role. His original authorial texts are strong Mexican public-domain candidates from 1 January 2013.
- **Unsigned 1912 memorial:** if treated as an anonymous/collective disclosed work, Article 29's 100-years-after-disclosure rule makes it a strong candidate from 1 January 2013, but authorship/collective-work classification should be documented.
- **Seymour material:** the archive identifies William J. Seymour (1870–1922), making his identified original texts strong candidates from 1 January 2023. Attribution to “the paper” must not silently prove Seymour authored every unsigned item.
- **Parham material:** the archive identifies Charles F. Parham (1873–1929). Under life plus 100, his authorial works remain protected in Mexico through 2029 and become public-domain candidates on 1 January 2030. Avoid reproducing his protected wording without permission or a documented legal exception.
- **Modern scholarship:** Cecil M. Robeck Jr.'s 2006 book and other modern histories remain protected. Facts can be sourced; their expression cannot be copied. Add page citations and a quotation/permission log.
- **CTS additions:** CTS digest prose and translations are separate derivative expression and need authorship/permission records. OCR availability does not itself grant reuse rights.
