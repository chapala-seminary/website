## High-priority decisions
1. **Quotation/attribution (#35).** The current CTS digest uses a memorable paired-benefits metaphor; the staged Dagg text instead says these benefits are not separate. Do not put the digest's editorial metaphor in quotation marks as Dagg's own words. Check the existing private bank's accepted variants, without publishing them; if the present digest remains assigned, faculty can revise *attribution* prospectively while preserving earned credit.
2. **Modern labels (#12, #14, #26) and inherited vocabulary (#3, #28).** The digests legitimately supply interpretive labels and modern paraphrases. If CTS substitutes raw Dagg passages, either teach the labels explicitly in an assigned CTS note or author new source-specific questions; do not assume the older wording and modern labels are interchangeable in fill-in grading. Audit EN/ES/FR/SW variant parity privately.
3. **Historical supplements (#1, #2, #39).** Dagg's biography, Baptist convention chronology and named later theological interlocutors are CTS contextual instruction. Retain them as attributed CTS commentary if the digests remain; do not ascribe them to the five original excerpts.
4. **Textual criticism (#20).** Dagg quotes Acts 8:37, which is absent from the main text of some modern Bible editions or appears in a footnote. This is not grounds to erase Dagg's Baptist position; add a short non-polemical translation note before students in different countries are tested on the passage.
5. **Historical-science framing (R2).** Dagg's nineteenth-century discussions of geological time and astronomy are arguments of his era. Preserve his authentic Baptist theological voice and distinguish it from present-day scientific claims. No forced doctrinal homogenization.
6. **Source identity.** The five readings are disjoint chapters from the same author/work, as already measured in CURRENT_READING_INDEPENDENCE_AUDIT.md; do not advertise them as five independent Baptist scholarly voices. The present CTS digests are five independent CTS editorial learning units, not independent original authors.
