THE GREAT REVIVAL AT AZUSA STREET
MISSION-HOW IT BEGAN AND HOW
IT ENDED.
On February 14th, we began meetings in Azusa
Mission. From the first day the power of God
rested upon the meetings in a wonderful way. The
altar was crowded at every service, and after the
first few meetings, it was necessary to provide extra
altar space, and soon we moved the seekers' meetings to the second floor, where there is a large room.
fitted up for that purpose. The work in Los Angeles
was in a sad condition. Those who had been the
leaders, in most cases, had proven so incompetent
that the saints had lost all confidence in them, and
this had resulted in a state of confusion that was
sad indeed to see. Scores were really in a backslidden state, and yet in their hearts they longed to follow Jesus. Scores of others were, and for months
had been, crying to God to send some one who
would preach the truth and lead His people on.'
The suffering of many had been great indeed.
As the message began to go forth, the saints came
from all directions, and inside of a few days the
place was crowded to the daors, and manv turned
awav. Sometimes there would be more th;n a hundred at the altar at a single service. One after
another would stand up in the meetings and confess
that they had gotten their eyes off Jesus, and go to
the altar, and in almost every case the anointing of
the blessed Spirit would be renewed, till soon the
very air was filled with notes of praise and shouts
of victory.
From the very first, sinners were saved, and an
average of at least ten a ,week were baptized in the
Holy Spirit and spoke in tongues, as He gave them
utterance. \Veek after week went by, and still the
interest did not wane, but on the contrary the power
increased, till the glory of God literally filled the
place. At times the Spirit would move upon the
saints, and they would sing in the Spirit, and praise
the Lord in the most wonderful way I ever heard.
At times there could be no regular order of service;
no one could preach or even testify. AlI we could
do was to sit with our souls bathed in a sea of
heavenly glory, and praise the Lord with alI our
hearts.
As stated above, the sufferings of many of the
saints had been great. Their joy now knew no
bounds. They saw in these wonderful meetings the
direct answer to the earnest prayers they had sent
up to God for months, and now they knew their
labor had not been in vain, and the light of Heaven
began to shine from their faces in a way most marvelous to behold. God had told some of them He
was going to do a work in Azusa St. Mission, and
they now rejoiced in the fulfilIment of His promise.
Some saw visions of the cloud of glory hanging over
the place, and had revelations of what God was
going to do, every one of which came true. Time
and space forbid our giving anything like a detailed
account of the meetings. As many as twenty-two
were baptized in the Holy Spirit in one week. Some
of the hardest sinners were saved. I believe that
literally hundreds who had more or less backslidden

WARNING.
Some months ago there was handed me, in Chicago, a book or pamphlet entitled, "In School with
the Holy Ghost." I had no idea who was the author
of this book as the messages were supposed to have
been spoken in prophesy.
.
As I have always felt l.~e giving everything a fair
trial before accepting or' rejecting it, I did so in
this case. As soon as I began to read it I felt an
inwa·rd warning against it. The more I read, the
stronger this warning became. Later I talked with
others, who at the "time, so far as I know, did not
know who the author of the book was; and they,
being deeply spiritual people with good spiritual discernment, felt the same way I did about the book.
I did not see anything dangerous in the book, but
the very tone of it did not appeal to me. It seemed
full of childish utterances. It seemed to me too bad
to charge God with the authorship of such a work.
Later I found the book contained many unscriptural
utterances, contradictions and inconsistencies. In
a word, I do not hesitate to say, that I do not believe
it to be of God, and therefore I cannot indorse it.
I understand that there is something of an impression abroad, that these messages were spoken in
N orth Avenue Mission, Chicago, and indorsed by
the Mission. This is not correct. Not one of these
messages was ever spoken in that Mission or indorsed by it while I was in charge as pastor there.
They were not spoken in the public assembly, according to the Scripture, so that those sitting by could
discern. They were spoken in private, and published
in the same way, so that neither the pastor nor the
Mission, as such, had any opportunity of knowing
anything about the matter till after they were published; and then, as stated above, I did not even
know who had spoken them, and 'rejected them
before I knew, in fact I only know by hearsay now.
In writing this article, nothing is further from my
mind than any thought of condemning the one who
'spoke these messages or anyone else. I do think
however that it was both unwise, and unfair to me as
pastor of the Mission, to publish and begin the circulation of such a book in the Mission, without consulting me in the matter.
:My object in this article is not to take up the matter contained in this book, and show in each instance
where it is wrong, from a Scriptural standpoint, but

TESTIMONY
simply to announce that I do not indorse the book
and why.

A GREAT REVIVAL IN DALLAS, TEXAS.
Writing under date of June 28th, Brother F. F.
Bosworth of 2213 Washington avenue, Dallas, Tex.,
gives an account of one of the most wonderful
revivals that we have known in the Pentecostal
movement. He, with his wife and a brother, went
to Dallas last October and began work. As the
work was in a Very low state, they began to wait
much on God, and He granted them a real spirit of
intercessory prayer. This continued for weeks, and
·.then the Lord really began to move and to pour out
His Spirit in a most ~blessed and wonderful way.
We have not space here to give a detailed account
of the work, but for months the fire and power of
God continued. Many were saved. Up to the date
of Brother Bosworth's letter, no less than one hundred and eighty-five had received the Holy Spirit
and spoken in tongues. \Vhat is' still better, there
was no waning of the power, but rather an increase
and the weekly average of baptisms was increasing,
all glorv to our blessed God, and His adorable Son
Jesus Christ. Let all the brethren everywhere give
thanks to God for this great work, which He has
wrought in the power of the Holy Ghost.
Our preciotls brethren, who have been so used in
this revival, stand with us on the doctrine of the finished \Vork of Calvarv. In his letter, Brother Bosworth states that the ~econd work theory has never
been introduced there, and may the Lord forbid that
it should ever be. O! may the simple truth of the
glorious Gospel goo forth, for when it does, results
like the above follow.
Brother Bosworth wrote me a kind, even urgent
invitation to attend the camp meeting that was to be
held there during the month of July, but, although I
hoped and fully expected to go, owing to so many
pressing duties, I was kept at home. I deferred an
answer to his letter till it was too late to reach him
before the meeting, in the hope that I could go, for
at least a few days. I feel sure that the Lord in the
near future will permit me to visit these brethren,
and help them a few days in this blessed work. Will
all the saints pray for this work, and especially for
these dear workers? May God send many revivals
like the one He has sent to Dallas.
