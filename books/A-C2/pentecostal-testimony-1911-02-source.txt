THE WORK IN CHICAGO.
Some months ago the Lord, very definitely, in the
Spirit, called us to humble our hearts and to seek, by
earnest prayer, to come closer to Him and deeper into
H im. The most of the saints did this. We were all
made to see that the · only way we could have God's
real power and see His mighty works wrought in our
midst, was to walk before Him in the Spirit, trusting
wholly in the precious Blood of Jesus and devoting
much time to humbly and prayerfully waiting upon
H im and seeking His face. We have been made to see
clearly that crowds of people and lively meetings, and
all such things, amount to nothing. God's plan is to
develop in His saints the character of Jesus Christ,
which will sustain the gifts of the Holy Spirit, and
then impart the gifts to H is people:
We know, and have known for years, that no amount
of works, booming, or even good teaching, will ever
bring the fulness of God's power. T he idea of a big
movement, with big men and preachers running it, is
distasteful to every soul who knows the truth. God

who are willing to surrender themselves wholly to
God and then live the surrendered life, those who make

SANCTIFICATION.
IS IT A DEFINITE, SECOND, INSTANTANEOUS WORK OF
GRACE?

This subject is attracting so much attention in the
Pentecostal movement just now, and is being so widely
discussed, that it seems necessary to devote some time
and space to it. I have received many letters asking
questions on the subject, and hundreds of my friends
who have heard me preach on the subject have earnestly requested me to deal with the subject more thoroughly in the paper, that others might receive the light
and blessing they have received. It might be well for
me to state that from my conversion, thirteen years
ago, to the present time, I have continually associated
with holiness people; and that for years, in my preaching, I referred to sanctification as a second work of
grace, bringing several things in my attempt to prove
it:
First, that many people, after being converted, had
r~ceived what seemed to them to be a definite experience.
Second, that the Scriptures clearly taught that there
was an experience to be received after conversion.
When I referred to the Scriptures I always used the
experience of the Apostles at Pentecost and the experience of the Samaritans as recorded in the 8th of
Acts ; also the account of the Spirit's falling on Cornelius under Peter's preaching recorded in Acts IO:
44-47, and the account of the outpouring at Ephesus,
Acts 19 :1-6.
Third, I used the exhortations of Paul in his epistles
to the churches as proof that the New Testament assemblies did not have all the Lord had for them.
Fourth, I used the condition of the religious world
today. I declared that large numbers of them were
Christian people, yet their lives proved that they were
not sanctified. They did not live it. Some of my teaching was that in justification we are pardoned ; in sanctification we are cleansed; that in justification the tre
is cut down; that in sanctification the stump is take

Now all the teaching of the second work people
has to do with the destruction or removal of the ·"old
man." They teach that a saved person has a dual nature. That while we are made a new creature, yet the
"old man" is in us, and that he ( the Adamic nature) is
removed in the second work of g'race.
Now as soon as it is established that the "old man"
is crucified and slain in conversion, the whole foundation from under their theory is entirely removed, and
their structure tumbles to the ground ; yet the plain
teaching in all the Scripture is to the effect, that the
"old man" was crucified with Christ, and that we are
justified on the ground that Jesus Christ took our place
and died in our stead. Now, why was it necessary for
Jesus Christ to die for us? Because the sentence of
death pronounced upon Adam the first, the "old man,"
was handed down to us and must be executed before
we could be justified. So when Jesus Christ went to
the Cross He not only took our sins upon Him, but
He, in the fullest sense, became our Substitute and took
our place, and the sentence of death which hung over
us was executed in Him; or, in other words, our "old
man," the Adamic nature, was crucified with Christ
that it might be destroyed or done away.
Now, when a pentitent sinner is made to see that
Jesus Christ died in His stead, his living faith identifies
him with Christ in His death : From that moment, from
God's point of view, or, as some say, in the eyes of the
law, he is dead. He has died in the person of his Substitute, Jesus Christ. He is now a candidate for baptism; that is, to be buried with Christ by baptism into
death, because his faith has identified him with Christ
in His death on the Cross. But God has quickened him
in Christ, when he believed in J esus Christ, and he
passes out of death into life, being made a new creature
in Christ. So, just as Christ came forth from the grave
to die no more, the believer in Christ comes up from his
burial in water to die no more, but rather to be identified ,vith Jesus Christ in his new life. There is no
other ground on which people are buried except death;
neither is there any excuse for burying any one in
water except that he is dead with Christ; and every student of the Bible will agree with me that it was the
"old man" that died and that the believer is supposed
to rise to walk in newness of life.

THE CHURCH.
We are frequently asked to give our op1mon concerning the Church, as to what it is, of whom composed, etc. We are also asked, frequently, whether
in our opinion Pentecostal people should stay in and
support the churches in this day. We hav~ seldom
answered such questions, as it seems to us every one
should understand these things as soon as they were
spiritually enlightened and had their understanding
opened, so that they would understand the Scriptures.
In the first place, the Church of Jesus Christ is composed of Pentecostal people. There never was a
Church until after Pentecost, and there is none recognized in the New Testament Scriptures, except the
Body of Christ, of which He is the Head, and which is
set in order by the Holy Spirit: for the Scriptures declare, I Cor. 12 :18, "But now hath God set the mem-

quoted above, that God sets the Church in order, and
each one is set in the place that the gifts of the Spirit
they have received qualifies them to fill, it is clear that

Wherever we find converts in the New Testament who

We conclude, therefore, that a Church, from a
Scriptural standpoint, is a company of people who
are called out of the world, made new creatures in
Christ. Jesus, buried with Him by baptism into death.
and filled with the Holy Spirit. From such a company
the Holy Spirit selects those whom He knows to hr
qualified for the ministry, and gives them a definite
call to preach the Gospel. These ministers will have

their control or hinder his message from going forth.
The Church of the Scriptures has a real scriptural experience, and holds to and contends for all the truth of
the glorious Gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ. She
walks before Him in the beauty of holiness in the
light of His truth and in the power of His Spirit, and
is thus kept entirely separated from the world and sin
and the snares of the devil.

human origin. Not one of them has any Scriptural
authority for its existence. In other words, God never
authorized any man, or set of men, to build and operate a church, nor to receive members into the churches.

true, holy, spiritual Church when they are born of
the Spirit and filled with the Spirit, as this is the only
normal Scriptural experience; and they are set in order
by the blessed Holy Spjrit, and equipped for work by
the gifts He imparts to them. The churches of men
reject very much of the deepest and most precious
truth of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, and exclude members and mioisters from their fellowship, if they witness that God has done for them the things He promises to do in His Word, and dare to preach the real
truth of God.

Lord Jesus Christ, and a hindrance to souls who are
seeking God's best. The churches of today unanimously reject the glorious Pentecostal outpouring of
the Holy Ghost that has brought such untold blessing
to tens of thousands of God's honest children. They

WEY THEY FAILED.
The fact that we do not see a church or institution
in the world that is up to the New Testament standard, is proof that the plan of God has not been carried
out. If the Gospel of Jesus Christ had been preached
as it was preached in the days of the Apostles, and
their methods followed, undoubtedly we would have
seen the same results that we see recorded in the New
Testament Scriptures. The only church described in
the New Testament is an institution which God has
separated from the world, and which is not only walking before God in the beauty of holiness, but in the
power of the Holy Ghost. The baptism in the Holy
Ghost is the seal of our finished salvation in Jesus
Christ. It is a real proof that we are saved to the
uttermost through the precious Blood of Christ. The
sign of the baptism, at the time it is given, is speaking
in tongues as the Spirit gives utterance. The proof
that a man, or company of men, who claims to have
received the Holy Ghost, or who has received I-{im, is
walking in the Holy Ghost, and that His power is
resting upon him, is the works of power which He
will perform among them. If people are filled with
the Holy Ghost and are walking in the Spirit, in every
case there will be real signs of His presence and power.
They will be heard to speak in tongues and interpret;
they will prophesy in the power of the Spirit; the
sick will be healed, and other wondrous works accomplished.
Now, as there have been .so few of such works seen
in the past, the above position seems to be a terrible
indictment of the religious world, in that it accuses it
of not having reached the standard of God, and thus
of robbing Him of His glory, as well as being guilty
of having failed to carry out the great commission
given the Churcp. by the Lord Jesus Christ. Mark 16 :
16-18; Matt. 28 :18-20. To simply state that the religious world, in the past, has failed to see and carry
out the plan of God, without pointing out the place
and cause and giving a clear reason why they failed,
would be of little value. Therefore we will attempt
to point out some of the mistakes which have been
made, and which resulted in failure. 'Without going
clear back and. .attempting ~o give the reasons in this
article why the Church first fell away, and without
dwelling too long upon those early reform movements,
which could not be expected to have all the truth when
first they emerged from the darkness of Romanism, we
will deal more particularly with matters of more recent
occurrence.
We will mention first the teachings of Alexander
Campbell and his followers. They taught to believe
and repent and be immersed ; putting faith before repentance. While a man might have an intellectual
faith before repentance, it is impg,5sible for him to
_have spiritual, or saving faith, be'fore he has truly
repented. They further taught that the Holy Ghost
was only given to the eleven Apostles, on the clay of
Pentecost, that they might perform such miracles as
would convince the people that Jesus Christ was raised
from the dead. Beyond this, their claim was that the
only Holy Spirit God's people receive in this day is
the Word of God. Most of them denied any real
work of grace, contending that there was no real
change of heart. Of course, believing this, they were
not apt to become new creatures in Christ Jesus,
though doubtless many of them were saved in spite of,
the erroneous ~achings of the leaders. Believing there
was no such thing as definitely r~ceiving the Holy
Spirit today, it is not to be wondered at that not one

of them, so far as we have any record, ever receiv.ea:
Him. · I have heard even their preachers ridicule the
idea of a man's heart being changed, and say blasphemous things concerning receiving the Holy Ghost.
Claiming to be unsectarian, and to have no creed but
the Bible, they became as sectarian as any of the socalled evangelical bodies. They reject very much of
the Word of God and substitute many of the theories
of men.
Next in order, we might mention the Baptists and
those of a like faith with them. The old order of
Baptist believed that the Bible was the inspired Word
of God, which the followers of Campbell did not believe. They thoroughly believed in the work of the
Holy Spirit in convicting of sin; they believed in true
repentance as essential to saving faith in Jesus Christ;
they believed in regeneration and baptism by immersion. Sad to say, they stopped here. Instead of their
following up the plain teachings of the Word, and
tarrying for and receiving the Holy Ghos~ immediately
after their conversion, they taught that, in some way
or measure, the Holy Spirit was received in conversion, so far as I know, laying very little stress on this
teaching. They taught then a gradual growth in grace
which would end in sanctification. As I understand it,
they were straight on the first principles of the Gospel.
They taught repentance and conversion and baptism
as the Scriptures do, and I believe God would have
met and baptized them in the Holy Ghost if they had
obeyed His Word and,sought in faith. How sad it is
that those who loved the truth and suffered so much
for its sake should have fallen short of the fulness of
God. Who can deny that, as a result of their failure
to receive the glorious gift of the Holy Ghost, they
have drifted almost as far away from God as those who
were less orthodox? Sad to say, when the truth concerning the Holy Spirit is brought to them today, they,
as a denomination, reject it. They have even been
guilty of ostracising and crowding out ministers of
their order who have received the Holy Ghost and
spoken in tongues.
Next, we would deal with those who are of the
Methodist persuasion. Up to conversion they taught
practically as the Baptist. While they recognized
Scriptural 'baptism, they generally administered the
baptism of the Church of Rome. They saw that the
Scriptures taught something definite beyond conversion. They further saw that the Scriptures taught
sanctification, and that it was not necessary to wait
so long for it as the Baptist taught. Right here they
made a mistake that is sad to contemplate: They invented a teaching unheard of in the Scriptures or
among the saints of the first centuries. Instead of
teaching their converts to be baptized in water, and
then receive the gift of the Holy Ghost according to
the Scriptures, they began to teach that sanctification
was a definite second work of grace. Instead of dealing with facts and assisting converts, through the
laying on ~f hands, prayer, etc., to receive the gift of
the Holy Ghost, they attempted to lead them into this
second work experience. But as they saw the Scriptures said so much about the matter of receiving the
Holy Ghost, they seeme'd to feel that He must be given
recognition, so they were actually guilty before God
and man of exalting their so-called second work to a
level with the glorious baptism in the Holy Ghost, and
taught that the terms, sanctification and baptism in
the Holy Spirit, in the Scriptures were synonymous.
Some taught, and it is still taught by some, that the
Holy Ghost is a complement to sanctification. There

are in Christ and under the Blood, we are holy. Instead of teaching one, thus saved, that he is full of

THE PAPER.
Something over two years ago the Lord, by His
Spirit, began to definitely impress me that He wanted
me to publish a paper that would stand firmly for the
real truths of the Pentecostal movement. On every side
these glorious truths were being compromised. Men
and papers were beginning to weaken, as to whether
the tongues is the evidence of the baptism in the Holy
Spirit. Some were also condemning all physical manifestations as being of the flesh. I believed, with all my
heart, that every person who was baptized in the Holy
Ghost spoke in tongues at the time he was filled. Fur- .
ther, I witnessed hundreds of cases where the Holy
Ghost was received, and I never saw one case in which
there were not some physical manifestations. God
made me to see clearly that there is a wide difference
between physical manifestations and fleshly manifestations; that in the first case the Holy Spirit operating
upon a physical being produces manifestations; that in
the second place the manifestation originates in the
flesh. One is produced by the Spirit of God and the
other is not. I saw also that men, failing to see the
plan of God, were attempting to form organizatiQns
inside of the new movement, in which, of course, they
were to hold positions of honor and power. I knew
that every deeply spiritual movement in the past had
been greatly hindered, and in many cases ruined altogether by men's organizing thefn.

to edit and publish such a paper, and that unless God
fitted me for it and gave me the help of the Spirit of
God in the work I could never do it. I shrank from
this duty for months. God made it plain to me that
there was to be no price placed on the paper, and that
I was not to obligate myself to publish it monthly. He

THE GREAT CRISIS-No. 3
For years it has been clear to me that we were drawing nearer and nearer to a great religious crisis. A
few years ago it was difficult for me to see just what
agency God would use to hasten this crisis. I knew
that the churches, in their present condition, could .
never do it. They have grown so much like the world
that a warm friendship has sprung up between them
and the world. I have been constrained to say at
times, that if God could tell the church and the world
apart He could do better than I could. Some one has
truthfully said, "If you want to see the world, go into
the church: If you want to see the church, go into the
world." Such a church as this never can stir the devil
in the unbelieving world and hasten the great crisis.
There is nothing in the teaching of the modern church
that stirs the devil. The sins of the world are not witnessed against, and the power of God is not sufficiently
manifested before the eyes of the world to excite the
rage of the enemy and bring such persecution as will
result in the lines being drawn between the church and
the world. The proof positive that every denominational church on the face of the earth is devoid of real
