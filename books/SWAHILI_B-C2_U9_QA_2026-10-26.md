## Release blockers found

### 1. Meaning-changing sentence in the lesson

The English lesson says that adoption does not end when a child misbehaves. The current Swahili sentence uses **“hakuishi”**, which ordinarily expresses “did not live” rather than “does not end/cease.” In context, this changes or obscures the intended doctrinal claim.

A qualified Swahili reviewer should replace it with an idiomatic construction meaning that adoption/sonship does not cease when a child misbehaves, then verify the revised sentence in context. No correction was applied in this audit.

### 2. Two overinclusive accepted-answer aliases

Two Swahili fill-in entries contain an accepted alias that repeats wording already printed immediately beside the blank. If inserted literally, one produces a doubled preposition and the other a doubled noun. The semantically correct short forms are already present as separate accepted aliases, so the redundant expanded aliases should be removed after authorized review.

This is an **answer-normalization defect**: it can accept grammatically malformed completions and makes automated grading less precise. The exact answers are intentionally omitted from this public report.
