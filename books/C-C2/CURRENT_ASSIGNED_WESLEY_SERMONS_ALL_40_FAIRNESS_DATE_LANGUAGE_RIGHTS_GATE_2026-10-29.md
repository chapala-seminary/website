### Two additional source-precision findings

1. **Q3's chronology is disputed, but the blank does not ask for the date.** The current question's blank concerns the London **street**, while its prose asserts the sermon was preached **18 days after 24 May 1738** (i.e., 11 June). The cited **1771 Pine edition** gives **18 June 1738** in its Sermon I footnote; the Museum of Methodism and Nazarene WHDL catalog also use June 18. In contrast, the scholarly Wesley Works commentary reports the June **11** Oxford appointment, and older historical scholarship explicitly disputes the printed June 18. This is a genuine *historical provenance conflict in the stem*, **not proof that a student was marked wrong for the date**. Faculty should add an edition-aware footnote and determine the historically preferred date before editing the public question; do not change private answers or invalidate earned credit.
2. **Q29's staged-source locator was incorrect in the earlier 36/40 report.** The two-person feeding-the-hungry comparison appears in **Sermon VII, “The Way to the Kingdom”**, not Sermon VIII. The currently assigned R4 §2 correctly contains it; the 1771 text confirms it. Correct the staged locator during the next faculty source collation, without modifying the public prompt or grade.

Other four-context checks: Q9's Charles Wesley attribution is supported by the current digest and a note in the 1771 transcription but absent from the staged sermon-window body; Q13's 1744 Oxford reception belongs to CTS history; Q24's 1763 Model Deed belongs to CTS institutional history. Keep source labels explicit, especially where the textbook is advertised as “John Wesley” alone.
