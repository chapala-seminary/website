## Historical source identity and excerpt direction

- Author: John Leadley Dagg (1794–1884). Intended original work: *Manual of Theology, Second Part: A Treatise on Church Order*, Southern Baptist Publication Society, **1859 imprint**; the transcription carries an **1858 copyright entry**. These are different dates, not proof of a separately inspected 1858 first-edition facsimile.
- Independent bibliographic witness for the 1859 imprint and 312-page second part: https://ebooks.faithlife.com/product/46674/manual-of-theology-second-part-a-treatise-on-church-order . **Do not copy this modern electronic edition**, with its enhancements, into CTS.
- Historical text/transcription and chapter inventory: https://founders.org/library/treatise-on-church-order-dagg/ and https://founders.org/library-book/a-treatise-on-church-order/ . The host carries a 2025 copyright notice for its site; the underlying Dagg original and the host's editorial/site rights must be evaluated separately.
- **Priority excerpt candidate:** Chapter II, “Local Churches,” one continuous passage if original printed-page boundaries and 3,000–6,000-word length support it. The chapter is independently listed in the source contents. Alternative for a distinct pastoral-safeguards objective: Chapter IX, “Discipline,” as its *own* continuous candidate, not spliced to Chapter II. **Neither original page range nor word count has been verified.**
- **Do not confuse** the second-part *Church Order* volume with Dagg's first-part *A Treatise on Christian Doctrine*, which is the separate B-C2 theological textbook.

## Mexico rights screen — current statutory text, not a legal opinion

The Mexican *Ley Federal del Derecho de Autor* available from the Chamber of Deputies shows **Última Reforma DOF 15-01-2026**: https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFDA.pdf .

- **Article 29(I):** ordinary patrimonial protection lasts for the author's life plus 100 years; it also addresses coauthors. Dagg died in 1884, so the original Dagg-authored historical text is a strong public-domain candidate under this rule. Confirm attribution and any separately authored insertions before relying on that calculation.
- **Article 78:** a translation/other derivative can receive protection for its original contribution, independently of the public-domain underlying work. The 2021 Spanish translation is a concrete exclusion.
- **Article 152:** use of public-domain works remains subject to respecting the author's moral rights.
- Provider access terms, modern editorial apparatus, Bible translations, photos/scans, and original copyright status are separate questions. This memo is **not** Wayne's legal or academic clearance and should not be treated as such.

## Editorial comparison prompts (original CTS planning text, not source-text translations)
