# B-C3 — Baptist Polity: source and rights manifest (provisional)

**Textbook:** Edward Thurston Hiscox (1814–1901), *The New Directory for Baptist Churches*. Philadelphia: American Baptist Publication Society, 1894 (608 pp). Original 1894 edition, not Hiscox's earlier 1859/1860 *Baptist Church Directory*, not the 1890 revision, and not the 1902 edition. `textbook.md` is a **five-chapter source selection**, NOT the full book. Original full OCR available below.

**Original 1894 source OCR:** https://archive.org/download/newdirectoryforb01hisc/newdirectoryforb01hisc_djvu.txt

**Original facsimile:** https://archive.org/details/newdirectoryforb01hisc ; https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/The_new_directory_for_Baptist_churches_.._%28IA_newdirectoryforb01hisc%29.pdf

These readings span the nature of the church, membership, officers, government, and discipline. Christian ordinances (ch V), worship (ch IX), Baptist councils (ch XII), baptism (ch XV), and the Lord's Supper (ch XVI) remain in the **full original source** but are NOT included in this selected reader; Claude may rebalance if the approved course objectives require these topics. Do not describe this selected textbook as the complete 608-page work.

**Rights:** Hiscox died in 1901; original 1894 publication predates 1931 in the United States, and an ordinary Mexico life+100 calculation ends 2001. Historical original text is a strong public-domain candidate in both jurisdictions, subject to final legal/edition review. This source is uncollated OCR, not modern editor's annotations, illustrations or translations. Do not import modern reprint apparatus without permission. Wayne must approve release.

**Academic warning:** Hiscox describes a specific late-19th-century Baptist polity; it does not represent all Baptist denominations today or CTS institutional governance. Contrast local-church autonomy and associations with modern diverse Baptist practice. Some historic disciplinary procedures and language need contextualization and pastoral sensitivity.
