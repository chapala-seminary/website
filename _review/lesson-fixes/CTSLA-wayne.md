# Biblical Languages: questions for Dr. Cook

- **When John wrote his Gospel (unit 7 b010 and unit 8 b018):** the two units disagree. Unit 7: "Sixty-five years earlier [than Patmos, AD 95] the same John, then a young fisherman from Galilee, had opened his Gospel" (so about AD 30). Unit 8: "around AD 90, an old fisherman from Galilee picked up his pen on the island of Patmos and opened his Gospel". Spanish says the same in each place. Which do you want? (Most date the Gospel around AD 85–95, and Revelation, not the Gospel, to Patmos.)
- **Unit 8, b018, John 1:1 "eighteen words":** both languages say John opened his Gospel "with eighteen words" ("con dieciocho palabras"). John 1:1 in Greek has seventeen words. Please check the count.
- **Unit 8, b010, "caritas":** "When Jerome translated ... around AD 400, he rendered agapê as caritas. Three centuries later, caritas came into English as charity." That would be about AD 700, well before English borrowed the word (around 1100–1200). Change "three centuries" (Spanish "Tres siglos después")?
- **Unit 9, b044, Eudokia / Eudoxia:** the English gives the proper name "Eudokia" ("good righteousness"); the Spanish gives "Eudoxia" («buena justicia»). The two languages name different words, and neither name means "good righteousness" (eudokia means "good pleasure"; Eudoxia means roughly "good repute"). What word did you intend here?
- **Unit 9, b051, kyrios:** "The same three-letter word, kyrios" ("La misma palabra de tres letras"). Kyrios (κύριος) has six letters. Change the wording?
- **Units 9 and 10, "Wayne and Andi":** a few places still name two teachers: unit 9 b072 ("as Wayne and Andi point them out to you"), unit 10 b044 ("Wayne and Andi's seminary"), unit 10 b070 ("another word approved by Wayne and Andi"); the Spanish says "Wayne y Andi" in each. Unit 11 names only Wayne. Should these name only you? (The grammar left over from the two-name version in unit 11, such as "Wayne have read", has been corrected to the singular.)
- **Unit 4, b024, who chose "created":** the English credits "the King James translators in 1611" with choosing "created"; the Spanish credits "los traductores de la Reina-Valera" with choosing «creó». Is the Spanish claim what you want to say?
- **Unit 1, b053 (course table, unit 11 row):** English "on a Greek word of your choice"; the Spanish says only "sobre una palabra griega" (no "de tu elección"). Add it?
