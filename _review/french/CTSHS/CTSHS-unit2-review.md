## Questions for Wayne

1. **"Helper" (John 14:16, 14:26).** LSG has "consolateur". Following convention 1, b009 adds one sentence: the Greek word (Paraclet) literally means the one called alongside to help. In MC 3, "the Comforter" and "the Helper" would both become "le Consolateur", so the "Helper" option reads "le Paraclet, celui qui aide".
2. **John 16:13 "guide".** LSG has "il vous conduira dans toute la vérité". The prose uses "conduire" throughout, and the SA keywords accept both "condui" and "guid".
3. **1 Corinthians 2:10 "the deep things of God".** LSG has "les profondeurs de Dieu". Fill 3 has the answer "profondeurs" and also accepts "profondeur", "choses profondes" and "profond".
4. **Romans 8:26 "groanings".** LSG has "soupirs inexprimables". Following convention 1, b011 adds that the Greek word literally means groanings. Fill 5 has the answer "soupirs" and also accepts "gémissements".
5. **Ephesians 4:30 "grieve".** LSG has "attrister", which is weaker than "grieve". I kept "attrister" throughout so that the prose matches the verse ("« Attrister » est un mot tendre, un mot d'amour").
6. **Isaiah 63:10.** LSG has "son esprit saint" in lowercase. I quoted it exactly.
7. **Acts 16:6–7.** LSG has "empêchés par le Saint-Esprit" (NKJV "forbidden") and, in verse 7, "l'Esprit de Jésus ne le leur permit pas" (NKJV "the Spirit"). I quoted LSG, and the prose still says "interdire" for the lesson's point. The phrase "Spirit of Jesus" fits the lesson, so I added no note about it.
8. **Acts 13:2 "worshiped".** LSG has "servaient le Seigneur dans leur ministère". In the prose I wrote "les croyants servaient le Seigneur". "Separate to Me" becomes "Mettez-moi à part". Fill 8 has the answer "Saul" (LSG).
9. **Yahweh (b007).** Following convention 2, I wrote "le Père, qui se nomme Yahvé (l'Éternel)".
10. **Fill 10 "yield".** I wrote "nous nous soumettons à lui". The answer is "soumettons", and Fill 10 also accepts "abandonnons", "livrons", "rendons" and "remettons".

## Problems in the English source

1. **b007.** It says that "the Father is named Yahweh". In Scripture, YHWH is the name of the one God, and the New Testament applies it to the Son as well. Some readers may object to tying it to the Father alone. I translated it as written.
2. **Fill 2.** The warning that the answer "nom" appears elsewhere in the prompt is a false alarm: the prompt contains the plural "noms", as the English contains "names". I kept it.
3. No wrong answer keys were found. Every `why` matches its question. Every exam item is taught in the lesson.

4. **b018 masthead.** Units 2 and 3 read "📚 Catalogue / Catálogo", while units 1 and 4–10 read "📚 Catalogue". Robert should pick one form for all units. I left it unchanged.

## Adaptations

- **"Holy Ghost" / "it" (b005, MC 3, Fill 1).** These are English-language problems: French has always said "Saint-Esprit", and French uses "il" for the Spirit. I kept the English terms and explained them: « Holy Ghost », littéralement le « Saint Fantôme », and « it », the pronoun used for things ("cela"). The heading uses "« Il », et non « cela »". Fill 1 has the answer "Fantôme" and also accepts "revenant", "spectre" and "Ghost".
- **SA prompts.** Several English SA prompts would score hits against their own keywords in French, so I reworded them (convention 10). For example, SA 2 says "la façon dont la Bible emploie son nom" and SA 6 says "le mot clé de la promesse de Jean 14:16". The meaning is unchanged.
- **Spiritual gifts.** The only reference is 1 Corinthiens 12:11 ("comme il veut"). It is translated neutrally, with no cessationist or continuationist slant added.
