# CTSHS Unit 9 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Galatians 5:22-23, names and order of the nine graces (whole unit).** The English uses KJV wording. Segond's list is: l'amour, la joie, la paix, la patience, la bonté, la bénignité, la fidélité, la douceur, la tempérance. Segond's words line up with the Greek like this: KJV "gentleness" = Segond **bonté**, KJV "goodness" = **bénignité**, KJV "faith" = **fidélité**, KJV "meekness" = **douceur**. I followed Segond throughout. The headings are now "La patience et la bonté" and "La bénignité, la fidélité, la douceur et la maîtrise de soi". In modern French "bénignité" is a rare word, so b017 adds "(le mot grec désigne la bonté active)". Is that acceptable?
2. **"Faith here means faithfulness" (b017, MC14).** Segond already translates "fidélité", so the English point would confuse a French reader. I kept the teaching and added one sentence: the Greek word (pistis) is the same as for "foi", but here it means faithfulness, as Segond translates it. MC14's stem was adjusted to match.
3. **Numbers 12:3 "very meek" (b017, MC16, Fill 8, SA7).** Segond says "Moïse était un homme fort patient", not "meek". I quoted Segond and added (convention 1) that the Hebrew says literally "humble", "doux". Fill 8 answers **patient** and also accepts humble, doux, débonnaire. MC16 quotes Segond.
4. **2 Peter 3:9 "longsuffering to us-ward" (b015).** Segond has "il use de patience envers vous". I wrote "le Seigneur « use de patience envers vous »". The prose glosses patience once as "longanimité" (the literal Greek sense). Fill 6 answers **patience** and accepts longanimité and endurance.
5. **John 15:5 "vine / branches" (b019, Fill 10, SA9).** Segond has "le cep" and "les sarments". I added one short sentence explaining that the cep is the vine stock and the sarments are its branches. Fill 10 answers **sarments** and accepts "branches".
6. **Other Segond differences, quoted as written.** Galatians 5:23: "la loi n'est pas contre ces choses" (for "against such there is no law"). Galatians 5:25: "Si nous vivons par l'Esprit, marchons aussi selon l'Esprit". Philippians 4:7: "qui surpasse toute intelligence", with "(c'est-à-dire toute compréhension humaine)" added. Philippians 1:6: "la rendra parfaite" (for "perform it"). John 15:11: "que votre joie soit parfaite" (for "full"). Galatians 5:19-21: Segond's shorter list of the works of the flesh (it has no "adultery" and no "murders"), quoted in full.
7. **1 Corinthians 13 (b022).** Segond translates agape there as "charité". I added a short parenthesis saying so, so that the student can follow "love is the greatest gift" in Segond.
8. **Spiritual gifts.** The unit does not discuss whether the gifts continue or have ceased, and the French adds nothing on either side. MC20's distractor "wait for a second blessing" is translated literally (« attendez une seconde bénédiction »), no more and no less pointed than the English.

## Problems in the English source

1. Answer keys: scrambled in English; corrected in pull request #83.
2. **The lesson quotes the KJV, not the NKJV.** Examples: "longsuffering… faith, meekness, temperance", "Holy Ghost", "ye are the branches", "alway rejoicing", "us-ward". This makes no difference to the French, but the English site should be checked.
3. **b022.** "Love is the one grace that Scripture calls by three names" is followed by three categories (commandment, gift, fruit), not three names. Also, 1 Corinthians 13:13 calls love the greatest of faith, hope and love, not "the greatest gift" as such. I translated both points as written.
4. **SA prompts.** Several English prompts hit their own keywords (for example, SA1 hits "fruit" and "spirit", and SA9 hits "fruit", "grow" and "spirit"). Following convention 10, the French uses 8 distinct ideas per question. No French prompt hits any group, and every French model hits all 8 (re-checked after the checker's keyword changes).
