## Questions for Wayne

1. **John 2:11, "beginning of signs".** LSG has "le premier des miracles que fit Jésus". That works against b010, which says "John does not call it a miracle; he calls it a sign". I quoted LSG and added, per convention 1: "Le texte grec dit littéralement : « ce commencement des signes »". The lead sentence is softened to "Jean ne présente pas ce geste comme un simple prodige". MC 8's `why` carries the same note. Fill 5 asks for "signes" (answerable from b010).
2. **John 4:42, "the Christ, the Savior of the world".** LSG has only "il est vraiment le Sauveur du monde". In b019 I quoted LSG and added a sentence saying that part of the Greek manuscripts read "le Christ, le Sauveur du monde". MC 20 keeps "le Christ, le Sauveur du monde" as the correct option. Its `why` cites 4:42 as LSG has it, plus 4:29 ("ne serait-ce point le Christ?"). This matches the CTSJohn Unit 3 handling.
3. **John 3:3, "born again" / "born from above".** LSG has "si un homme ne naît de nouveau". In b013 I added that the Greek word also means "d'en haut". Fill 7 has the answer "de nouveau" and also accepts "d'en haut", "à nouveau", "une seconde fois" and "une deuxième fois".
4. **John 1:12, "received Him".** LSG has "l'ont reçue … elle a donné", where the pronoun refers to la Parole. I quoted LSG and added "« elle », c'est la Parole, c'est-à-dire Christ".
5. **John 1:39 and 1:46, "Come and see".** LSG has "Venez, leur dit-il, et voyez" (1:39) and "Viens, et vois" (1:46). I used "Venez, et voyez" as the heading and the pattern name. Fill 1 has the answer "voyez" and also accepts "vois" and "voir".
6. **John 4:4, "He needed to go through Samaria".** LSG has "il fallait qu'il passât par la Samarie". MC 16 speaks of "cette nécessité", so the divine-necessity point still holds.
7. **Other LSG wording I used.** "Fils unique" (3:16, for "only begotten"), "serpent d'airain … perche" (Nb 21:9), "source d'eau qui jaillira" (4:14, for "fountain"), "maison de trafic" (2:16), "celui qui agit selon la vérité" (3:21), "cruche" (waterpot) and "un chef des Juifs" (3:1).
8. **b013, "the old man".** I rendered it "le vieil homme", the Pauline term. It is a natural choice in French, but you may prefer "l'homme naturel".
