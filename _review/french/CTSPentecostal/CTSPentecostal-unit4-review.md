## Questions for Wayne

1. **"Initial evidence" (b014, b051, b052, b063, MC7, Fill 10, SA2, SA9).** I used *signe initial*, the usual French Pentecostal term (also used in Unit 6), and *signe obligatoire* for "required evidence". I did not use *évidence*, which in French means "obviousness" and is an anglicism here. Fill 10 answer: "signe"; it also accepts "preuve", "évidence" and "marque".
2. **"Gentile(s)".** LSG says "païens" (Actes 10:45, 11:18). In Kinshasa "païen" sounds like "unbeliever/pagan", so in the lesson I used *non-Juif(s)*, with one gloss in b033: "(un « païen », dit la Bible)". SA6 accepts both. Please confirm.
3. **1 Corinthiens 14:39 (b049, Fill 9, SA9).** NKJV says "do not forbid to speak with tongues"; LSG says « n'empêchez pas de parler en langues ». I quoted LSG. The lesson's next sentence ("a gift the church is commanded not to forbid") now uses *empêcher*. Fill 9 answer: "empêchez"; it also accepts "interdisez" and "défendez".
4. **Centurion (b033, Fill 6, SA6).** I used LSG "centenier"; Fill 6 also accepts "centurion".
5. **Simon "the sorcerer" (b030, Fill 4).** I used "Simon le magicien", since LSG Actes 8:9 says he "exerçait la magie".
6. **Acts 19:2 (b037).** The lesson's wording ("had not so much as heard that the Holy Spirit had been given") follows a reading the NKJV text does not use ("whether there is a Holy Spirit"). LSG says "qu'il y ait un Saint-Esprit". The lesson does not quote the verse, so I translated the lesson's paraphrase faithfully. You may want to align it.
7. **Acts 11:17, "stand in God's way" (b035, SA6).** LSG says "pouvais-je, moi, m'opposer à Dieu?", so I used *s'opposer à Dieu*.
8. **Theological terms.** I used "baptême dans le Saint-Esprit" (Unit 6 does the same) and "seconde bénédiction". "Outpouring" is *effusion*. "Spirit-filled" is *remplis de l'Esprit*. The tone is kept as even as the English, neither warmer nor cooler.
