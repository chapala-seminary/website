## Questions for Wayne

1. **Esther 4:14, "come to the kingdom" (b003, b007, b008, b017, b022, MC15, Fill 6, SA5, SA10).** Segond has "que tu es parvenue à la royauté" ("royal position") and "pour un temps comme celui-ci". I used Segond throughout, so the title phrase is "Pour un temps comme celui-ci". Fill 6 blanks **royauté**. It also accepts "royaume", "dignité royale" and "trône".
2. **Esther 4:16, "If I perish, I perish!" (b017, MC16, SA5).** Segond has "et si je dois périr, je périrai". I quoted it exactly.
3. **"Gallows" (b021, MC20, Fill 10, SA8).** Segond has "le bois" (Esther 7:9-10). I used "bois" and added one sentence: the Hebrew says literally "l'arbre", meaning a pole or gallows. Fill 10 blanks **bois**. It also accepts "potence", "gibet", "poteau" and "arbre".
4. **Haman as Amalekite (b015, MC11, Fill 4, SA4).** Segond calls him "l'Agaguite" (Esther 3:1). The link to Agag and Amalek is the usual reading, and I kept the lesson's "Amalécites". SA4 also accepts "agag". Do you want a short note about this?
5. **Names.** Following Segond, I used Vasthi, Mardochée, Assuérus (also called Xerxès), Haman and Suse. Fill 3 also accepts "Vashti" and "Vasti".
6. **"Harem" (b012, SA2).** I kept "harem" and glossed it with Segond's "maison des femmes". SA2 accepts both.
7. **Tolkien quotation (b018, MC17).** I translated Gandalf's words myself, not from the published French translation of *Le Seigneur des anneaux*. Is that acceptable, or should we use the published wording?
8. **Hebrews 1:3, "upholding all things by the word of His power" (b020, MC19, SA7).** Segond has "soutenant toutes choses par sa parole puissante". The meaning is the same, so I quoted it without adding a note.

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC5 contradicts the lesson.** The keyed answer (B) says Mordecai "broke God's command against intermarriage" and "saw an opportunity". The lesson (b012) now says the opposite: neither Esther nor Mordecai had a real choice, and it was "imperial conscription". The `why` ("our crooked choices") belongs to the older version of the lesson. I translated it faithfully. I recommend replacing MC5.
2. **MC4 also conflicts with b012.** Its correct option says "Mordecai entered his young cousin Esther", but the lesson says she was "taken like the others". I translated it as written ("présenta").
3. **Unsourced claims in b013 (MC9, MC10, SA3).** "About 125 commands of Jesus, 21 of them 'fear not'", "Black Bart, 29 stagecoach crews" and the "Johns Hopkins study" have no sources. Please check them before they go to a wider audience.
4. **b015 and MC12, "bought the king's favor with silver".** In Esther 3:9-11 Haman offers 10,000 talents, and the king answers, "L'argent t'est donné". This may mean the king refused the money. The claim is a simplification.
5. **SA prompts give away their own keywords.** SA1 has "book". SA2 has "esther" and "queen". SA4 has "haman" and "plot". SA5 has "mordecai". SA6 has "counselor" and "doctor". SA7 has "silent". SA8 has "reversal". SA10 has "providence", "unseen" and "silent". Following convention 10, I reworded the French prompts. None now reaches 3 hits; at most 1 hit remains.
6. **Ephesians 1:18 (b020).** "The eyes of the heart" is not the NKJV wording (the NKJV has "eyes of your understanding"). Segond happens to have "les yeux de votre coeur", so the French is correct.
