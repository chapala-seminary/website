## Questions for Wayne

1. **"Blessed" vs. LSG "Heureux".** LSG has "Heureux" in every Beatitude. I used "heureux" wherever the lesson says "blessed / what the world calls blessed", and "bénédiction" where it says "blessing". The play on "blessed" in b011 and b035 therefore reads as a play on "heureux / bonheur".
2. **Matthew 5:4, "mourn" vs. LSG "les affligés".** The lesson (b015-b059) is built on mourning and weeping. Following convention 1, I quoted LSG and added one sentence in b015: the Greek word literally means those who weep, who are in mourning. The three numbered points use "pleurer". MC6 and the b059 quote use LSG "consolés".
3. **Matthew 5:5, "meek" vs. LSG "les débonnaires".** The heading and quotes use "débonnaires", which is old-fashioned for Kinshasa readers. The teaching uses "douceur / doux", and b017 notes once that the Greek means "doux". Moses is called "meek" in the lesson, but LSG Numbers 12:3 says "un homme fort patient", so I added a short note on the Hebrew. MC7, MC8, SA3 and Fill 5 use "douceur".
4. **Fill 5, "strength under control".** I translated this as "la force maîtrisée". Answer: "maîtrisée". It also accepts contrôlée, maîtrisé, tenue en bride and dominée.
5. **Matthew 5:9, "peacemakers" vs. LSG "ceux qui procurent la paix".** I used LSG in all quotes, MC17 and SA7. "Artisan de paix" is the noun in the teaching, and "peace-lover" is "ami de la paix". LSG has "fils de Dieu", so MC17 keeps "fils (enfants)".
6. **Matthew 5:6, "filled" vs. LSG "rassasiés".** Fill 6 answer: "rassasiés". It also accepts remplis, comblés, satisfaits and rassasié.
7. **Matthew 5:17, "destroy" vs. LSG "abolir".** I changed the heading in b030 to the LSG wording. MC18 and Fill 10 use "abolir / accomplir", and SA9 keywords accept both "aboli" and "détrui".
8. **Matthew 5:27, LSG "Vous avez appris".** In 5:21 LSG has "entendu". I quoted each verse as LSG has it. The heading in b032 uses "Vous avez entendu qu'il a été dit... Mais moi, je vous dis".
9. **Matthew 5:48, "Father in heaven" vs. LSG "Père céleste".** I quoted LSG in b033 and b036. Nothing in the exam depends on this.
10. **Short-answer prompts.** Under convention 10, several English prompts named their own keywords (poor/spirit, hunger/thirst, pure/heart, salt/light). I reworded them, for example "la première béatitude" and "la sixième béatitude", so a prompt cannot pass by itself. They ask the same question.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b041 (Father Damien).** The story leaves out its point. Damien knew he had leprosy because he felt *no pain* from the boiling water, but the English never says so. He also served on Molokai about 16 years (1873-1889), not nineteen. I translated it as written.
2. **b025 (Christ of the Andes).** The dispute was settled in 1902, and the statue was erected in 1904. The statement that it was cast from melted cannons is a popular tradition that historians doubt. I translated it as written.
3. **b017 (Churchill).** The claim that his blindness to his faults "eventually cost him the trust of his people" has no source and is doubtful as history. I translated it as written.
4. **b048** is unclear in English ("reject his people as he saw them rejecting him"). I translated it as literally as French allows.
5. **SA7.** The keyword "reconcil" and the model's "reconciling us to Himself" are not in the lesson text, so a student cannot learn them from the lesson. The same goes for "humbl" in SA3, where the surgeon is never called humble.
6. **SA5 model** speaks of Dr. Cook's "daughter", while the lesson says "older sister". This is consistent, not wrong.
