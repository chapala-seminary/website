# CTSMatt Unit 13 — French draft review notes

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Matthew 28:9, "Rejoice!"** Segond has "Je vous salue." The lesson (b013) and MC3 rely on "Rejoice!" I quoted Segond in b013 and added one sentence: "Le mot grec employé ici signifie littéralement « Réjouissez-vous ! »" MC3's option reads "Jésus lui-même, qui les salua (littéralement : « Réjouissez-vous ! »)".
2. **Matthew 28:18, "All authority."** Segond has "Tout pouvoir." b025 quotes Segond and adds a sentence saying the Greek word means authority, the right to command. The rest of the lesson says "autorité." Fill8's answer is "pouvoir" (Segond's word), and it also accepts "autorité" and "puissance." SA8 accepts both "autorit" and "pouvoir."
3. **Matthew 28:20, "to the end of the age."** Segond has "jusqu'à la fin du monde." b025 adds that the phrase literally means "l'achèvement de l'âge." In b029, the lesson's own paraphrase "until the age is ended" became "jusqu'à l'achèvement de cet âge." MC16, MC18 and the quotations use Segond's "fin du monde." Is this acceptable for the eschatological balance?
4. **John 21:17, "Feed My sheep."** Segond has "Pais mes brebis." Fill5's answer is "brebis," and it also accepts "moutons" and "ouailles."
5. **John 20:26, "A week later."** Segond has "Huit jours après." I used Segond's phrase in b019 and in the SA5 model.
6. **Matthew 28:13, "stole Him."** Segond has "le dérober." Fill3 was recast around the infinitive. Its answer is "dérober," and it also accepts "voler," "enlever," "emporter" and "prendre."
7. **Immanuel.** Segond spells it "Emmanuel" (Matthew 1:23). Fill10's answer is "Emmanuel," and it also accepts "Immanuel" and "Emanuel."
8. **"Disgust" as the fourth "D."** Following the Spanish ("Rechazo"), I used "rejet" (rejection) in the heading, in b029, MC10 and SA10. The French "dégoût" would sound odd for Saul's hatred. The alliteration (distress, doubt, denial, disgust) is lost in French: the four words are détresse, doute, reniement and rejet. Is that OK?
9. **Short-answer prompts.** I paraphrased SA1, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 9 (for example, "le disciple qui refusa de croire sur parole" instead of "Thomas") so that no prompt passes its own keywords. Is that OK?

## Problems in the English source

- **b019, SA5 model, fill4:** these place Jesus' first appearance (John 20:19-26) in "the upper room." John says only that the disciples were together behind shut doors. This is traditional rather than textual. Translated as is ("chambre haute"), except in the SA5 model.
- **Fill7:** its prompt adds "long journeys to plant churches among the Gentiles," which the lesson never teaches. This is harmless, but the blank is answerable only from the first clause.
- **SA4 keywords "hope" and "promise":** the lesson never says "hope." The model adds that "those who die in Christ will live again," which goes slightly beyond b017. Kept.
- **MC19:** the correct option bundles "his temptation to stay silent" with doubts. In the lesson, silence belongs to the *denial* section (b021), not to what conquered his doubts. This is minor, and the answer key still stands.
- **b021:** "preached Him boldly in the open street" is not in Acts 2, which gives no location. This is minor.
