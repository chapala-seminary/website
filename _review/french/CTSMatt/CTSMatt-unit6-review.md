## Questions for Wayne

1. **Matthew 9:13, "to repentance."** Segond follows the shorter Greek text: "je ne suis pas venu appeler des justes, mais des pécheurs" (no "à la repentance"). The lesson (b023), MC10's `why` and SA6's model all rely on "to repentance." I quoted Segond exactly and added one sentence to b023 saying some Greek manuscripts add "à la repentance," as in Luke 5:32. SA6's model keeps "à la repentance" as a paraphrase. Checker: kept, reworded to "Certains manuscrits grecs ajoutent ici « à la repentance », comme en Luc 5:32" (Luke 5:32 LSG does contain "à la repentance"); this is a Greek-text note, not "other versions say". MC10's option now quotes Segond's exact words with an ellipsis. Please confirm.
2. **Matthew 8:26, "rebuked."** Segond has "menaça" ("threatened"). I quoted Segond and added a sentence in b017 explaining that the Greek means to rebuke with authority, as when giving an order. SA4 accepts "menac" and "réprimand."
3. **Matthew 9:36, "weary and scattered."** Segond has "languissante et abattue." MC17's option uses Segond's wording. I added a sentence in b027 giving the literal Greek sense ("épuisées et jetées çà et là," i.e. scattered). The SA9 keywords cover both wordings.
4. **Matthew 9:38, "Lord of the harvest."** Segond has "le maître de la moisson." MC18 and b030 use "maître." b027 notes that the Greek word also means "Seigneur."
5. **Matthew 9:2, "Son, be of good cheer."** Segond has "Prends courage, mon enfant." I used Segond in b021 and MC8.
6. **Matthew 9:17.** "Wineskins" is "outres" (Segond). The fill-in answer is "outres," and it also accepts "peaux" and "peaux de bouc."
7. **"Gentile."** I used "païen" (the usual French word for non-Jews in a New Testament setting). SA2 also accepts "non juif" and "étranger." Is "païen" acceptable, or would you prefer "non-Juif"?
8. **Short-answer prompts.** SA1, 2, 3, 5 and 8 are paraphrased (for example, "l'homme couché sur un lit" instead of "le paralytique") so that the prompt does not give away its own keywords. Is that OK?
