# CTSBible Unit 7 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **"Evidence" vs. "proof" in French.** The standard French form of the maxim is "L'absence de preuve n'est pas la preuve de l'absence." In it, *preuve* means "evidence", but *preuve* is also our word for "proof" in "corrélation, non preuve". Elsewhere I used **indices** for "evidence" ("les trois choses que peuvent faire les indices") so the two ideas stay separate. I kept the familiar French proverb in b015/b016 and in MC 8 and SA 4. Is that acceptable, or would you prefer "L'absence d'indices n'est pas l'indice d'une absence"? That version is less natural, but it is more consistent.
2. **Hittites / LSG "Héthiens".** LSG never says "Hittites". It says *Héthiens* (checked: e.g. Genèse 15:20, Exode 3:8). In b016 I used the scholarly name "Hittites" and added "(les « Héthiens » de la Bible Segond)" so readers can connect the two. Fill 6 accepts Hittites/Hittite/Héthiens/Héthien.
3. **Matthew 23:24 (b018).** Verified against LSG: "Conducteurs aveugles! qui coulez le moucheron, et qui avalez le chameau." The draft quoted "coule le moucheron", which is not LSG wording. The checker now quotes the exact LSG words "qui coulez le moucheron, et qui avalez le chameau" and explains "straining" as *filtrer avec soin*. The image of "straining a gnat" is less familiar in French than in English. Is the explanation clear enough?
4. **"The LORD gave it into Israel's hand" (b023).** This is a paraphrase, not a quotation. I rendered it as "l'Éternel l'a livrée entre les mains d'Israël", which follows LSG's usual wording. MC 15 says "Dieu", as the English does.
5. **"Apologist".** I used *apologète*, which is the standard French term but may be unfamiliar to Congolese lay readers. The meaning is clear from context ("le croyant enthousiaste, désireux de défendre la Bible").

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC 15 `why`:** It says "It can date the fire", but the lesson (b023) says archaeology can *confirm that a city was destroyed by fire*. This is minor and I translated it as written.
2. **SA 10 keywords:** "extrem" is never taught in the lesson. Only the model answer uses "extremes". I kept an equivalent group (`extrêm/extrem`).
3. **SA 2 keywords:** "excavat" and "excav" are duplicate stems, so they act as one group. In French I merged them as `fouill/excav`.
4. **SA 1 keywords:** In both English and Spanish, "real" also matches "really" and "realize", which can produce false hits. In French I used `réel/reel/réalit/realit`.
5. **Unit-1 dependency (b013, MC 5–7, SA 3):** The lesson relies on the proof / corroboration / illustration framework from Unit 1. That is fine, but the French Unit 1 must use the same terms (*prouver / corroborer / illustrer*, with *indices* for "evidence").
