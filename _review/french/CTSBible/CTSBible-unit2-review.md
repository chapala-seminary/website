# CTSBible Unit 2 — French draft review notes

LSG quotations re-checked by the checker against the offline Louis Segond 1910 text (tools/lsg.py): Luc 24:44, Jean 10:35, Luc 11:51 (and the cited Deutéronome 31:24–26, 2 Timothée 3:16). All quoted wording now matches LSG exactly.

## Questions for Wayne

1. **John 10:35.** NKJV says "cannot be broken" and LSG says "ne peut être anéantie" ("cannot be annihilated / done away with"). The lesson leans on this verse in b017, b026 ("unbreakable"), MC 8, MC 20, Fill 6 and SA 9. I used the LSG wording everywhere, and "unbreakable" became "dont il a dit qu'ils ne peuvent être anéantis". Fill 6 has the answer "anéantie", and "brisée", "annulée" and "abolie" are also accepted. Is that acceptable?
2. **Luke 24:44.** LSG puts "la loi de Moïse… les prophètes… les psaumes" in lower case, and the verb is "il fallait que s'accomplît". I kept LSG as written in b012 and in the MC 5 stem, while the lesson prose uses capitals (la Loi, les Prophètes, les Écrits) for the divisions. Is that mix acceptable?
3. **"God-breathed"** is translated "inspirée de Dieu", the LSG wording in 2 Timothée 3:16. It is less vivid than "soufflée par Dieu". Which do you prefer?
4. **"Assayer"** is translated "essayeur", the correct but uncommon French word. In b009 I glossed it once as "l'expert qui contrôle l'or". Fill 2 also accepts "contrôleur de l'or" and "expert en or". Is that enough for Kinshasa readers?
5. **"Defile the hands"** is translated "rendent les mains impures", the usual French rendering of the Mishnah phrase.
6. **Names and terms.** I used Petits Prophètes (Minor Prophets), Premiers and Derniers Prophètes (Former and Latter), Josèphe, *Contre Apion*, Artaxerxès, Nebucadnetsar (LSG spelling), Zacharie, Yavné, and "Il est écrit" (LSG). The title of section 7 is "Un chameau à ne pas avaler", which echoes Matthieu 23:24 in LSG.
7. **Track names.** I translated them "Certificat" and "Associé" and kept Th.M. and M.Div. as they are. Please confirm these match the other French units.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b021.** The lesson says the Hebrew count reaches "22 … or 24" by the same groupings. Those groupings (the Twelve, Samuel, Kings, Chronicles, Ezra-Nehemiah) give 24. The count of 22 also joins Ruth to Judges and Lamentations to Jeremiah. This is a minor imprecision, and I translated it as written.
2. **b013, b017, MC 10 and SA 4.** "Chronicles stands last" and "Psalms, the first … book of the Writings" are true of the Talmudic and printed order. In the Aleppo and Leningrad codices, however, Chronicles opens the Writings. The argument still holds from the Talmudic order. A short qualification might help.
3. No wrong answer keys, mismatched `why` explanations, untaught exam items or contradicting model answers were found. Every SA item had a model answer.
