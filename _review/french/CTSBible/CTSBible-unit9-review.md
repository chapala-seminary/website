# CTSBible Unit 9 — French draft review notes

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Luke 10:30.** NKJV "A certain man went down"; LSG "Un homme descendait de Jérusalem à Jéricho." The lesson's point about "down" carries over directly. Fill 2's answer is "descendait" (also accepting "descendit" and "descend"). The reference "(Luc 10:30)" was added after the quotation in the lesson, since MC 4 cites it.
2. **Acts 17:6, "politarchs".** LSG does not use the Greek title. It reads "devant les magistrats de la ville." I kept "politarques" in the lesson, MC 10, Fill 7 and SA 5, presenting it as Luke's Greek term, which is what the argument depends on. A Congolese reader who checks his LSG will not find the word there. Should we add a short note such as "(LSG : « magistrats de la ville »)"?
3. **John 9, the Pool of Siloam.** LSG has "réservoir de Siloé", and Bethesda is a "piscine" (John 5:2). I followed LSG for both. That means the "pools" become "piscines et réservoir", and the section title uses "piscines et dallages".
4. **Scripture check (checker).** Every quotation and LSG-based wording was verified against the offline LSG text: Luc 10:30; Marc 10:46, 15:21; Jean 4:5-6, 5:2, 9:7, 11:18, 18:1, 19:13; Luc 24:13; Actes 13:7, 17:6, 18:12, 19:35, 28:7; Marc 4:38. All match. Emmaus is "à soixante stades" (LSG has no "about"), and Bethany is "à quinze stades environ". Acts 19:35 LSG has only "le secrétaire", so "secrétaire de la ville" is a description, not a quotation.

## Problems in the English source

1. **Bethesda's "five-sided" shape** (lesson §4, Fill 5). The excavated pool is a trapezoid made of two basins. The five porticoes are usually explained as four porticoes around the edges plus one across the middle dividing wall, not a five-sided pool. I translated the claim as written. It should be corrected in the English too.
2. **MC 9 says Luke is right "every time".** The lesson's wording is softer: "in test after test… he gets it right." This is a small overstatement in the exam.
3. **SA keyword lists** (for Robert). Every `keywords_en` list is really 4 concepts, each given in English and Spanish ("geography", "geograf", "land", "tierra"…). In the English site, 3 hits may therefore need only 2 concepts, because "geography" also matches "geograf". In French I wrote 8 distinct concept groups, so the French grading is somewhat stricter than the English.
4. **Fill 3's alternate "Arabah"** is not taught in the lesson. In French I accepted "Salée" ("mer Salée" is an LSG name for the Dead Sea) and did not include "plaine".

## Adaptations

- I converted imperial units to metric for Congolese readers:
  - "about seventeen miles", "more than three thousand feet" → "environ vingt-sept kilomètres", "plus de neuf cents mètres" (lesson and MC 4).
  - "seven hundred feet below" the level of the sea → "quelque deux cents mètres" (lesson and SA 3).
- MC 13's distractors "A hundred miles" and "One mile / two miles" became kilometres.
- "The spade has answered them" became "les fouilles leur ont répondu", because the English idiom does not work in French.
