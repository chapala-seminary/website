## Questions for Wayne

1. **LSG verified.** A checker looked up every quotation in the offline LSG 1910 text (Luc 1:3–4, 2 Timothée 3:16, 2 Pierre 1:21, Matthieu 23:24, 24:35, Romains 1:20). All match verbatim. "Ainsi parle l'Éternel" and "Il est écrit" are standard LSG formulas.
2. **2 Timothée 3:16.** NKJV has "given by inspiration of God". LSG has "Toute Écriture est inspirée de Dieu". Fill 2 now blanks the participle: answer "inspirée", and "inspiration" is also accepted. MC4 quotes the LSG wording.
3. **2 Pierre 1:21.** NKJV has "holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit". LSG follows a different Greek text: "c'est poussés par le Saint-Esprit que des hommes ont parlé de la part de Dieu". It has no "holy" and no "of God" after "men". The lesson does not depend on those words, so I quoted LSG as it stands.
4. **Matthieu 23:24.** LSG reads "qui coulez le moucheron, et qui avalez le chameau". Most readers today will not know "couler" in the sense of "to filter", so I added a short note in the lesson: « Couler » veut dire ici filtrer. Elsewhere I use "filtrer les moucherons" (lesson, Fill 8, MC18, SA8). Please confirm that you are happy with this added explanation.
5. **Romains 1:20.** For NKJV "eternal power and Godhead", LSG has "sa puissance éternelle et sa divinité". MC9's explanation follows this and says "sa divinité".
6. **"Evidence" vs "proof".** In French, *preuve* covers both words. Unit 1 depends on the difference between them, so I used *preuve au sens strict* for proof (b022, MC14, SA6). For evidence I mostly used *preuves* or *éléments de preuve*. The heading "Rules of Evidence" became "Les règles de la preuve", which is the standard legal term in French. A French reader should confirm that this stays clear.
7. **"Enacted Word" (baptism and Lord's Supper).** I rendered this as "la Parole en acte" and used "la Sainte Cène". Both words are neutral across denominations.
8. **Dynamic view.** I used "conception dynamique" and "théorie de la dictée / naturelle / partielle".

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC6 explanation:** "Absolute authority is an offensive posture." The lesson never uses "offensive" (it contrasts throne vs. fortress). Translated faithfully as "posture offensive, et non défensive".
2. **SA4 model answer:** it gives "conscience" as part of general revelation and says inspiration put the word down "accurately". The lesson mentions only creation (Romans 1:20) and does not say "accurately". I kept "conscience" next to "création" and dropped "accurately". Please confirm.
3. **Fill 8 / b024 imagery.** In Jesus' saying, straining out gnats means being fussy about small things. The lesson uses "strains at gnats" for the believer who overclaims, which is not quite the same idea. I translated it as written. Note that "strain at" is the KJV wording; NKJV has "strain out".
4. **MC10 explanation:** it lists the five forms as "Living, Preached, Natural, Written, Enacted". The lesson calls the second one "the preached and prophetic Word". This is consistent, just shorter, so there is nothing to fix.
