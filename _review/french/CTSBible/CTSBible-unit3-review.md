# CTSBible Unit 3 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **"Straining a gnat" (b027, SA6).** LSG Matthieu 23:24 reads "qui coulez le moucheron, et qui avalez le chameau." I used « couler le moucheron » in quotation marks. Students searching their Bible for "gnat" will find "moucheron". The SA6 keyword is "moucheron".
2. **"From Abel to Zechariah" (b027).** LSG Luc 11:51 says "depuis le sang d'Abel jusqu'au sang de Zacharie", and I quoted it that way. The NKJV phrase in the lesson is a shortened form without "blood".
3. **LSG verification (checker).** Both verses were checked against the offline LSG 1910 text: Matthieu 23:24 (« qui coulez le moucheron ») and Luc 11:51 (« depuis le sang d'Abel jusqu'au sang de Zacharie ») match exactly. No other Scripture is quoted in this unit.
4. **"Breathed-out Word" (b009, b029).** I wrote "la Parole inspirée, soufflée par Dieu". This ties to LSG 2 Timothée 3:16 ("inspirée de Dieu") and keeps the "breathed" image. In b018, "God breathed them out" became "Dieu les a inspirés".
5. **"Apocryphes" vs "deutérocanoniques".** French Catholics use "apocryphes" for what Protestants call the pseudepigrapha (Enoch and similar books). For the books Protestants call "Apocrypha" they say "deutérocanoniques". I kept "apocryphes" as the Protestant term, and b023 gives "livres deutérocanoniques / deutérocanon" as the Catholic and Orthodox name. A Congolese Catholic reader could still be confused by this.
6. **Sirach.** I used "le Siracide". Many French Catholic Bibles call this book "l'Ecclésiastique", but that would clash with Jerome's term « ecclésiastique » in b027, MC18, Fill 10 and SA8, so I left it out.
7. **Antilegomena (b011, Fill 3).** I kept the Greek word in italics and glossed it as « contestés ». The fill-in also accepts the French spelling "antilégomènes".
8. **Muratorian Fragment.** The standard French name is "fragment de Muratori", so the Fill 4 answer is "Muratori" (it also accepts "muratorien").

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC16 option B and SA6 model.** They mention a fixed first-century "Alexandrian canon", but the lesson never names or discusses it. b027 only says the codices are "not a fixed canon of Jesus' day". The SA6 model's "set a believer up to stumble" is also not in the lesson.
2. **MC7.** It names Athanasius's "Festal Letter", but the lesson (b011) only says "in 367 Athanasius names all twenty-seven". The item can still be answered.
3. **MC20 option B ("73 books").** The Catholic count of 73 is never taught. It is a distractor only, so this is minor.
4. **Possible factual problem in b027: the lower tier "held for a thousand years, until Trent".** The same Councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397) that b018 praises also put Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach and Maccabees in their Old Testament lists. The Council of Florence (1442) listed them too. Trent was the first to make it *dogma*, but "a thousand years" of an unbroken lower tier overstates the case. I translated it faithfully. You may want to add a qualifying sentence in English.
5. **MC17 / SA9 say "1 Enoch"; the lesson says "Enoch".** This is consistent enough, and I kept both forms.

No answer keys were found to be wrong. The source had a model answer for every short-answer item.

All 10 models hit 7–8 groups; no prompt hits more than 1.

## Adaptations

- KJV → "la Bible du roi Jacques (King James)" (b023, b027, MC19), since few Congolese readers know the KJV by name.
- b023: after "absent from most Protestant Bibles today" I added "comme la Louis Segond", the Protestant Bible these readers actually hold. Delete it if you prefer strict fidelity.
- Track names: Certificat / Associé; Th.M. and M.Div. are kept as they are.
- In b009 and b018, the "assayer" became "essayeur", the correct French technical term. The French reader should judge whether "un essayeur d'or" or "un orfèvre qui certifie l'or" would be clearer in Kinshasa.
