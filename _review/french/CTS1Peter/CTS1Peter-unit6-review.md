## Questions for Wayne

1. **1 Peter 2:20, "commendable before God" (b011, MC2, Fill 1, SA1).** Segond has "c'est une grâce devant Dieu". I quoted Segond and added one sentence: the Greek word means what is pleasing to God and worthy of his approval. MC2's stem quotes « une grâce devant Dieu ». Fill 1 answer: **grâce**. It also accepts louable, chose louable, chose agréable and faveur.
2. **1 Peter 2:18, "the harsh" (b011, MC1, SA1).** Segond has "ceux qui sont d'un caractère difficile". MC1 follows Segond. SA1 also accepts sévère, cruel and brutal.
3. **1 Peter 2:22, "deceit" (b018, MC9, Fill 6).** Segond has "fraude". I added a gloss: the Greek means cunning or trickery. Fill 6 answer: **fraude**. It also accepts tromperie, ruse and mensonge.
4. **1 Peter 2:23, "reviled… committed Himself" (b020, b021, MC13, Fill 7, SA7).** Segond has "injurié, ne rendait point d'injures… s'en remettait à celui qui juge justement". The heading and exam items follow Segond.
5. **1 Peter 2:24, "on the tree" (b022, b024, Fill 8).** Segond has "sur le bois". In b024 I added "Le « bois », c'est ici la croix". Fill 8 answer: **bois**. It also accepts croix and arbre. Segond's "meurtrissures" stands for "stripes".
6. **1 Peter 2:25, "Shepherd and Overseer" (b027, b028, Fill 10).** Segond has "le pasteur et le gardien de vos âmes". I added a gloss: the Greek means "one who watches over", which is where the word "évêque" comes from. Fill 10 answer: **gardien**. It also accepts évêque, surveillant and protecteur. Segond writes "pasteur" in lower case. Outside quotations I capitalized "le Pasteur et le Gardien" to match the English.
7. **Gethsemane prayer (b016, MC8).** The English, "Take this cup from Me. Nevertheless, not My will, but Thine, be done", blends Luke 22:42 and Mark 14:36. I quoted Luke 22:42 in Segond and added the reference.
8. **Hebrews 4:15 (b019).** The English has "a High Priest who can be touched with the feeling of our weaknesses". I used Segond's "souverain sacrificateur" (convention 4) and quoted « compatir à nos faiblesses ».
9. **"Fence-crawler" (b027, MC20, SA9).** The drafter's coinage « franchisseuse de clôtures » is not natural French; the checker replaced it with « brebis fugueuse » plus a description (« qui se faufilait sans cesse à travers la clôture »). A French reader may still prefer another phrase.
10. **"Commendable" in the English SA1 keywords ("commend").** The French group is grâce/louable/agréable, so a student who uses either Segond's word or the plain word gets the hit.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b025 / MC18 / Fill 9, George Wilson.** In *United States v. Wilson* (1833), Chief Justice Marshall did rule that a pardon must be accepted. But the historical record does not show that Wilson was hanged. President Jackson had already pardoned the death sentence, and the case concerned the pardon not being pleaded for his other conviction. The sermon form of the story ("George Wilson was hanged") is widely repeated but doubtful. I translated it faithfully.
2. **b012, "some sixty million slaves".** This figure, popularised by William Barclay, is much higher than most current estimates for the Roman Empire (roughly 5–10 million). I translated it as written.
3. **SA prompts that hit their own keywords.** In the English, SA1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 9 pass on the prompt alone. I reworded the French prompts so that none scores more than 1 hit.
4. **SA6 keyword "appeal".** The lesson never uses this idea, so a student cannot learn it from the lesson. The French group (attrait/attirer/séduire) matches the lesson's "seuls les vrais fruits les attirent".
5. **SA4 keyword "lower".** This comes from "take the lower place". In French it is « la dernière place », and I keyed it that way.
6. All MC answer keys are correct, and each `why` matches its own question.

## Adaptations

- In b025, I made it explicit that George Wilson was American and that the President and the Supreme Court were those of the United States (« le président des États-Unis », « la Cour suprême des États-Unis »), so that Congolese readers do not take them for their own institutions.
- *In His Steps* (b016) keeps its English title, with the French gloss « Sur ses traces ».
- I kept the sports and choir examples in b015 as they are. They work in Kinshasa.
