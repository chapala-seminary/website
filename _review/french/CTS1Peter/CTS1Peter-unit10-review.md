## Questions for Wayne

1. **1 Peter 4:10, "steward(s)".** LSG has "dispensateurs" (literally, people who hand things out), not "steward/manager". The unit title, b020 and MC14 rest on "steward = manager". I used Segond's "dispensateur" throughout, added "Le mot grec désigne l'intendant d'une maison" in b020, then kept "un dispensateur est un gérant". Fill 6 answer "gérant"; it also accepts "intendant", "administrateur", "gestionnaire", "économe". The unit title is "Dispensateurs de la grâce". Please confirm, or would you prefer "intendants" in headings?
2. **1 Peter 4:10, "manifold grace".** LSG has "des diverses grâces de Dieu" (plural, "various"). b022 builds on "manifold = many-colored". I quoted Segond and added "le mot grec signifie littéralement « multicolore »" (convention 1). Fill 8 answer "diverses"; it also accepts "variées", "multiformes", "multicolores".
3. **1 Peter 4:8, "fervent love".** LSG has "une ardente charité … La charité couvre". I used Segond's wording in quotes and Fill 4 (answer "ardente", accepts "fervente/fervent"). I added one sentence in b018 saying that "charité" here translates *agapè*, brotherly love, because modern French "charité" usually means almsgiving. Outside quotes I use "amour".
4. **1 Peter 4:7, "be serious and watchful in your prayers".** LSG: "Soyez donc sages et sobres, pour vaquer à la prière" — no "watchful". The lesson, MC10 and SA9 use "watchful". I quoted Segond and added in b016 that the Greek word rendered "sobres" also means staying awake and watchful (strictly, Peter uses two verbs, *sōphronēsate* and *nēpsate*; *nēphō* carries the "watchful" sense). MC10 reads "Être sages et sobres, vigilants dans la prière".
5. **1 Peter 4:1, "he … has ceased from sin".** LSG: "en a fini avec le péché". This matches the sense well; MC2 uses it.
6. **1 Peter 4:3, "enough".** LSG: "C'est assez". Fill 2 answer "assez" (accepts "bien assez", "suffisant", "suffit").
7. **1 Peter 4:4, "flood of dissipation".** LSG: "le même débordement de débauche". Quoted as Segond; the lesson's "rushing stream" image is kept in my own words ("torrent d'excès").
8. **"A thousand years are as a day" (b016).** The lesson alludes to it without a reference. I quoted 2 Pierre 3:8 (LSG), with the reference added. Note that Segond's order is "un jour est comme mille ans, et mille ans sont comme un jour".
9. **1 Peter 4:11, "oracles of God".** LSG: "comme annonçant les oracles de Dieu". MC19 option reads "Annonçant les oracles (les paroles mêmes) de Dieu".

## Problems in the English source

1. **SA keywords.** In the English, several prompts reach 3+ of their own keywords (SA1: christ, arm, mind, will, god; SA6: steward, …; SA8: steward, grace, god, gift; SA9: end, near, prayer, love, serve; SA10: end, …). Following convention 10, I chose French groups so that each prompt reaches at most 2 hits. Several English groups are also generic ("god", "one", "life", "will").
2. **b016 "seventy years".** This is presented as an "average" lifespan. It echoes Psalm 90:10, but it is not a statistic. I translated it as written.
3. **b020 "nearly half of the parables … touch on stewardship".** This is a commonly repeated claim, and the estimates vary widely. I translated it as written ("On a fait remarquer…").
4. **b021 C. T. Studd.** Calling him "the finest cricket player in all of England" overstates it. He was a leading England cricketer of the 1880s, but not clearly the best. The money he sent at the end of his life is usually told about his inheritance gifts in 1887, which went partly to Booth's Salvation Army, among others. Please check this anecdote. I translated it as written.
5. **b013 on 1 Peter 4:6.** The lesson gives one interpretation (believers now dead) as settled. Other evangelical readings exist. I translated it faithfully; I am only noting it because Unit 9 presented the views even-handedly.
