## Questions for Wayne

1. **1 Peter 1:1, "pilgrims of the Dispersion".** LSG has "à ceux qui sont étrangers et dispersés", so there is no noun "Dispersion" and no word "pilgrims". The lesson relies on both words (b015 heading, b016, b017, b030, MC4, SA2). I quoted LSG and used "Étrangers et dispersés" as the heading. In b016 I added: "Le texte grec dit littéralement « aux étrangers de la dispersion »". The lesson then explains the two words *dispersion* (diaspora) and *étrangers*, with "pèlerins" given as another rendering. Fill 4 (diaspora) still works.
2. **1 Peter 1:2, "for obedience and sprinkling of the blood".** LSG has "afin qu'ils deviennent obéissants, et qu'ils participent à l'aspersion du sang de Jésus-Christ". The meaning is the same, but the wording is longer. I used it verbatim in b024, b026, MC16 and the SA5 prompt.
3. **Exodus 24:8, "sprinkled it on the people".** LSG has "il le répandit sur le peuple" ("poured/threw"), which weakens the link to the "aspersion" of 1 Peter 1:2 that b024/b026 depend on. In b026 I quoted LSG and added: "Le verbe hébreu traduit ici par « répandit » signifie jeter ou asperger : c'est le même geste que Pierre appelle l'aspersion." Elsewhere I wrote "répandit le sang", or "sang répandu en aspersion" for the image (b024, MC17, Fill 8). Please confirm.
4. **"Foreknowledge" / "foreordained".** "Prescience" (LSG) is used throughout, and it is the Fill 6 answer; "préconnaissance" and "connaissance anticipée" are also accepted. For the Reformed reading in b020, I rendered "foreordained them beforehand" as "l'a prédestiné d'avance", and for the other reading "savait d'avance qui croirait". Both views are kept equally weighted, as in the English. Please have the French reader confirm that "prédestiné" does not sound more loaded than the English "foreordained".
5. **b013, John 21 and Luke 22:62.** "Do you love Me?" / "Feed My sheep" use LSG's "M'aimes-tu?" / "Pais mes brebis", and "went out and wept bitterly" follows LSG "étant sorti, il pleura amèrement". 1 Peter 5:1 is quoted as LSG: "témoin des souffrances de Christ".
6. **"Jewish or Gentile" (b017)** became "d'origine juive ou non juive", not "païenne", following the Acts convention.
7. **Fill 1, "rock".** The answer is "roc"; I also accept "rocher", "roche" and "pierre". "Pierre" is accepted because the French name itself means "stone/rock". Please confirm.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b016 / b017 overlap.** Both paragraphs explain the five provinces, "now Turkey", the resident-alien image and heavenly citizenship. **b024 / b026** also both retell Exodus 24 and the covenant blood. These look like two drafts that were both kept. I translated both pairs.
2. **MC7** quotes "strangers and pilgrims", which is the wording of 1 Peter 2:11, not 1:1. In French I used LSG 2:11's "étrangers et voyageurs", which still fits.
3. **SA keywords.** The English prompts alone hit their own keywords in SA3 (4 hits), SA4 (5), SA5 (4), SA7 (4) and SA8 (4), so a student could pass by copying the question. Under convention 10, I replaced those groups in French with distinct ideas from the lesson. Each French prompt now reaches at most 2 hits.
4. **SA1 model** says Peter "strengthens his brethren, exactly as Jesus foretold" (Luke 22:32). **SA9 model** mentions "an unshakable inheritance kept in heaven" (1:4). The lesson does not teach either point. The French SA1 model leaves out the Luke 22:32 point; the French SA9 model keeps the inheritance phrase (as the English does), but neither point is used as a keyword.

Validator note: the warning on Fill 4 is a false alarm. "Diaspora" is a single technical term with only one correct answer.
