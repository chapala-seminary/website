# CTSJohn Unit 8 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **John 11:43 "Lazarus, come forth!"**: Segond has "Lazare, sors!" ("Lazarus, go out!"). I quoted Segond in b024, b026, sa7 and fill 8. In b024 I added one sentence: "Le texte grec dit littéralement : « Lazare, ici, dehors ! »" Fill 8 answer is "sors". It also accepts "sors dehors", "viens dehors", "dehors" and "sortez", which match the NKJV sense.
2. **John 11:25 "though he may die, he shall live"**: Segond reverses the order: "Celui qui croit en moi vivra, quand même il serait mort" ("will live, even if he were dead"). MC 10 now blanks the end of the verse: "quand même il serait ___", with options tombé / endormi / **mort** / dans le doute. The correct index (2) is unchanged.
3. **John 11:39 "by this time he stinks, for he has been dead four days"**: Segond has "il sent déjà, car il y a quatre jours qu'il est là" ("he already smells, for he has been there four days"). I followed Segond in the b024 quotation. MC 12's option is not a quotation, so it now says "était mort depuis quatre jours et sentait déjà", which keeps the English sense ("dead four days").
4. **John 11:44 "grave clothes"**: Segond has "bandes" (strips of cloth). I used "bandes" throughout (b024, b026, sa7, sa8) instead of a separate word for "grave clothes".
5. **"Permissive will of God"**: I rendered it "volonté permissive de Dieu", the standard French theological term. Fill 4 accepts "permissive", "permissif" and "permissives". Please confirm the term is acceptable.
6. **John 11:3**: Segond includes "voici" ("Seigneur, voici, celui que tu aimes est malade"). I kept it in b015 and MC 1 option D.
7. **John 11:24**: In b022 Martha's words are quoted with "[…]", because Segond puts "lui répondit Marthe" in the middle of the verse.

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC 15 and fill 9 (and b024)**: The lesson says "Jesus likened death to a sleep" but goes straight on to the child-on-the-couch picture, which is Cook's illustration and not Jesus' (John 11:11–13 is never cited). MC 15's correct option attributes "the Father carries us to wake in the right place" to Jesus. I translated it faithfully.
2. **SA 7 model**: It ends with "all who are in the graves will hear that same voice" (John 5:28). The lesson never teaches this; its closing says the voice will call "every believer". I translated it as written.
3. **SA 3 model**: It says the sickness came "that the people might believe" (compare John 11:15, 42). The lesson never mentions that purpose.
4. **SA 1 model**: It says "the triumph of death" where the lesson heading says "triumph over death". I used "triomphe sur la mort".
5. **SA keywords**: Several English keyword lists repeat words from the prompt (for example sa1 tragedy/trial/tears/triumph, sa5 jesus/wept, sa6 resurrection/life, sa7 lazarus/come/forth), and sa2 includes the unrelated "world". Following convention 10, I chose 8 distinct French ideas from each model, and none of the French prompts passes its own keywords.
6. **Fill 1**: There is only one alternate ("4"). This is a number with one clear answer, so the validator's warning here is a false alarm.

## Adaptations

- **Heading b012**: "Dead Man Walking" is a US prison phrase and film title. I made it "Un mort qui marche", which reads naturally without the US reference.
- **"falls asleep on the couch"**: I wrote "s'endort au salon" (falls asleep in the sitting room), a more general setting.
- **Exam button**: "Submit Exam & Check Pass" uses the wording already used elsewhere in the French edition: "Soumettre l'examen &amp; vérifier la réussite".
