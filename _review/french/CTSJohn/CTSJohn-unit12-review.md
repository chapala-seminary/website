## Questions for Wayne

1. **"It is finished" (19:30).** LSG says "Tout est accompli". I used this wording for the unit title, the b020 heading, b021 and the closing line of b028. In b021 the contrast "He did not say, 'I am finished'" became « C'en est fini de moi ».
2. **"I am He" (18:5-6).** LSG says "C'est moi", so the "I AM" echo is lost. Following convention 1, I added one phrase in b015: "le texte grec dit littéralement « Je suis »". MC 2 uses "C'est moi".
3. **Job 14:14.** LSG reads "Si l'homme une fois mort pouvait revivre…", which is a wish, not a question. In b025 I quoted LSG and then gave the Hebrew sense, "Si l'homme meurt, revivra-t-il ?". MC 18 and the SA 9 model use the literal question and mark it as such.
4. **"Gave up His spirit."** LSG says "rendit l'esprit". The lesson stresses that the giving was active, so b021 adds that the Greek literally means he "livra" (handed over) his spirit.
5. **"Blessed are those…" (20:29).** LSG says "Heureux ceux qui n'ont pas vu". Fill 8 answer: "vu".
6. **"Feed My sheep" (21:17).** LSG says "Pais mes brebis". Fill 9 answer: "brebis". It also accepts "moutons", "agneaux" and "troupeau".
7. **Mary Magdalene.** LSG says "Marie de Magdala". At first mention I wrote "Marie de Magdala (Marie-Madeleine)". A French prompt cannot cleanly split "____ Magdalene", so Fill 6 blanks the whole name. Answer: "Marie de Magdala". It also accepts "Marie-Madeleine", "Marie" and "Madeleine".
8. **"He saved others; let Him save Himself" (b018).** John does not record this taunt. I used LSG Luc 23:35 ("Il a sauvé les autres; qu'il se sauve lui-même"), which is closest to the English wording, in b018, MC 9 and SA 3.
9. **Smaller LSG choices:**
   - "Behold the Man!" became « Voici l'homme ».
   - "chief priests" became "principaux sacrificateurs".
   - "your King" became lowercase "votre roi", as in LSG.
   - "grave-clothes" became "bandes de lin" (LSG "les bandes").
   - "the right to become children of God" became "le pouvoir" (LSG Jean 1:12).
   - "more than five hundred" became "cinq cents frères" (1 Co 15:6).
10. **Fill 1** answer: "Gethsémané" (LSG Matthieu 26:36). It also accepts "jardin des Oliviers", the common French name for the place. Please confirm that this alternate is acceptable.
11. **Short-answer prompts.** Several prompts were reworded (SA 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10) so that the prompt alone does not pass its own keywords (convention 10). The meaning of each prompt is unchanged.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b021 and Fill 5.** The lesson says "In the language Jesus spoke, the word was one syllable — tetelestai". Tetelestai is Greek, the language John wrote in, and it has four syllables; Jesus probably spoke Aramaic on the cross. I translated it as "un seul mot" (one word), as the Spanish edition does. The claim about "the language Jesus spoke" is unchanged. *(Updated 12 Oct 2026 after the English correction; see "Updated after English corrections" above.)*
2. **b019.** In the Rembrandt painting where the artist put himself in the scene, he appears in *The Raising of the Cross* (1633). The lesson says the painting of the three crosses. The illustration was translated as written. *(Updated 12 Oct 2026 after the English correction; see "Updated after English corrections" above.)*
3. **b027 (George Smith).** The Moravian missionary was Georg Schmidt, in South Africa from 1737. Popular accounts of his story vary: he baptized several converts, and he died in Germany. The lesson's details may be the sermon-illustration version of the story. It was translated as written. *(Updated 12 Oct 2026 after the English correction; see "Updated after English corrections" above.)*
4. **MC 1** says "In Gethsemane", but John 18:1 only says "a garden". The name comes from Matthew and Mark. This is minor.
5. **SA 6 model** adds that the eyewitness testimony "authenticates" the resurrection "like an eyewitness in court". The lesson does not say this in b024, though b028's courtroom theme supports it. It was translated as written.
6. I found no wrong answer keys. Every SA item has a model answer.

## Adaptations

- **b015.** The English uses Benedict Arnold as a byword for betrayal. Congolese readers would not know him, so I replaced him with Julius Caesar's cry, « Toi aussi, mon fils ! », which is widely known in francophone schooling. Note that this is a different Caesar from the one in 19:15. If you prefer to avoid that, the alternative is "le nom même de Judas est devenu synonyme de traître".
