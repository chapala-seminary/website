## Questions for Wayne

1. **Psalm 24:4 "clean hands".** LSG has "les mains innocentes" (innocent hands), not "clean hands". I used "mains innocentes" everywhere: b013/b021/b022/b024, MC14, MC18 and Fill 6. The SA keyword groups also accept "mains pures / propres / nettes". In the Hebrides story, the deacon's own question ("are our hearts clean?") became "nos cœurs sont-ils purifiés ?" (b024, MC17, Fill 7).
2. **Psalm 24:4 "has not lifted up his soul to an idol".** LSG reads "ne livre pas son âme au mensonge" (to falsehood), so it has no idol. The lesson does not depend on this phrase. The SA6 model says "ni aux idoles ni au mensonge", which covers both readings.
3. **Psalm 24:3 "stand in His holy place".** LSG reads "Qui s'élèvera jusqu'à son lieu saint?" I matched b013 ("le Juge qui décide qui peut s'élever jusqu'à son lieu saint"), MC6 and MC14 to that wording.
4. **Psalm 24:7 "Lift up your heads, O you gates".** LSG has "Portes, élevez vos linteaux" (raise your lintels). The image of gates "lifted high and wide" in b027/b031 still works, so I kept it.
5. **Psalm 24:5.** In LSG the verse goes on: "La miséricorde du Dieu de son salut". As in the NKJV excerpt, I stopped the quotation at "la bénédiction de l'Éternel".
6. **Joshua 7:21.** "Robe from Babylon" is "manteau de Schinear" in LSG, and "wedge of gold" is "lingot d'or". I added "(c'est-à-dire de Babylonie)" so the lesson's point stays clear. Please confirm that you are happy with this added explanation.
7. **Matthew 21:33.** LSG has "haie" (hedge), where the lesson says "wall", and "l'afferma à des vignerons". The paraphrase in b016 follows LSG.
8. **Isaiah 44:3.** Quoted exactly from LSG: "Car je répandrai des eaux sur le sol altéré, Et des ruisseaux sur la terre desséchée".
9. **Ark terms.** I used the LSG words throughout: "propitiatoire" for Mercy Seat (also the Fill 8 answer), "jour des Expiations" for Day of Atonement, and "souverain sacrificateur" for High Priest. "Propitiatoire" is the standard term but may be unfamiliar to lay readers, so a short explanation could be added if you wish.
10. **MC distractors quoting Scripture.** Checked against LSG: Ps 13:1 (MC5, MC17), Ps 2:1, Ps 8:4, Lc 18:22, Lc 10:7 / 1 Tm 5:18, 2 Rois 2:14 and Ps 85:6 are all verbatim. Ps 85:6 in LSG is "Ne nous rendras-tu pas à la vie ?", which loses the word "revive", but it is only a wrong option. MC11's distractor "L'argent est la racine de tous les maux" keeps the popular misquote, as the English does ("Money is the root of all evil"); LSG 1 Tm 6:10 says "l'amour de l'argent est une racine de tous les maux". Fine as a wrong option.
11. **Names.** I used the LSG forms: Uzza, Acan, Obed-Edom, Bethléhem, Béthanie. Fill 2 accepts Uzzah/Uza/Ouzza, and Fill 5 accepts Achan.
12. **"Tenant farmers" / "sharecropper".** These became "fermiers" (also the Fill 4 answer) and "métayer". I kept Carlson's book title in English with the gloss « Le métayer ».
13. **"Preoccupation".** I rendered it as "préoccupation" (Fill 9). It also has the sense of "worry" in French, but the lesson's explanation makes the meaning clear.
14. **"Faith then new birth" and similar.** Nothing in this unit touches the soteriology guardrails. "Le Seigneur de la grâce libre et fidèle, qui ne leur imputait pas leurs péchés" (b027) follows the English closely.

## Problems in the English source

1. **SA2 model vs lesson.** The lesson (b013, MC6) says the King is "the Judge who decides who may stand in His holy place". The SA2 model calls Him "the One worthy to stand in His own sanctuary", which is a different idea. I translated it as written.
2. **b013** says there are "two important questions" but names only one. The second (v. 3, "Who may ascend…?") is only implied.
3. **MC17, MC18, SA7** give the date 1949 for the Hebrides Revival. The lesson never gives a date.
4. **MC19 and SA8** speak of "broken" commandments or tablets inside the ark. SA8's "broken tablets of the law" could suggest the smashed first tablets, which were not in the ark. Following the lesson, I rendered it as transgressed ("loi transgressée", "commandements transgressés").
5. **SA1 model** says the cart was used "rather than as God had commanded". The lesson does not teach this; it only mentions that the Levites were prepared and that holiness was taken lightly. I translated it anyway.
6. **SA5 model** mentions Ai, "devoted things" and the tithe, none of which are in the lesson. These details are accurate but go beyond what the lesson teaches.
7. **MC16 why** adds the phrase "giving Christ our best and our all", which is not in the lesson. This is minor.
8. **SA10** has the keyword "too late". The lesson never uses that idea, so the French model adds "trop tard" to reach it.
9. **b012** is labelled "Psalm 24:1" but quotes verses 1–2. I changed the label to "Psaume 24:1-2".
10. All 20 MC answer keys check out against the lesson, and no answer key is wrong.

15. **SA10 prompt.** The draft reworded it as "what lesson…", dropping "warning about welcoming the King". A fully literal French prompt would by itself score 4 keyword hits (the English prompt also does). The checker used "Quel avertissement… quant à la manière dont nous recevons Christ ?", which keeps the idea of a warning and scores only 2 hits. Please confirm.
