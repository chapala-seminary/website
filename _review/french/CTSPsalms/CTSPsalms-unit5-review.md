# CTSPsalms Unit 5 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Ps 119:11 "hidden in my heart" vs. LSG "Je serre ta parole dans mon cœur".** LSG says "serrer" (to hold or keep close), not "cacher" (to hide). I quoted LSG and built the section on it. The heading is "Serrée dans le cœur", and the prose, MC11, MC12, fill 7 and SA6 all use "serrer". The SA6 keyword group accepts both "serr" and "cach".
2. **Ps 119:89 "settled" vs. LSG "subsiste".** The fill 8 answer is "subsiste". It also accepts demeure, est établie, est fixée and reste. MC13 and SA7 use "subsiste". The b024 phrase "settled forever in heaven" becomes "elle subsiste à toujours dans les cieux".
3. **Ps 119:97 "It is my meditation all the day" vs. LSG "Elle est tout le jour l'objet de ma méditation".** Fill 9 now blanks "l'objet de ma ____", and the answer is still "méditation".
4. **John 1:14 "the Word made flesh".** LSG has "la parole a été faite chair" (feminine, lowercase *parole*). I wrote "la Parole faite chair" and did not use the older "le Verbe". Fill 2 answer is "chair".
5. **James 1:5 "gives to all liberally" vs. LSG "donne à tous simplement".** I quoted LSG. MC6 option B and its `why` say "simplement" so they match the verse.
6. **John 4:23 in b032.** NKJV/lesson: "...in spirit and truth, and that is the kind of worshiper God is seeking." LSG ends the verse with "car ce sont là les adorateurs que le Père demande". I quoted the LSG clause and left out the lesson's separate paraphrase, so the idea is not said twice. Is that acceptable?
7. **Exodus 18 "God-honoring men"** became "des hommes craignant Dieu", the LSG phrase in Ex 18:21. Fill 5 accepts Jéthro, Jétro, Réuel (the LSG form in Ex 2:18) and Raguel (kept because the English accepts it, though LSG does not use it).
8. **"It sounds like Jesus"** (b013, MC8) became "elle ressemble à Jésus", meaning "it resembles Jesus". A literal "sonne comme Jésus" is not natural French. Please confirm.
9. **Romans 5:20 in b033.** "Where sin abounds, grace abounds much more" is given in LSG wording ("là où le péché a abondé, la grâce a surabondé"), without quotation marks or a reference, as in the English. MC20 and SA10 use the same wording.

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC3 and its `why`.** They say Psalms 19 and 119 are "devoted entirely/wholly" to the Word of God. The lesson (b009) only calls them "two great psalms of the Word". Also, Psalm 19:1-6 is about creation, so "entirely" is not accurate for Psalm 19. I translated the item as written.
2. **MC14, MC15 and b024 (Henry V).** The 1414 statute under Henry V was aimed at Lollards and at reading Scripture in English (Wycliffe's Bible), not at reading any Bible at all. The lesson's wording overstates it. I translated it faithfully.
3. **MC16.** The stem pairs a New Testament that "would not burn" with Bible pages that "survived" in Chile. The Chile pages were washed ashore by water, not saved from fire. The item is acceptable, but the wording is loose.
4. **SA6 keyword "memorize".** The lesson never uses the word "memorize" (it says "hidden in the heart"). The French group accepts mémoris…/par cœur. The model answer uses "par cœur".
5. **SA3 keyword "Solomon".** The model answer never mentions Solomon. The French model still hits 6 of 8 groups.
6. **SA prompts reworded.** The literal English prompts for SA4 (Jethro, Moses, counsel, godly, wisdom), SA5 (lamp, feet, Savior…) and SA8 (Brazil, Chile, living power, Word) would score 3 or more hits on their own. I rephrased them without naming those words. The questions they ask are unchanged.
