# CTSGalatians Unit 8: review notes

## Questions for Wayne
1. **Galatians 5:6, "faith working through love"**: Segond says "la foi qui est agissante par la **charité**". I quoted Segond in b004, b025 and MC13, and the MC13 answer is "charité". In b025 I added one sentence saying that the Greek word is *agapè*, meaning love. The b024 heading reads "La foi agissante par la charité".
2. **James 2:26, "as the body without the spirit"**: Segond says "sans **âme**". I quoted it as written. The lesson does not depend on this wording.
3. **Galatians 1:8-9, "under God's curse"**: Segond says "qu'il soit **anathème**". The lesson's paraphrase ("sous la malédiction de Dieu") is kept in b027, MC16 and Fill 10. SA8 accepts both anathème and malédiction.
4. **Galatians 5:23, "self-control"**: Segond says "la **tempérance**". MC15 uses "La tempérance", and its `why` explains it as "la maîtrise de soi".
5. **"Even that faith is itself a gift of God"** (b017, SA3): I translated this as written. Given the CTS Arminian-leaning stance, please confirm you want this wording. Ephesians 2:8's "cela" (that) is often read as the whole of salvation, not faith alone.
6. **"Saving grace … always bears fruit"** (b027, MC19): I translated this as written. I neither strengthened nor softened its perseverance overtones.
7. **SA prompts reworded**: SA2, SA5, SA8, SA9 and SA10 were rephrased slightly (for example "the Saviour" instead of "Christ"). The meaning is the same. Without this, the French prompt alone would match its own keywords.

## Problems in the English source
1. **MC11 why**: it quotes "Faith without works is dead", which is the wording of James 2:26, but cites James 2:17. The French cites "Jacques 2:17, 26".
2. **SA2 model**: it brings in "branches", which the lesson never mentions (the lesson has root, trunk and fruit). I translated it faithfully.
3. **MC9**: the correct option says "before men". The lesson defines James's sense as "shown to be righteous by the evidence of a changed life" and only uses "before men" for the fruit seen above ground. This is acceptable, but the wording is loose.
