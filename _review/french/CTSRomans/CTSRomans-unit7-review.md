Checker re-verified every quotation against the offline LSG (Romains 8:1-39, Psaume 23:4, Psaume 44:22 via Romains 8:36, 2 Samuel 18:33, and Apocalypse 12:10 for MC 17's "l'accusateur de nos frères"): all exact, no corrections needed.

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Romans 8:1, second half.** LSG ends the verse at "en Jésus-Christ". It does not have "who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit", a textual variant that the NKJV includes. The lesson does not rely on that clause, so b015 quotes LSG as it stands.
2. **"Spirit" lowercase in LSG (8:2, 8:4-6, 8:9).** LSG writes "la loi de l'esprit de vie" and "l'affection de l'esprit". The quotations keep LSG's lowercase. The prose and MC 2 say "l'Esprit de vie", with a capital, because the lesson clearly means the Holy Spirit.
3. **Romans 8:6, "carnally / spiritually minded".** LSG has "l'affection de la chair / l'affection de l'esprit". The prose explains this as "la pensée tournée vers…", and the MC 4 stem includes that gloss. Fill 4 has the answer "mort", so it is not affected.
4. **Romans 8:9, "you are not in the flesh but in the Spirit".** LSG has "vous ne vivez pas selon la chair, mais selon l'esprit". Following convention 1, b023 adds "Le texte grec dit littéralement : … « dans la chair » … « dans l'Esprit »".
5. **Romans 8:3, "likeness of sinful flesh".** LSG has "dans une chair semblable à celle du péché", and b018 adds the literal Greek sense. Fill 3 has the answer "semblable" and also accepts "ressemblance".
6. **"Groan / groanings" (8:22-23, 26).** LSG has "soupirer" and "soupirs inexprimables". I used "soupir(s)" throughout, adding "(ou gémissements)" once and in MC 8. Fill 7 has the answer "inexprimables" and also accepts "indicibles" and "ineffables". The SA keywords accept both "soupir" and "gémi".
7. **Romans 8:28, "work together".** LSG has "concourent au bien". b037 adds that the Greek verb literally means "travaillent ensemble", and MC 10 uses "CONCOURENT". The SA 7 keywords accept "concour" and "ensemble".
8. **Romans 8:29, "conformed".** LSG has "semblables à l'image de son Fils", and b043 adds "le mot grec signifie « rendus conformes »". MC 14 uses LSG's wording.
9. **Romans 8:20, "futility".** LSG has "soumise à la vanité", and b030 adds a short note on the sense ("futile, sans résultat").
10. **Romans 8:38, "principalities".** LSG has "dominations", and LSG places "puissances" after "choses à venir". The prose, MC 20 and SA 10 follow LSG.
11. **Psalm 44 in b048.** I quoted the form Paul uses in Romans 8:36 (LSG: "on nous met à mort tout le jour"), not Psaume 44:22 LSG ("on nous égorge tous les jours").
12. **2 Samuel 18:33.** The KJV-style "would God I had died for thee" became LSG "Que ne suis-je mort à ta place!"
13. **Perseverance wording (b016, b043, b051, MC 20, SA 9).** "Not now, not ever", "the believer's final glorification is so certain…", and "stands secure in a love that nothing… can break" are rendered at exactly the English strength ("ni maintenant, ni jamais", "si certaine", "en sécurité"). I added nothing and softened nothing. "Foreknew" is rendered as LSG's "connus d'avance", with no comment on the basis of election, as in the English. Please confirm that this level of assurance language is what you want, since perseverance is an open question at CTS.
14. **Fill 6 (Aramaic).** French needs an adjective here, so the answer is "araméenne" ("dans la langue ____"). It also accepts "araméen" and "aramaïque".
15. **Masthead b001.** I wrote "L'Épître aux Romains", the usual French title, rather than a literal "Le livre des Romains".

## Problems in the English source

1. **b038, factual conflation.** "He wept over the death of his infant son. He cried out, *Absalom, my son…*" The infant son is the child of 2 Samuel 12. Absalom was a grown son who died in a revolt (2 Samuel 18). The sentence reads as though they were the same event. I translated it faithfully, and the Absalom quotation is labeled 2 Samuel 18:33. The image of "the mountaintop killing Goliath" is also figurative, since the fight took place in the valley of Elah.
2. **b045.** "Paul has been building an argument for sixteen verses", but the list that follows covers 8:1-30, which is thirty verses. I kept "seize".
3. **SA 10 keywords.** "for" is a 3-letter stem that matches almost any English answer. In French the group is now the greater-to-lesser argument ("plus petit / plus grand / moindre") plus "contre nous".
4. **SA 8 keywords.** "ministr" does not appear in the lesson's Irving or Christenson stories, or in the model answer. I replaced it with a "scout / garçons" group, which the lesson does teach.
5. **SA 2 and SA 4 prompts.** In English these prompts pass their own keywords: SA 2 contains gravity, aerodynamics, Spirit and sin, and SA 4 contains adoption, Abba and Father. I reworded the French prompts so they no longer pass on their own.
6. **SA 5 model.** It adds "we hope for what we do not yet see; we wait patiently" (8:24-25), which the lesson body does not teach. Only the heading mentions hope. The French model refers instead to "l'espérance de la gloire à venir".
7. **MC 12 `why`.** It lists Irving's outcomes rather than explaining the keyed answer ("God does not deliberately cause calamities"). This is a minor problem, and I translated it as written. All answer keys are correct.
