# CTSParables Unit 1 — French draft review notes

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Luke 4:23 ("Physician, heal yourself").** The lesson (b009, MC3) says Jesus calls this saying a parable. Segond has "ce proverbe" (and so does the NKJV). Following convention 1, I quoted Segond and added: "Segond traduit ici « ce proverbe » ; le texte grec dit littéralement « cette parabole »." I added the same short note to the MC3 `why`.
2. **Amos's plumb line (b010, b035, b038, b041, SA10).** Segond 7:7–8 has "un niveau" (a level), not a plumb line, and has the Lord standing "sur un mur" (on a wall), not beside it. I kept "fil à plomb" as the lesson's image and unit title. At the first mention (b010) I added one sentence saying Segond has "un niveau" but the Hebrew means the mason's tool, traditionally understood as a plumb line. In b035 I wrote "debout sur un mur" to match Segond. SA10 accepts both "fil à plomb" and "niveau".
3. **Luke 15:2 "receives sinners."** Segond has "accueille des gens de mauvaise vie". I quoted Segond and added "Le texte grec dit littéralement « des pécheurs »" (b024). MC18 uses Segond's wording, and SA7 accepts "pécheur" or "mauvaise vie".
4. **Matthew 13:34 "to them."** Segond has "il ne lui parlait point" (lui = la foule). I added "c'est-à-dire à la foule" so that "lui" makes sense, which also matches MC7's option ("the multitude").
5. **"Judicial hardening" (b016).** I rendered it as "endurcissement judiciaire, c'est-à-dire un jugement" so that readers without training understand it as God's judgment and not a law court. I kept it even-handed between the Reformed and Arminian readings, as the English is.
6. **"Mustard seed."** Segond has "grain de sénevé". I used that and glossed it once as "(la graine de moutarde)".
7. **The curious and the committed.** I translated "the committed" as "ceux qui s'engagent" because there is no tidy French noun for it.

## Problems in the English source

1. **Luke 4:23 / MC3:** the NKJV the course quotes itself reads "this proverb", not "parable". The claim holds only for the Greek *parabolē*. Consider adding a phrase saying so in the English.
2. **b035 / Amos 7:7:** the English says the Lord stood "beside a wall". The NKJV says "on a wall".
3. **SA1 and SA6:** the English prompts pass their own keywords ("parabol", "mashal", "family"; "dodd", "jeremias", "blomberg"). The French prompts are reworded so they do not; the meaning is unchanged.
4. **Fill 6:** the English alternates "one" and "guy" are not really correct. They were not carried over.
5. **Fill 10:** the answer "stress" depends on an English technical term ("end stress"). In French it is "accent final", so the answer is "final" (as in Spanish).

## Adaptations

- "Farmer" became "cultivateur", and "builder dropping a plumb line" became "maçon qui laisse pendre son fil à plomb". Both are familiar trades in Kinshasa.
- "Be nice to people" became « soyez gentils avec les autres ».
- Nothing US-specific needed any other change.
