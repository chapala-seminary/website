## Questions for Wayne

1. **Luke 18:13, "God, be merciful to me a sinner!"** LSG has "Ô Dieu, sois apaisé envers moi, qui suis un pécheur." I quoted it in b003, b009 and b030. In b009 I added: "Le texte grec dit littéralement « sois rendu propice envers moi » : c'est un appel à la miséricorde." This keeps the link to the mercy seat. MC3 now speaks of the word translated "sois apaisé". The Fill 1 answer is "apaisé"; it also accepts "propice", "miséricordieux", "favorable" and "clément".
2. **Luke 18:9, "trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others."** LSG has "se persuadant qu'elles étaient justes, et ne faisant aucun cas des autres". It is used verbatim in b008 and MC1.
3. **Luke 18:11, "prayed thus with himself" / "stood by himself."** LSG has "priait ainsi en lui-même". For the alternative reading in b010 I wrote that the Greek can also be understood as "debout, à l'écart", rather than saying "some translations".
4. **2 Corinthians 10:12, "are not wise."** LSG has "ils manquent d'intelligence". I quoted it in b024 and added: "Le texte grec dit littéralement qu'ils « ne comprennent pas » : ils ne sont pas sages." The Fill 5 answer is "intelligence"; it also accepts "sagesse", "discernement", "jugement" and "bon sens". The correct option in MC12 is "Manquent d'intelligence".
5. **Matthew 20 wording.** "Whatever is right" became "ce qui sera raisonnable", "made them equal… borne the burden and the heat" became "tu les traites à l'égal de nous, qui avons supporté la fatigue du jour et la chaleur", and "is your eye evil" became "vois-tu de mauvais oeil" (all LSG). The SA2 keyword is "mauvais oeil" or "mauvais œil".
6. **"The thief on the cross."** LSG Luke 23 has "malfaiteur", and Matthew 27:38 has "brigands". I used "le malfaiteur sur la croix" and mentioned the traditional "bon larron" once in b018. The SA8 keywords accept malfaiteur, larron, brigand and voleur.
7. **Luke 23:42, "into Your kingdom"** is "dans ton règne" in LSG.
8. **"Mercy seat"** is "propitiatoire" (LSG, Hébreux 9:5). For "propitiated" in b011 I wrote "apaisé envers lui, rendu propice par ce sacrifice", which echoes LSG 18:13.

## Problems in the English source

1. **SA8:** the English prompt alone hits 3 of its own keywords ("eleventh hour", "thief", "cross"), so it passes by itself. The French prompt was reworded to avoid naming them and now hits 1. SA4, SA6 and SA10 each hit 1 in English; in French these were replaced with distinct ideas from the lesson, following convention 10. All 10 French models hit 8 of 8.
2. **b011:** the daily lamb (tamid) was a burnt offering, and calling it "an atonement for the people" is an interpretation (Bailey's). The cited Luke 1:8–10 supports only the incense hour and the praying crowd, not the sacrifice. I translated it as written.
3. **MC4 `why`:** it quotes the humble-exalted saying but not the "justified" sentence it is testing. This is minor, and I kept it.
4. **MC20:** the stem says "the two parables" are relatives of the elder brother. The lesson (b006) says the Pharisee and the early workers are. The meaning is clear, and I kept it.
5. **b015:** the English cites "(19:30; 20:16)" for "the last will be first, and the first last". That word order is 20:16; 19:30 has it reversed (and LSG adds "plusieurs"). The French quotes 20:16 and says "voir aussi 19:30".
6. **Fill 4:** the validator warning ("answer appears elsewhere in the prompt") is a false alarm, because the verse itself repeats "derniers".
