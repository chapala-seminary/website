# CTSParables Unit 10 (The Unforgiving Servant): French draft review notes

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Matthew 18:22, "seventy times seven."** Segond has "septante fois sept fois". In b009 I added one sentence explaining that "septante" is Segond's old word for "soixante-dix". MC2 and MC15 quote "septante". Fill 2's answer is "septante", and it also accepts "soixante-dix" and "70".
2. **Genesis 4:24, Lamech.** Segond has "Lémec", and its numbers are "sept fois" / "soixante-dix-sept fois". I quoted the verse in b009. The lesson's point still works. The SA6 keywords also accept "Lamech".
3. **Matthew 18:27 and 18:32–33.** Segond says "le maître" (not "the king") after 18:23, "remit la dette" (not "forgave"), and "avoir pitié" (not "compassion") in 18:33. The b003 epigraph and b013 follow Segond. In b013 the narrative says "le maître" where the English says "the king". SA1 accepts "pitié".
4. **Matthew 18:29.** Segond's wording is "Aie patience envers moi, et je te paierai" (without "tout"). So in French the fellow servant's words are "almost the same", as the lesson says, though the difference is slightly larger than in English.
5. **Matthew 6:12, "forgive us our debts."** Segond has "pardonne-nous nos offenses… à ceux qui nous ont offensés". That loses the debt image the lesson relies on. Following convention 1, b016 ends with "le texte grec dit littéralement : « remets-nous nos dettes… »" (moved by the checker out of the middle of the Moody story). MC7 quotes Segond.
6. **Romans 12:19, "I will repay."** Segond has "À moi la vengeance, à moi la rétribution". b021 adds one sentence: the Greek literally says « c'est moi qui rendrai ». Fill 8's answer is "rétribution", and it also accepts "je rendrai / rendrai / je paierai / rendre".
7. **Genesis 50:20, "You meant evil… God meant it for good."** Segond has "Vous aviez médité de me faire du mal: Dieu l'a changé en bien". That loses the repeated verb. b023 adds a sentence on the Hebrew. MC19 and Fill 9 quote Segond. Fill 9's answer is "bien".
8. **Hebrews 8:12.** I quoted only the second clause: "je ne me souviendrai plus de leurs péchés".
9. **Lord's Prayer and communion.** I used "le Notre Père" for the Lord's Prayer, "sainte cène" (convention 7), and "la table du Seigneur".
10. **b014 (perseverance).** I translated it neutrally: "se demandent si ce serviteur représente un croyant qui perd le pardon… ou une personne qui ne l'a jamais vraiment reçu". The French is no stronger either way than the English.

## Problems in the English source

1. **SA2, SA3, SA5, SA6, SA9 and SA10:** the English prompts contain several of their own keywords (e.g. SA9 "meant evil", "joseph"; SA10 "debt…"; SA2 "moody", "matthew 6"). Following convention 10, I rewrote the French keyword groups as lesson ideas the prompt does not contain. I also reworded the SA5 and SA6 prompts so they no longer name Lincoln, the boots or "seventy times seven". Every French prompt has 0 hits, and every model answer has 8 of 8.
2. **SA4 model** says those we trusted most are hard to forgive "because they broke our faith", and those we dislike are hard "because we find no love for them". The lesson (b018) asks the question but gives no reasons. I translated the model faithfully.
3. **SA9 model** says Joseph "released them from revenge" and that forgiveness "trusts God's purpose". This goes slightly beyond b023, but it is consistent with the lesson.
4. **SA6 English keyword "rebuke"**: Matthew 18:15 in the lesson says "go to him privately". "Rebuke" appears only in Luke 17:3 (b022). The French group accepts "repren-" and "témoin".
5. **MC10 `why`** (control over health) belongs with b018's point about giving the offender control. It does not explain the puncture image. This is minor.
6. **Fill 1 accepts "loose" and "release"**, but "letting loose" is not what the lesson says. The French blank is "laisser ____", with the answer "aller".
