## Questions for Wayne

1. **"Tares" / "darnel".** LSG has "l'ivraie" throughout Matthew 13, and "ivraie" is also the botanical name of darnel. So I wrote "l'ivraie enivrante" (the species name, *Lolium temulentum*) for "bearded darnel" (b008, MC1). This drops the English distinction between the biblical word and the botanical one.
2. **"Mustard seed".** LSG has "un grain de sénevé", an old word many Congolese readers will not know. The first time it appears (b005, b015), I explained it: "le « sénevé » est le vieux nom français de la moutarde". After that I used "sénevé" in quotations and headings, and "moutarde" for the plant itself. The SA3 keywords accept both.
3. **"The end of the age" (13:39, 40, 49).** LSG has "la fin du monde". Following convention 1, b009 and b026 add "littéralement « l'achèvement de l'âge »". MC18 and SA1/SA7 use LSG's "fin du monde". Please check that this does not tilt the eschatology.
4. **13:33, "hid in three measures".** LSG has "mis" (put), but the lesson and Fill 5 rely on "hid". In b019 I added "le texte grec dit littéralement que la femme « cacha » le levain". The Fill 5 prompt uses "mit".
5. **13:32, "greater than the herbs".** LSG has "plus grand que les légumes" and "viennent habiter dans ses branches" (not "nest"). Both are quoted verbatim. The lesson's own sentences say "font leur nid".
6. **Genesis 18:6.** The English says "when the Lord visited Abraham". I wrote "l'Éternel", since the passage (18:1) uses YHWH.
7. **Jim Elliot quotation.** It is not Scripture, so I translated it myself: "Il n'est pas fou, celui qui donne ce qu'il ne peut garder pour gagner ce qu'il ne peut perdre." Please check it against any published French form you know of.

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC11** says "the parables of Mark 4 and Matthew 13", and its keyed answer includes "is received differently" (the sower). This unit does not teach Mark 4. The point comes from Unit 7. I translated it as written.
2. **b017** says the seminary student in Unit 7 said, "I only began with a mustard seed of faith, but it grew." In Unit 7, the student's story has no such quotation; the words "mustard seed of faith" appear only in a Unit 7 `why`. I translated it as written.
3. **b023** mentions "the young woman in West Africa" without any introduction. She was introduced in Unit 7, so readers of this unit alone will not know who she is.
4. **b019, "roughly fifty pounds"** of flour. Three seahs are usually estimated at about 22 kg. I wrote "environ vingt-deux kilos" (≈ 50 lb). The Spanish edition says "veinte kilos".
5. **SA keywords.** Several English groups repeat words from their own prompts: "mustard" (SA3), "treasure"/"pearl" (SA4), "leaven" (SA6), "dragnet"/"householder" (SA7), "christian"/"woman at the well" (SA8), "elliot" (SA9). Under convention 10, I replaced these in French with distinct ideas from the lesson. Each French prompt now reaches 0–1 hits, and each French model hits all 8 groups.
6. **Fill 2** accepts "end", and **Fill 7** accepts "several", which is wrong for a count. I did not carry "several" over into French. The Fill 7 validator warning (only "7" as an alternate) is a false alarm, because it is a number.
