## Questions for Wayne

1. **Matthew 21:33, "went into a far country".** LSG has "quitta le pays" ("left the country"), with no "far". The lesson builds b013, MC6 and Fill 1 on "far country". In b013 I quoted LSG and added that the Greek means he went abroad, far from home. MC6 quotes « quitta le pays ». The Fill 1 answer is "pays"; it also accepts "contrée", "région", "terre" and "pays lointain".
2. **Matthew 21:34, 41, "its fruit" / "the fruits in their seasons".** LSG has "le produit (de sa vigne)" in both verses. Section VI depends on the word "fruit". In b015 I added "Le mot grec traduit ici par « produit » signifie littéralement « les fruits »", and in b029 "c'est-à-dire ses fruits". Matthew 21:43 keeps "les fruits" in LSG.
3. **"Chief cornerstone" (Ps 118:22 / Matt 21:42).** LSG has "la principale de l'angle". In b025 I added one sentence explaining that this means the cornerstone ("pierre angulaire"). Elsewhere I used "pierre angulaire". The Fill 7 answer is "angle"; it also accepts "pierre angulaire", "angulaire" and "coin".
4. **Matthew 21:42, "This was the LORD's doing".** Because the verse is cited as Matthew, I quoted Matthew's LSG wording ("C'est du Seigneur que cela est venu"), not Psalm 118:23 ("de l'Éternel"), even though b025 introduces it as "Psaume 118:22-23".
5. **2 Chronicles 36:15, "rising up early … because He had compassion".** LSG has "donna de bonne heure à ses envoyés la mission de les avertir, car il voulait épargner son peuple". After the quotation I added: "Le texte hébreu dit littéralement que Dieu avait pitié, qu'il avait compassion de son peuple." The SA4 model follows LSG ("voulait épargner").
6. **Mark 12:6, "one son, his beloved".** LSG has "Il avait encore un fils bien-aimé". The MC11 stem quotes « un fils bien-aimé ».
7. **Matthew 21:41, "destroy those wicked men miserably".** LSG has "Il fera périr misérablement ces misérables". In SA6, both "périr" and "détrui-" count as a hit.
8. **Hebrews 13:15.** The LSG verse puts "sacrifice de louange" before "le fruit de lèvres qui confessent son nom", so I quoted that fuller phrase to keep it grammatical.
9. **Terms.** I used "vignerons" (LSG) for the tenants in the parable and the title, and "métayers" for the Sharecroppers of the outline and the application ("nous sommes des métayers"). "Settlement" became "Règlement des comptes" (the outline word is "Règlement"). "Juridical parable" is "parabole juridique", as in Units 1, 2 and 5 (Unit 6 still has "parabole judiciaire"; worth aligning there). "Gentiles" became "non-Juifs" / "non juif" (also in MC5, replacing "Gentile nations").
