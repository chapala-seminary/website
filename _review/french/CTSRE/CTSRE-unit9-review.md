# CTSRE Unit 9 (Esther 8–10): French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **"Gallows" (b009, b018, MC 8, Fill 1, SA 1).** Segond has "bois" (Esther 7:10, 9:13–14). I quoted "pendu au bois" and added one line to b009 saying that the Hebrew word literally means "tree" or "wood", i.e. a gallows. SA 1 accepts both "bois" and "potence".
2. **"Estate" (b009, MC 1, Fill 2).** Segond 8:1–2 says "la maison d'Haman". Fill 2 now quotes Segond 8:2 ("établit Mardochée sur la ____ d'Haman"). The answer is "maison", and it also accepts "biens", "domaine", "propriété" and "maisonnée". In the lesson and MC 1 I wrote "la maison (les biens) d'Haman".
3. **"Dread" (b014, b016, MC 6, MC 17, Fill 3).** Segond uses two words: "la crainte des Juifs" (8:17) and "l'effroi que leur inspirait Mardochée" (9:3). I kept Segond's word in each place. Fill 3 answers "crainte" and also accepts "effroi", "peur", "terreur" and "frayeur".
4. **Esther 8:11, "including children and women" (b012, MC 4).** Segond reads "avec leurs petits enfants et leurs femmes". In French this could be read as the Jews' own families. I quoted Segond in full and added one sentence: "Il s'agit des enfants et des femmes de ces forces ennemies elles-mêmes." That is the lesson's own reading, now stated outright. Please confirm.
5. **Xerxes / Vashti.** Segond calls the king "Assuérus" and the queen "Vasthi". I kept "Xerxès" because the lesson's history depends on it, and glossed it once in b023: "(le roi Assuérus de la Bible)". MC 14 uses "Vasthi".
6. **"Blaspheme His name" (b031).** I used "déshonorons son nom" because French "blasphémer" sounds like spoken cursing. Use "blasphémons" if you prefer the stronger word.
7. **b029 / MC 19 / SA 9 ("salvation depends not on us… regardless of how badly we fail").** I translated this exactly ("quelle que soit la gravité de nos échecs") and added nothing about eternal security either way. Please confirm the wording fits CTS's open position.
8. **Feast name.** I wrote "la fête des Purim", following Segond, which uses the plural ("les Purim", 9:28–32).

## Problems in the English source

1. **b009, MC 1 (and SA 1 model).** These say Mordecai is "given" or "gains" Haman's estate. In 8:1 the king gives Haman's house to Esther, and in 8:2 she sets Mordecai over it. The MC 1 key is acceptable but imprecise.
2. **Fill 2.** It quotes Esther 8:2 from the **NIV** ("estate"), but the site's text is the NKJV, which reads "house". The French follows Segond ("maison").
3. **b022, MC 12–13.** These claim the pur symbol is *why* ("hence") the disciples cast lots for Judas's replacement (Acts 1:26). The text gives no such link. Casting lots was Israel's practice long before Purim (Lev 16; Josh 18; Prov 16:33).
4. **b023, MC 14–16, SA 5.** Xerxes as an "orthodox Zoroastrian", "monotheistic", who "destroyed Bel Marduk" is presented as fact, though the MC 14/15 `why` lines admit it is debated. "Precursor to later religious intolerance" is speculative. The MC 16 key ("religiously intolerant") gives Esther's risk a religious cause. The book itself names the risk as the law against coming before the king without being called (4:11), and that law was not religious.
5. **b019/b025 vs SA 7 model and MC 9 `why`.** The lesson flatly calls the 75,000 "evidence of widespread anti-Semitism". The SA 7 model and the MC 9 `why` hedge, saying the modern term "requires some care" and calling it "political enmity". The model therefore partly disagrees with the lesson. SA 7's keyword "entrench" never appears in the lesson. Translated as is.
6. **b018.** "Either another plot was uncovered or the work was unfinished" is speculation. Esther 9:13 gives no reason. This is minor.
