## Questions for Wayne

1. **Ahasuerus / Xerxes (b011).** The lesson's NKJV aside ("This was the Ahasuerus who reigned over 127 provinces") was replaced by Segond's Esther 1:1 ("de cet Assuérus qui régnait depuis l'Inde jusqu'en Éthiopie sur cent vingt-sept provinces"). I also noted that Segond, like the Hebrew, reads "Assuérus", and that some translations put "Xerxès". In the narrative I kept "Xerxès" wherever the English has Xerxes, and used "Assuérus" in Scripture quotations and exam items.
2. **"Won his favor" (b022, Esther 2:9).** Segond reads "trouva grâce devant lui", not "won his favor". The lesson objects to the wording "won his favor". I quoted Segond, dropped the sentence criticizing that particular translation, and kept Glenda's point with "le texte hébreu dit littéralement qu'elle « obtint » ou « reçut » sa faveur".
3. **"Kindness" = hesed (b023, Esther 2:17; fill 9).** Segond has "elle obtint grâce et faveur devant lui", so the hesed word comes out as "faveur", not "bonté". I quoted the verse and said that the Hebrew word rendered "faveur" is hesed. Fill 9 now reads "La faveur qu'Esther obtient…". The answer "hesed" is unchanged.
4. **Proverbs 27:17 (b029; fill 10).** Segond 1910 reads "Comme le fer aiguise le fer, Ainsi un homme excite la colère d'un homme", which works against the lesson's "iron sharpens iron". I quoted it exactly and added "Le texte hébreu dit littéralement qu'un homme « aiguise le visage » de son prochain". Please confirm, or I can drop the quotation and keep only the proverb.
5. **"Accountability" (b029, fill 10).** French has no good single noun for this (the "redevabilité" calque is not natural). I used the verb phrase "rendre des comptes". Fill 10's answer is "rendre des comptes", and the alternates are "rendre compte", "rendre ses comptes" and "rendre nos comptes".
6. **Hegai (b021, fill 8).** Segond spells the name "Hégué" in 2:3 and "Hégaï" in 2:8, 2:15. I used "Hégaï" throughout, noted the other spelling once, and fill 8 also accepts "Hégué".
7. **Proverbs 3:5 (b033).** God's imagined speech borrows Proverbs 3:5. I used Segond's wording ("ne t'appuie pas sur ta sagesse") and marked it "(voir Proverbes 3:5)".

## Problems in the English source

1. **Exam items the lesson never teaches.** The lesson ends its chapter 2 coverage at Esther's favor with the king, but these items assume 2:7, 2:10, 2:19–23 were taught:
   - The eunuchs' plot, its report through Esther, the royal chronicles, and Mordecai going unrewarded: MC 12–16, SA 5, SA 6, and part of SA 9.
   - Mordecai at the gate as an official or judicial post: MC 9, SA 4.
   - Esther as an orphaned cousin raised by Mordecai: MC 5, SA 2. The lesson only calls her "cousin".
   - Hiding her identity on Mordecai's order as part of a plan: MC 6, MC 17, SA 3. The lesson only says she "was not allowed to reveal her origins" and treats it negatively.
   - The Daniel parallel: MC 20, SA 7. The lesson mentions Joseph only as a contrast, not a parallel.
2. **Model answers vs. lesson.** SA 3 and SA 10 present the concealment as God-positioning and a model to imitate. The lesson (b021, b026–b027) treats Mordecai and Esther's concealment and assimilation as disobedience that God overrules. Translated faithfully.
3. **Herodotus (b012, fill 1).** The source says he wrote "around 472 BC". Herodotus was born c. 484 BC and wrote the *Histories* c. 440–425 BC. Kept as written.
4. **"Ahasuerus" (b011).** The source calls it a title meaning "high father / venerable king". It is generally understood as the Hebrew form of the same Persian name as Xerxes (Xšayāršā), not a separate title. Kept as written.
5. **Kish (b019, fill 4).** The Kish of Esther 2:5 is Mordecai's great-grandfather (or ancestor), probably not Saul's father. The link to Saul is a clan or typological echo, not direct descent. Kept as written.
6. **Who was deported with Jehoiachin (b020).** The source says "Mordecai and Esther were taken into captivity with Jehoiachin". Esther 2:6 most naturally refers to Mordecai (or Kish) only, and Esther was likely born in exile. Kept as written.
