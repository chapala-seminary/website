## Questions for Wayne

1. **Ruth 2:1, "a man of great standing" (b014, MC1).** Segond has "un homme puissant et riche", which leans toward wealth, while the lesson says the phrase means more than money. I quoted Segond, then added: « le texte hébreu dit littéralement "un vaillant homme de valeur" ». MC1 quotes Segond and gives that literal sense.
2. **Ruth 3:11, "woman of excellence" (b078).** Segond has "une femme vertueuse", and "toute la porte de mon peuple" instead of "all the people of my town". The lesson's point is that Ruth and Boaz get the same description. I quoted Segond, explained "the whole town", and said that in Hebrew it is the same description as Boaz's in 2:1.
3. **Ruth 2:20, "has not forsaken his kindness… one of our close relatives" (b033, b034, MC9).** Segond has "qui se montre miséricordieux…" and "il est de ceux qui ont sur nous droit de rachat". I quoted Segond and noted that the Hebrew word behind « miséricordieux » is hesed. I used **"parent-rédempteur"** as the term throughout, to match unit 8, and kept Segond's "droit de rachat" wherever I quote a verse. Please confirm this one term for the whole course. Units 4, 7 and 8 currently mix "parent rédempteur", "parent-rédempteur" and "parent racheteur".
4. **Ruth 3:10, "kindness" (b076, MC15).** Segond has "Ce dernier trait témoigne encore plus en ta faveur que le premier", with no word "kindness". I quoted it and added that the Hebrew word rendered « trait » is hesed (bonté, amour de l'alliance).
5. **Ruth 3:9 / Ezekiel 16:8, "spread the corner of your garment" (b075, MC14, SA4).** Segond's Ruth 3:9 already says "étends ton aile sur ta servante", which suits the lesson. Segond's Ezekiel 16:8 has "le pan de ma robe". I quoted both verses and wrote that the same Hebrew word means « pan » and « aile ». See also source problem 6.
6. **Ruth 3:7, "in good spirits" (b072, MC13, SA5).** Segond has "son coeur était joyeux". I used this phrase in place of "good spirits". The argument still works.
7. **Ruth 3:3, "put on your best garment" (b067).** Segond has "remets tes habits". I quoted it and added that the Hebrew word means an outer cloak or mantle.
8. **Ruth 2:22, "lest others fall upon you" (b035).** Segond has "qu'on ne te rencontre pas dans un autre champ", which loses the sense of danger. I added « le texte hébreu dit littéralement "qu'on ne se jette pas sur toi" ».
9. **Isaiah 61:3, "beauty for ashes" (b011, MC19).** Segond has "un diadème au lieu de la cendre". I used Segond's wording as the heading and added the literal sense (« une parure, une beauté »).
10. **Matthew 11:30, "my yoke is easy" (b043, fill 7).** Segond has "mon joug est doux". The blank is on « joug », so it still works. The validator warns that fill 7 has only one alternate ("jougs"). That is a false alarm, because there is no real synonym.
11. **Ruth 3:9, "maid" (b074, fill 9).** Segond already has "ta servante". So that the prompt does not give the answer away, fill 9 says « le mot qu'elle emploie pour se désigner signifie littéralement ____ ». The answer is "servante", and it also accepts esclave and domestique.
12. **b095, "They will know we are Christians by our love".** This is a hymn line, not Scripture, so I introduced it with « Comme le dit un cantique ». The verse closest to it is Jean 13:35, if you would rather quote that instead.
13. **Quotes I added.** Where the English only refers to a verse, I inserted short exact Segond phrases: Ruth 1:9 and 3:1 (b064), 2:4 (b019), 2:8 (b025), 2:12 (b028), 3:18 (b082), Josué 1:3 (b073), and Ruth 3:9 (b075). ## Problems in the English source

1. **Ephah (b032, MC8, fill 5).** "An ephah is 12 bushels" is wrong. An ephah is about 3/5 of a bushel (about 22 litres). "Roughly 30 pounds" is at the high end of the usual estimates. I kept "12 boisseaux" and "environ 13 kg (30 livres)" as written.
2. **Harvest period (b036, fill 6).** The barley-to-wheat harvest lasted about seven weeks (roughly April to June), not "4 months from March to June". I translated it as written.
3. **Boaz = "strength and swiftness" (b015, MC2, fill 2).** The usual meaning given is "in him is strength". "Swiftness" is doubtful. I translated it as taught.
4. **Isaiah 61 as "the 5th Servant Song" (b011, MC19).** Most scholars count four Servant Songs; 61:1-3 is sometimes added as a fifth. I kept the claim. The question about the Ethiopian eunuch (Isaiah 53, Acts 8:32) is left unanswered in the lesson.
5. **b007 heading** says "Ruth Chapters 1 and 2", but the class covers Ruth 2–3. I translated it as written.
6. **b075** labels as "Ezekiel 16:8" a quotation that is actually Ruth 3:9 (NIV, with a stray footnote marker "[b]"). I quoted the real Ezekiel 16:8 and Ruth 3:9 from Segond.
7. **Numbering.** There are two items numbered "5" (b043, b044), and the Chapter 2 Applications list skips 6 (b058 → b059). I kept the numbering.
8. **Mixed English versions.** Matthew 11:28-30 (b043) is ESV, Philippians 4:6 (b057) is NIV-like, and b101 has a typo ("heats"). All of these are now Segond.
9. **b047** asks what 1 Timothy 5:18 says "about Ruth Chapter 2:12". The link (2:12 is Boaz's prayer for her reward) is unclear. I translated it literally.
10. **Typos:** "brrothers" (b009), "galavanting" (b017), "redeemer her" (b079). I translated the intended meaning.
