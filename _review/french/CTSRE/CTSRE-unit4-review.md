# CTSRE Unit 4 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Ruth 4:1, "my friend" (b010, Fill 1).** Segond has "toi un tel", not "mon ami". I quoted Segond and kept Glenda's point: the Hebrew can mean "friend" but also simply "a certain someone". "John Doe" became **monsieur Untel**. Fill 1 no longer prints "toi un tel", because that would give away the answer. It also accepts "Untel", "M. Untel", "un tel" and "quidam".
2. **Ruth 4:7-8, the sandal (b017, MC6, Fill 4, SA3).** Segond has "soulier". Following convention 1, the lesson says "Ôter son soulier, c'est-à-dire sa sandale". Fill 4 answer is **soulier**, and it also accepts "sandale(s)" and "chaussure".
3. **Ruth 4:11, "wealth" / chayil (b021, MC8, Fill 6, SA4).** Segond has "Manifeste ta force dans Ephrata". I quoted it and said that Segond's "force" translates chayil. I also noted that Segond renders the same word "puissant" (2:1) and "vertueuse" (3:11), which supports Glenda's "same descriptors" point. Fill 6 now says "the word Segond translates « force »".
4. **Matthew 10:16, "gentle as doves" (b032, MC18, SA8).** Segond has "simples comme les colombes". I quoted it and added that the Greek word means pure and without malice.
5. **1 Corinthians 13:12, "we see in a mirror dimly" (b037, SA10).** I quoted Segond: "au moyen d'un miroir, d'une manière obscure".
6. **Names (Segond spellings).** I used Naomi, Machlon, Kiljon, Pérets, Hetsron, Nachschon, Isaï and Léa. For "Salmon" I followed Ruth 4:20; 1 Chronicles 2:11 in Segond has "Salma". Fills accept Perez/Pharès (Fill 7), Jessé (Fill 9) and Leah (Fill 5).
7. **"Gentiles" became "païens" (b037, MC19, SA10).** SA10 also accepts "non juif" and "étranger". Would you prefer "non-Juifs" or "les nations"?
8. **The Narnia quote (b037, MC20).** The French is my own translation, not the published French edition. The title is the usual French one, *Le Lion, la Sorcière blanche et l'Armoire magique*. "Safe" became "sans danger".

## Problems in the English source

1. **b021 / MC8 / Fill 6.** The NKJV does not have "wealth" in Ruth 4:11. It says "prosper in Ephrathah". "Wealth" comes from another version (e.g. NASB), and the "again" points back to an earlier unit. I translated the point using Segond's "force".
2. **b020 / Fill 5.** "They brought up the heads of the 12 tribes": Rachel and Leah bore 8 of Jacob's 12 sons. The other four were born to Bilhah and Zilpah. I translated it as written.
3. **b023 / MC13.** The lesson says the women bless Boaz "that he become famous, restore her life, and sustain her old age". In Ruth 4:14-15 the women bless the LORD. The one who will be famous, restore her life and sustain her old age is the redeemer, which most commentators take to be the child Obed (v. 15, "cet enfant"). MC13's key ("bless Boaz and name the child") inherits this.
4. **b023 / MC10 / Fill 8.** "Obed" is usually glossed "servant" ("one who serves"). "Worshiper" is a possible sense but not the standard one. I kept "adorateur".
5. **b022.** 1 Chronicles 2:9-15 starts with Hezron. "Perez bore Hezron" is in 2:5. Also, "the house of Perez … His descendants" muddles ancestors and descendants. I translated the meaning plainly.
6. **Typos.** In b024, "puts I best" should be "puts it best". In b031, "Sodom/Gom" means Sodom and Gomorrah. Both are fixed in the French.
7. **Short-answer prompts that pass their own keywords in English.** SA4 (rachel, leah, perez, tamar, chayil) and SA9 (sacrifice, boaz, ruth). Following convention 10, I reworded the French prompts. No French prompt reaches 3 hits now.
8. **SA3 / MC6.** "Legal confirmation of the transfer" is not in b017. It is supported by Ruth 4:7 ("cela servait de témoignage"), and I used that in the French model.
