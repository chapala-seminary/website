# CTSRE Unit 5: French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **"The citadel of Susa" (b010, fill 1).** LSG has "Suse, la capitale". I kept "la citadelle de Suse" in the lesson and added that the Hebrew literally says "Suse, la citadelle". Fill 1's answer is *Suse*. It also accepts Susa, Shushan and Suze.
2. **Gallows (b015, fill 4).** LSG has "un bois" (Esther 5:14, 7:10). In the lesson and in fill 4 I used the ordinary word "potence", with a note in b015 that Segond says "un bois" (Esther 5:14) and that the Hebrew word means a wooden pole. The fill answer (Mardochée) does not depend on this word.
3. **"Times of the Gentiles" (b028–b029, fill 8, MC10–11, SA4).** In Luke 21:24 LSG has "les temps des nations". I used "le temps des nations" throughout and added that the Greek literally says "les nations", meaning the non-Jewish peoples, the "païens" or "Gentils". Fill 8's answer is *nations*. It also accepts païens, Gentils, non-Juifs and nation.
4. **2 Timothy 3:16-17 (b008).** The English quotation is not the NKJV ("God-breathed… servant of God"). I quoted LSG ("inspirée de Dieu… l'homme de Dieu"). The lesson does not dwell on the image, so I did not add "soufflée".
5. **Jeremiah 29:10-14 (b021).** The English quotation is NIV-style ("plans to prosper you and not to harm you"). LSG has "projets de paix et non de malheur". I quoted LSG 29:10-11 and 14 and gave the references.
6. **Cyrus's faith (b022).** I quoted Romans 4:3 in LSG ("Abraham crut à Dieu, et cela lui fut imputé à justice"). "Salvific act" is rendered "un acte qui relève du salut". This is Glenda's personal opinion and is marked as such ("À mon avis"). Please confirm the wording.
7. **"Prince of Persia" (b039).** LSG has "le chef du royaume de Perse" (Daniel 10:13). I used that wording and added the reference. See also Problem 9.
8. **Women in ministry (b034–b035).** This section argues one side (women in prophetic and teaching roles) and calls the Catholic Church "very patriarchal". I translated it faithfully. CTS normally presents the views neutrally, and many readers in Kinshasa are Catholic or come from a Catholic background. Do you want this paragraph softened for the French edition?
9. **Ted's saying "don't just do something — stand there" (b037, SA8).** A word-for-word French version makes no sense, so I wrote "ne vous dépêchez pas d'agir — restez là et attendez".
10. **"Historical non-fiction" (b018, MC6).** I used "récit historique véridique", since "non-fiction" is an anglicism.

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC13 and the SA5 model ("God will not tolerate anti-Semitism for long").** The lesson never states this purpose. Its three purposes in b031 are: God uses whomever He wishes, Purim, and providence. The SA5 keywords "anti" and "protect" depend on it.
2. **MC18 and the SA7 model: "Conable".** The lesson (b032) names **Tom Constable**. I used "Constable" in the French.
3. **MC11 and SA4 ("from Babylon's dominance until Christ's return").** The lesson says the time of the Gentiles "began with the fall of Babylon". That is the opposite starting point. "Christ's return" is also not in the lesson. I translated the items as written.
4. **MC20 and SA10 ("India/Pakistan to Cush/Africa").** The lesson never mentions Cush. It lists Libya, Egypt and Sudan. SA10 also relies on Esther and Mordecai being "positioned" in Susa, which is not taught.
5. **b029 / MC10 / fill 8 / SA4: "the only OT book set during the time of the Gentiles… the last history book".** By the lesson's own timeline (b025), Ezra, Nehemiah, Haggai, Zechariah and Daniel also fall in this period, and Nehemiah (about 445 BC) comes after Esther.
6. **b035.** It says "in 1 Timothy… a few chapters earlier [Paul] speaks of women prophesying (they simply need an interpreter)". Women prophesying is in 1 Corinthians 11, not 1 Timothy. The "interpreter" rule is about tongues (1 Corinthians 14:27-28), not prophecy.
7. **b010.** "Darius being Xerxes' relative". Darius I was Xerxes' father.
8. **b025 / b026.** Temple work actually stopped under Cambyses and Smerdis (Ezra 4:24). Esther 2:16 puts Esther's coming to the king in the seventh year of the reign (about 479 BC), not 478. The story "runs from 482" but begins with a banquet in 483. These are minor points.
9. **b039.** It calls "Prince of Persia" "one of the names of Satan". Daniel 10 describes a hostile spiritual prince, not a name of Satan.
10. **b035.** The shaved-headed prostitutes of Aphrodite on the Acrocorinth is a disputed historical claim. This is a minor point.

## Adaptations

- **b031.** "Members of Congress and Parliament" is rendered "députés et parlementaires".
- **b021.** For readers outside Mexico, "we are all foreigners here" is rendered "notre Église, ici au Mexique… des étrangers dans ce pays".
- **Prompts reworded.** A literal translation of SA2, SA3, SA6, SA7 and SA9 named their own keywords, so I rephrased them without changing what they ask. For example, SA6 asks for "the two authors Glenda cites" instead of naming Baxter and Jobes. No prompt now hits more than one keyword group.
- **SA10.** The keyword "127" is under 4 letters (validator warning), but it is a number with no false-positive risk, so I kept it.
- **Image alt text (b027, b040).** The validator requires `<img>` tags to be identical, so the alt text stays in English. Only the visible figcaptions are translated.
