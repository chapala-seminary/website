# CTSRE Unit 2 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Ruth 1:13 (b026).** The lesson quotes "It is much more bitter for me than for you because the Lord's hand has come out against me." That wording is not NKJV, and Segond has "car à cause de vous je suis dans une grande affliction de ce que la main de l'Éternel s'est étendue contre moi" (no "more bitter"). I quoted Segond, then added: « Le texte hébreu dit littéralement : "C'est bien plus amer pour moi que pour vous." »
2. **Ruth 1:17 (b028).** "May the LORD do so to me, and more also…" Segond has "Que l'Éternel me traite dans toute sa rigueur, si autre chose que la mort vient à me séparer de toi!" I quoted Segond and added the literal Hebrew sense ("Que l'Éternel me fasse ainsi, et qu'il y ajoute encore"). The Elohim → LORD point still works, because Segond has "ton Dieu" in 1:16 and "l'Éternel" in 1:17.
3. **Ruth 1:21 (b031; MC15, fill 8, SA6).** The lesson relies on "I went out full… brought me home again empty." Segond has "J'étais dans l'abondance à mon départ, et l'Éternel me ramène les mains vides." I quoted Segond and added the literal Hebrew sense "Je suis partie pleine… vide," so that the full/empty wordplay stays.
4. **Ruth 1:8 "deal kindly" (b021, MC7, fill 4).** Segond has "use de bonté," so the keyword is « la bonté (en hébreu hesed) ». The fill-in answer is "bonté", and it also accepts hesed, chesed and bienveillance.
5. **Names.** I used Segond's spellings: Naomi (not Noémi), Elimélec, Orpa, Mara, Bethléhem. SA7 also accepts "Elimelek", and fill 7 accepts "Marah".
6. **Divine name.** In b018 I wrote "Yahvé (l'Éternel)", following convention 2, because that is where the lesson discusses the name. Everywhere else I used "l'Éternel". I rendered "the Great I AM" as « le grand "JE SUIS" » to echo Exodus 3:14 in Segond.
7. **Quotes I added.** Where the English only paraphrases or cites a reference, I inserted the exact Segond text: Deuteronomy 23:3 (b016), Ruth 1:6 (b018), Ruth 1:8 (b021) and Ruth 1:22 "au commencement de la moisson des orges" (b032). For Psalm 51:17 (b042), the English paraphrases only the second half of the verse. I quoted the whole verse in Segond. Please confirm that adding these quotes is acceptable.
8. **Glenda's voice.** The first-person feminine forms are deliberate: « je me suis sentie », « ma vie de chrétienne ».

## Problems in the English source

1. **b016, MC5, fill 3, SA2 say "mentioned nine times".** The Hebrew verb *shuv* (return) is usually counted 12 times in Ruth 1. In Segond, return verbs (retourner/revenir/ramener) appear 9 times. I kept "neuf" as written.
2. **b015 says Naomi "sees" that Bethlehem is prosperous.** The text says she *heard* it (1:6). MC6 has this right. The French follows the English (« voit »); change to « apprend » if you correct the English.
3. **Name meanings (b012, b013, MC3–4, fill 1–2).** "Orpah = stiff-necked" and "Ruth = friendship" are popular etymologies, and scholars treat both as uncertain. I translated them as taught.
4. **MC8, fill 5, SA4 call the levir "a relative".** Deuteronomy 25:5-10 specifies the dead man's brother. The extension to a wider kinsman belongs to Ruth 3–4. I translated "parent" (relative) as written, and fill 5 also accepts "beau-frère".
5. **MC19** ("a true convert is welcomed") draws an inference that the lesson only implies (b016, b028). It is acceptable, but the lesson never states it.
6. **Typos in b025 and b037:** "Hittitites", "bare a son", "Look at how her tarries now" (should be "He tarries"), "They first chapter". I translated the intended meaning.
7. **b036** ends "it is ultimately what leads our God to the cross". The logical link is unclear. I translated it literally.
