# French Batch 4: what the reviews found

This batch covers four courses: Ruth and Esther, Genesis Intensive, Bible Characters and Bible Characters II (44 units).

I checked every claim about the English site against the live course files and dropped any that did not hold up. Matters of taste were also dropped. Nothing here repeats the settled conventions or the questions already raised in Batches 1–3.

## 3. Decisions for the whole French edition, and theology flags

These are new decisions.

1. **Kinsman-redeemer.** Segond never uses a title, only "celui qui a droit de rachat". The drafts mix "parent rédempteur", "parent-rédempteur" and "parent racheteur". Pick one form for all courses (Ruth and Esther 1, 3, 8; Bible Characters 8).
2. **Esther's "gallows".** Segond says "le bois". Ruth and Esther uses "potence" in the lesson text, while Bible Characters II 4 grades "bois" as the fill-in answer. Pick one, and accept both in fill-ins.
3. **Xerxes or Assuérus.** Segond has "Assuérus". The drafts keep "Xerxès" in the lesson's history and use "Assuérus" in quotations and exam items. Approve that split?
4. **Quoting published French books.** The Narnia line (Ruth and Esther 4) and Gandalf's words (Bible Characters II 4) are the translators' own versions. Should the French use the published French editions' wording?
5. **Adding Segond quotations where the English only alludes to a verse.** Translators did this in Ruth and Esther 2 and 3, Bible Characters 10 and Bible Characters II 9, so readers can find the verse. Allow it generally?
6. **Words that sound wrong in Kinshasa.** The drafts replace these:
   - "ministres" → "serviteurs de Dieu" (Genesis 1, 7)
   - "croisé" → "héraut intrépide" (Bible Characters II 7)
   - "vaudou" → "féticheur" (Bible Characters II 7)
   - "allégeance" → "fidélité" (Genesis 8)

   Approve this as a standing rule?

**Theology flags the reviewers raised.** All of these are faithful translations of the English. The question is whether the English should change.

- **Women in ministry (Ruth and Esther 5, b034–b035).** This section argues one side, which goes against your neutral stance for CTS. It also calls the Catholic Church "very patriarchal", and many Kinshasa readers are Catholic. b035 also misplaces 1 Cor 11 as "1 Timothy" (see section 2).
- **Eternal security**
  - Ruth and Esther 9, b029, MC 19, SA 9: salvation depends on God "regardless of how badly we fail".
  - Bible Characters II 6: the reviewer asks you to check that "position devant Dieu" (for "standing") says no more and no less than the English.
- **Two CTS courses disagree about Mordecai and Esther.** Ruth and Esther 6 (b021, b026) calls their concealment and assimilation disobedience. Bible Characters II 4 (b012) now says they had no real choice.
- **"God repented."** Segond says "se repentit" at Gen 6:6 (Genesis 4) and Jonah 3:10 (Bible Characters II 1). The drafts either leave it unquoted or gloss it. Do you want a short sentence on what this means?
- **Genesis 1 (b013, MC 9).** The reviewer asks whether the gloss on "archetypal" Adam and Eve keeps the paragraph even-handed.
- **Ruth and Esther 5, b022.** Glenda's view that Cyrus's act was "salvific" is marked as her opinion. Please confirm the wording.
- **Bible Characters 7, b022.** The drafts add a note that "mon esprit" (Zech 4:6, lowercase in Segond) means the Spirit of the LORD.

**Authorship and sourcing in Ruth and Esther (Glenda Rogers' Lakeside course).** No reviewer questioned that Glenda wrote the course. They did flag:
- **It reads like class notes.** It names class members ("Ted made the point…", "Marcy smiled…"), refers to "Ted's Acts class", and credits a map to "Glenda's class presentation" (Unit 5, b040). The drafts keep Glenda's first-person feminine voice on purpose (Unit 2).
- **Bible text pasted from other versions.** Ruth 1:1–2 (Unit 1) and the "Ezekiel 16:8" quote (Unit 3) are NIV text, footnote markers ("[a]", "[b]") included. Other quotations are ESV- or NIV-style. Glenda says she prefers the NKJV. The French now uses Segond throughout.
- **Authors cited by name:** Tom Constable (misspelled "Conable" in the exam), Karen Jobes, J. Sidlow Baxter, Howard Hendricks. The Hendricks quote compares failing to apply Scripture to "abortion". The reviewer asks whether to keep that wording for Kinshasa (Unit 1, Q 7).
- **Esther's author.** Unit 5, b010 says the author "could have been a Jew". Unit 7, b010 says "a non-Jew most likely wrote it". Most scholars and Jewish tradition hold the author was Jewish.
