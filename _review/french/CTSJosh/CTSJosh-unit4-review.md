# CTSJosh Unit 4 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Joshua 6:2, "I have given" vs. LSG "je livre".** This is the main problem in the unit. The lesson builds on the past tense (b012, b045, b060, b070, b075, b083, MC13, fill 7, SA7), but LSG uses the present tense: "Vois, je livre entre tes mains Jéricho". I quoted LSG verbatim wherever the verse is cited. Then I explained that the Hebrew is an accomplished (perfect) form, literally "j'ai livré", and used "J'ai livré" as the lesson's own refrain. The MC13 stem adds "(selon l'hébreu, comme l'explique la leçon)". The fill 7 answer is the LSG word *livre*, and *ai livré*, *donne* and *ai donné* are also accepted. The SA7 prompt and model quote LSG and note the Hebrew past. Where the lesson repeats "J'ai livré" as a refrain (b045, b060, b070, b075, b083), it is presented as the literal Hebrew sense, not as LSG wording. Is this acceptable?
2. **Joshua 5:1, "their heart melted; there was no spirit in them" vs. LSG "ils perdirent courage et furent consternés".** I quoted LSG and added "en hébreu, littéralement : leur cœur se fondit…", which keeps the lesson's "you melt" image. The MC3 correct option uses LSG wording.
3. **"Captain / Commander of the LORD's host" becomes LSG "le chef de l'armée de l'Éternel".** I used *chef* throughout, including in the headings and the masthead. Fill 4 also accepts *capitaine*, *commandant*, *prince* and *général*.
4. **Joshua 5:15, "sandal" vs. LSG "souliers".** I used "ôter ses souliers" everywhere, including in the lesson refrain ("the sandals have to come off"). SA5 accepts *soulier* or *sandale*.
5. **Joshua 5:14, "What does my Lord say".** LSG writes "mon seigneur" in lowercase. I kept it verbatim. The lesson treats the Captain as divine, so this may be worth noting for readers.
6. **Galatians 6:1, "restore… considering yourself" vs. LSG "redressez-le… Prends garde à toi-même".** I quoted LSG. In b080, "considering yourself" became "ce « prends garde à toi-même »". Fill 10 uses *douceur* (LSG).
7. **"Mustard seed" (b055).** LSG uses "grain de sénevé", and I followed it. Congolese readers may know *moutarde* better.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b041 / MC9.** "Wayne once asked Dr. McCarthy, who was Hudson Taylor's representative" cannot be literally true, since Hudson Taylor died in 1905. This is a classic anecdote (usually told by J. Wilbur Chapman) that has been put into Wayne's mouth. MC9 says "According to Wayne". I translated it as written.
2. **b027.** "John Chapman" is probably J. Wilbur Chapman. Also, "Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God" (1741) came seven years after the Awakening began (1734), so "helped spark" the revival (b026) overstates it.
3. **b016.** The Jordan at Jericho is described as "a mile wide and 150 feet deep" in flood. The depth is not plausible. I translated it as written (45 m).
4. **b052.** The 1 Corinthians 1:26–29 quotation is ESV/RSV wording, not NKJV. In French it is LSG, so there is no effect on the French.
5. **SA9 model.** It says victory over sin comes when "hidden sin is confessed and dragged into the open". The lesson stresses that the victory was already won at the Cross and is received by faith. The keyword "hidden" is not taught.
6. **SA10.** The keywords "humility", "judge", "compassion" and "hidden" are not lesson vocabulary. Section VI speaks of sandals off and gentleness. I added *douceur* to the compassion group.
7. **SA2 model.** It calls step 3 "their diet changed", but the lesson's point is "Be Ready to Let God Change You". The French model keeps both ideas.
