## Questions for Wayne

1. **Rev 1:3, "Blessed".** LSG has "Heureux". The lesson (b013), Fill 2 and SA2 depend on Revelation opening with "a blessing". After the LSG quotation in b013 I added one sentence: the Greek word translated "heureux" means the happiness of someone God blesses, a beatitude. Fill 2 answer: "bénédiction"; "béatitude" is accepted.
2. **Rev 1:3, "this prophecy".** LSG has "la prophétie", not "cette prophétie". In b015 I wrote that verse 3 "parle des « paroles de la prophétie »".
3. **Rev 1:5, "washed us from our sins in His own blood".** LSG has "qui nous a délivrés de nos péchés par son sang" ("freed"), and it uses the present tense "nous aime". This is a Greek textual variant, not a translation choice, so I did not add the "literally" sentence. The lesson itself later says "freed us by His blood". I used LSG wording in b027, MC19, Fill 9 (answer "sang", still works) and SA7.
4. **Rev 1:6, "kings and priests".** LSG has "un royaume, des sacrificateurs". This is also a textual variant. MC19's correct option is now "A fait de nous un royaume, des sacrificateurs pour Dieu". The SA7 keywords accept "sacrificateur", "prêtre" and "royaume".
5. **Rev 1:8.** LSG leaves out "the Beginning and the End", and it has "dit le Seigneur Dieu" (not "the Lord"). b029 and b030 quote LSG. The lesson's explanation, that God is "the beginning and end of all things", is kept as commentary. It also matches Rev 21:6 and 22:13 in LSG.
6. **"In the Spirit" (b029).** LSG has "ravi en esprit" (1:10, 4:2). I quoted that and added: "le texte grec dit littéralement « je fus en Esprit »". Please confirm the capital E.
7. **"Seven Spirits" (1:4).** LSG writes "sept esprits" with a lowercase s, and I kept that inside quotations and in MC18. The lesson says this pictures the fullness of the Holy Spirit, and the French says so too. Should the commentary capitalize "Esprits"?
8. **"Ruler over the kings of the earth"** is "le prince des rois de la terre" (LSG), used in b027 and MC18.
9. **Millennial terms.** The French uses the standard forms prémillénariste / postmillénariste / amillénariste (‑isme), "dispensationaliste" and "enlèvement" for "rapture". Fill 7 and Fill 8 accept common variants. (The checker confirmed the balance; see Checker's changes.)

## Problems in the English source

1. **b018 / Fill 6, the incense certificate.** The certificates (*libelli*) that let people trade are documented in the Decian persecution (A.D. 250), not in John's first-century setting. The lesson presents them as part of "the Roman world that John's first readers knew". I translated it faithfully.
2. **Idealist wording.** The lesson (b017) says the struggle is "between Christ and evil". MC12, MC12's `why` and the SA4 model say "good and evil". This is minor, and I translated each one as written.
3. **SA keywords.** Several English groups overlap ("jesus"/"christ"/"god"; "approach"/"interpret"; "present"/"each"/"christian"). These are weak, generic stems. Under convention 10, each French question has 8 distinct ideas from the lesson. Every French model hits all 8 groups, and no French prompt hits any of them.
4. **SA models.** The French models are shorter, at 4–6 sentences, and they are taken from the French lesson. The English SA6 model adds "a humble and teachable spirit" and SA9 adds "calendar of disasters", neither of which the lesson teaches. I kept them because they do not conflict with the lesson.

## Adaptations

- "A.D. 70" became "l'an 70 après Jésus-Christ"; "a ledger" became "un livre de comptes".
- In b011 I added one sentence noting that the French title of the book, "Apocalypse", comes from *apokalypsis*. French readers know the book only by that name, and the word commonly means "catastrophe".
- There are no US-specific examples in this unit.
