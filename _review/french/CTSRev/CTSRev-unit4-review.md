Segond's "à l'heure" can sound like "during the hour", which would tilt toward one view. b017 quotes Segond exactly, then notes that the Greek word also means "épreuve" and that the Greek says literally "je te garderai **de** l'heure de l'épreuve" (matching the English "from"). The two views and "the course leaves that question open" are given equal weight. The checker confirmed the balance matches the English and made the SA4 model say "de l'heure" too (see below). Please confirm you are happy with the note.
4. **Rev 3:10, "kept My command to persevere".** Segond has "la parole de la persévérance en moi". I quoted Segond and did not add a note, because nothing in the exam depends on it.
5. **Rev 3:8, "a little strength" (b015, MC9, Fill 5 prompt).** Segond has "peu de puissance". I used it throughout the lesson, for example "elle n'avait que « peu de puissance »".
6. **Rev 3:15-16, "hot" (b018, b019, MC14, Fill 7, SA5).** Segond has "bouillant". This fits the lesson's point that the Greek means "boiling", so the heading became "ni froid ni bouillant". SA5 also accepts "brûlant".

9. **Rev 3:20, "dine" (b023, b024).** Segond has "je souperai". I kept Segond's word.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b019, "hot, in the original, carries the sense of boiling, or 'zesty'".** The Greek *zestos* does mean "boiling". English "zest/zesty" has no link to it, so the wording suggests a false etymology. I gave the sense ("ce qui bout, vif et plein de saveur") without claiming a link to the word.
2. **Fill 8, "the rights of the people".** The usual explanation of the name Laodicea is "justice/judgment of the people" (*laos* + *dikē*). The lesson hedges ("understood to mean something like"), and the English already accepts "justice" and "judgment". I kept both.
3. **SA7.** The keywords "money" and "witness", and the model's "cannot serve both God and money", are not taught in the lesson. I kept these ideas in the French keywords, mixed with lesson terms ("acquis", "Évangile").
4. **SA6.** The keyword "deceiv" is not a lesson word. The French group also takes "ignor", "ne sait pas", "illusion", which are closer to the lesson's "does not know itself".
5. **SA9.** The model says the door has "no handle"; b023 says "no latch". The French lesson says "ni loquet ni poignée".
6. **Short-answer prompts give away their own keywords.** SA1 has sardis, name, alive and dead. SA3 has philadelphia, open and door. SA5 has lukewarm, cold and hot. SA9 has rebuke, door and knock. SA10 has sardis, philadelphia and laodicea. Following convention 10, I reworded the French prompts. None of them reaches 3 hits.
7. **b016 heading, "Kept Through the Hour of Trial".** "Through" fits only one of the two views that b017 says the course leaves open. The French follows the English ("gardée à l'heure de l'épreuve"). You may want a neutral heading in both languages, e.g. "Kept in the Hour of Trial" / "gardée lors de l'heure de l'épreuve".
8. **The English models are long.** I wrote shorter French models (4–6 sentences) that still hit 6–8 groups.
