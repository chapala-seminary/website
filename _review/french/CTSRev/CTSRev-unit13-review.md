## Questions for Wayne

1. **Rev 20:4, "they lived and reigned" (NKJV) vs. LSG "Ils revinrent à la vie, et ils régnèrent".** Segond's wording already contains the "came back to life" reading that premillennialists argue for. Amillennialists read the same Greek word ("ezēsan", "they lived") differently. To keep the views even, b017 quotes LSG and then adds one sentence: "le texte grec dit littéralement « ils vécurent, et ils régnèrent »". This gives the next unit the neutral Greek wording to work from. Please also watch this in Unit 14.
2. **Rom 11:29, "irrevocable" (NKJV) vs. LSG "Dieu ne se repent pas de ses dons et de son appel".** b015 quotes LSG and adds that the Greek word means literally "sans repentir", that is, irrevocable. Fill 6: the answer is "irrévocables"; it also accepts "sans repentir" (the KJV/LSG sense), "irrévocable", "sans retour" and "définitives".
3. **1 Cor 15:23, "each in his own order… Christ the firstfruits" vs. LSG "chacun en son rang. Christ comme prémices".** I quoted LSG in b015 and Fill 4 (answer "prémices"; also accepts "premiers fruits"). SA3 keywords use "en son rang"/"prémices" in place of "orders".
4. **Isa 11:6, "the wolf lies down with the lamb" vs. LSG "Le loup habitera avec l'agneau".** I quoted LSG in b015 and Fill 5. Isa 11:9 has "l'Éternel" for "the LORD".

6. **"Chiliasm."** I used "chiliasme". Fill 7 also accepts "millénarisme", which is the broader French word for the same early belief. Please confirm that it is acceptable.
7. **"Scofield Reference Bible."** I used "la Bible annotée de Scofield (Scofield Reference Bible)" at first mention and "la Bible de Scofield" after that. The French edition is usually called "Bible annotée Scofield", so the reviewer may want that exact title.

## Problems in the English source

1. **SA6 model** says dispensationalists take Daniel's seventieth week as a future seven-year period. The lesson never mentions Daniel's seventieth week. I translated it as written.
2. **SA8 model** adds two replies the lesson does not teach: that Scripture describes nations and survivors in the kingdom, and the comparison with the risen Christ's forty days among mortals. The lesson's reply (b021) gives only three points. I translated it as written.
3. **b019** lists six dispensations ("such as innocence, conscience, promise, law, grace, and a coming kingdom"). Scofield's usual scheme has seven, adding "human government". Because the lesson says "such as", this is not strictly an error, but readers may notice it.
4. **Balance check.** b011–b023 present the case for premillennialism and the objections to it at roughly equal weight, and they end with the view's replies. The French keeps the same order and the same weight. I did not soften or strengthen any argument.
