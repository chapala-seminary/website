# CTSPent Unit 1 — French draft review

## Questions for Wayne

1. **John 1:1-5: "the Word ... Him" vs. LSG "la Parole ... elle".** LSG uses *la Parole* with feminine pronouns (*elle*, *par elle*, *en elle*). The lesson says "the eternal Word has already done His creative work". I wrote "la Parole éternelle a déjà accompli son œuvre" and kept "la Parole (le Christ)" in MC1, MC18 and SA7. A Congolese reader may know *le Verbe* (from older Catholic and Darby usage), so SA7 also accepts "verbe". v.5: NKJV "did not comprehend it" becomes LSG "ne l'ont point reçue". The lesson does not depend on this.
2. **Genesis 1:2: "hovered" vs. LSG "se mouvait".** The lesson's mother-swan image depends on "hovering". LSG has "l'esprit de Dieu se mouvait au-dessus des eaux", with *esprit* in lowercase. In b015 I wrote "l'Esprit de Dieu *se mouvait* au-dessus des eaux, comme la femelle du cygne qui plane au-dessus de son nid…" so the LSG verb stays and the image still works. Lesson prose uses capital "Esprit". MC3 puts "se mouvait" in quotation marks. Is this acceptable?
3. **Genesis 1:26 "in Our image" vs. "as Our image".** LSG reads "Faisons l'homme *à* notre image". I rendered the contrast as *à* / *en tant que*. MC6 option C is "En tant que (comme)".
4. **Genesis 1:27: NKJV "male and female He created them", LSG "il créa l'homme et la femme".** I quoted LSG verbatim. Nothing in the lesson depends on the difference.
5. **Genesis 1:29-30 "every green plant".** LSG v.30 has "toute herbe verte", and v.29 speaks of "toute herbe portant de la semence" and trees. I used "toute herbe verte" in b026, MC10, fill 9 and SA6. These verses are only paraphrased, not quoted.
6. **The divine name in b011.** "Moses introduces Yahweh" is rendered "Moïse présente Yahvé, l'Éternel". I kept the name because the paragraph is about the name itself and added LSG's "l'Éternel" so readers connect the two. Do you prefer "Yahweh", "Yahvé", or only "l'Éternel"?
7. **Fill 10 "consummation".** The answer is *accomplissement*, and *consommation* is listed only as an alternate. In everyday French, *consommation* means eating or drinking, so it would confuse readers.
8. **SA prompts reworded.** If translated literally, prompts SA1, SA3 and SA5 would have scored 3 keyword hits on their own. I rephrased them without changing what they ask: SA1 asks which genre and why it is more than a figurative account, SA3 asks what God does in the first three days and then in the next three, and SA5 asks about the presence of the Spirit of God over the waters (checker reworded it).

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC11 / b010, population density.** 33 million people over about 57 million square miles of land comes to about 0.6 per square mile, not 1.4. I translated the 1.4 figure faithfully as "une personne pour deux kilomètres carrés".
2. **MC17 and SA9.** The stem asks for the "likely cause" and the key is "satanic rebellion". The lesson only says "many conservative scholars see" this and then "whatever caused the chaos". The `why` says "Most" (the lesson says "Many") and adds "Lucifer's fall" and "a scene of judgment", which the lesson never teaches. SA9 keywords "lucifer" and "judgment" are also not taught.
3. **MC19 (plurality / Trinity in "Let Us").** The lesson never discusses this.
4. **MC15 `why`.** "During Israel's wilderness wanderings" is not in the lesson. The key itself (1450 BC) is supported.
5. **MC4 `why`.** It says days 4-6 fill the domains. The lesson places vegetation, which fills the land, on day 3.
6. **SA10 keyword "provid".** The lesson says God "sustains", not "provides". I kept the group and added *soutien/soutient*.
7. **b008.** Writing predates Moses by far more than four centuries (cuneiform and hieroglyphs go back to about 3200 BC). Also, "writing did not exist" was not really Wellhausen's argument; it was an earlier critical claim. I translated it as written.
8. **SA models.** All the English models are a single short sentence. The French models are 2–3 sentences built from the lesson text, and each hits 4–7 keyword groups.

## Adaptations

- Miles are converted to kilometres. "1.4 per square mile" becomes "à peu près une personne pour deux kilomètres carrés", and "50 to 100 miles" becomes "80 à 160 kilomètres".
- The American catfish example in b010 is kept, with the location explained as "le sud de ce qui est aujourd'hui les États-Unis". Congolese readers know the river catfish, so no substitute was needed.
- b035 "📚 Catalog / Catálogo" becomes "📚 Catalogue" (French only).
- Neolithic is rendered "proches de l'âge de pierre" for readers without formal training.
