## Questions for Wayne

1. **1 Samuel 17:47, "the battle is the LORD's".** LSG has "la victoire appartient à l'Éternel". I quoted Segond in b013. Following convention 1, b014 opens with "Le texte hébreu dit littéralement : « la bataille est à l'Éternel »". Your own phrase elsewhere stays "la bataille appartient au Seigneur" (b015, b027). MC4, MC5 and SA2 use "victoire". Fill 3 answer is "victoire", and it also accepts "bataille" and "combat".
2. **Psalm 23:4, rod and staff.** LSG has "Ta houlette et ton bâton". I mapped them in Segond's order: rod = houlette, staff = bâton (b020, MC14, Fill 6 answer "bâton"). But a French "houlette" is the shepherd's crook. That means the hooked tool ends up being the one that "drives off the wolf", and the plain stick is the one that "draws the sheep back". Should the explanation be reversed, or kept as it is?
3. **Psalm 23:2, "makes me to lie down".** LSG has "Il me fait reposer". I added "le texte hébreu dit littéralement « il me fait coucher »" so that the point about a sheep lying down still works (b018).
4. **Psalm 23:6, "goodness and mercy shall follow me … forever".** LSG has "le bonheur et la grâce m'accompagneront … jusqu'à la fin de mes jours". I added "le texte hébreu dit littéralement « poursuivront »" to support the sheepdog image. MC15, MC16 and SA7 use Segond's words. Note that "forever" becomes "to the end of my days" in French.
5. **The dying boy's "fourth finger" (b021, Fill 8).** "L'Éternel est mon berger" has four words, and "mon" is the third. I dropped "fourth finger". The French says he held "le doigt du mot « mon »". Fill 8 answer is "mon".
6. **Psalm 23:1, "I shall not want".** LSG has "je ne manquerai de rien". Fill 4 answer is "manquerai".
7. **Psalm 51:4 and 51:10.** LSG has "J'ai péché contre toi seul" (there is no doubled "You, You only"). It also has "crée en moi un coeur pur, Renouvelle en moi un esprit bien disposé" ("bien disposé" means willing, not "steadfast"). I quoted Segond as it is, with no note.
8. **Acts 13:22.** LSG has "homme selon mon coeur, qui accomplira toutes mes volontés". Segond spells "coeur" without the ligature. I kept that inside quotations and wrote "cœur" elsewhere. Fill 1 accepts both spellings.
9. **"Cast down" sheep (b018, Fill 5).** This is not in Segond's Psalm 23. I rendered it "renversée", and Fill 5 also accepts "abattue" and "couchée sur le dos".
10. **Names (Segond).** Bath-Schéba, Urie, Nathan, Goliath, Isaï.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b014.** The lesson quotes Psalm 23:4 in KJV form ("Yea, though I walk"). The NKJV omits "Yea". This is a minor point, and the French uses Segond anyway.
2. **SA2 prompt.** The English prompt hits 3 of its own keywords (battle, lord, name). I reworded the French prompt so it does not pass on its own (convention 10). Several English groups are generic ("god", "lord", "good", "life").
3. **SA8 model.** The phrase "which no hired hand would ever do" (John 10:12) is not taught in the lesson. I translated it anyway ("mercenaire").
4. **SA4 model and MC8 why.** Both use "without a word". This is in 1 Peter 3:1, but the lesson only says "by the conduct". The French "sans parole" is still Segond's wording.
5. **b025 anecdotes.** Please check the source of the WWI identity-disc story and the "Lassie, come home — for the dear Lord's sake" line. I could not confirm that this line comes from *Lassie Come-Home*. I translated both as they are written.
