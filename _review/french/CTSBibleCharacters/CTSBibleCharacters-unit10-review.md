## Questions for Wayne

1. **1 Samuel 3:1, "no widespread revelation".** LSG: "les visions n'étaient pas fréquentes". Quoted as LSG in b013; meaning unchanged.
2. **1 Samuel 3:9-10, "Your servant hears".** LSG: "ton serviteur écoute" ("listens"). This actually fits the lesson's stress on listening. Eli's version (3:9) has "Parle, Éternel", which I used in b007, b009 and MC4.
3. **1 Samuel 7:3, "prepare your hearts for the LORD".** LSG: "dirigez votre cœur vers l'Éternel" ("turn your heart toward"), with "ôtez du milieu de vous les dieux étrangers". Quoted as LSG in b018 and the MC6 `why`, with an ellipsis where LSG adds "et les Astartés".
4. **1 Samuel 7:12, Ebenezer / "helped us".** LSG spells it "Eben-Ezer" and says "l'Éternel nous a secourus". I glossed the name as "pierre du secours". Fill 4 answer: "Eben-Ezer", also accepting "Ebenezer".
5. **1 Samuel 8:5, "Give us a king".** LSG 8:5 reads "établis sur nous un roi pour nous juger, comme il y en a chez toutes les nations". ("Donne-nous un roi" only appears in 8:6.) I quoted 8:5 in b023 and MC10.
6. **1 Samuel 16:7, "outward appearance".** LSG: "l'homme regarde à ce qui frappe les yeux, mais l'Éternel regarde au cœur" ("what strikes the eyes"). Following convention 1, b025 adds: "Le texte hébreu dit littéralement « à ce qui est devant les yeux », c'est-à-dire à l'apparence extérieure". Fill 8 uses the LSG wording. SA5 accepts "apparence", "extérieur" or "frappe les yeux".
7. **1 Samuel 12:3, "bribe".** LSG: "De qui ai-je reçu un présent, pour fermer les yeux sur lui ?" b028 adds a one-sentence gloss: the Hebrew word rendered "présent" means a bribe ("pot-de-vin"). The MC16 option reads "un pot-de-vin (un présent)".
8. **1 Samuel 15:22, "to obey is better than sacrifice".** LSG uses a noun: "l'obéissance vaut mieux que les sacrifices", and "sacrifices" is plural. That plural form is used throughout. Fill 9 answer: "sacrifices", also accepting the singular.
9. **"The LORD forbid that I should stretch out my hand against the LORD's anointed" (b025).** This English wording is 1 Samuel 26:11, but MC15 cites 24:6. I quoted LSG 24:6 in b025 and SA6 so that they match MC15. Please confirm 24:6 rather than 26:11 ("Loin de moi, par l'Éternel ! de porter la main sur l'oint de l'Éternel !").
10. **"Tabernacle in Shiloh" (b007, Fill 1).** LSG calls it "la maison de l'Éternel à Silo" (1:24) and "le temple de l'Éternel" (3:3). I kept the lesson's "tabernacle". Fill 1 answer: "Silo", also accepting "Shiloh".
11. **"Messiah/Christ = the Anointed One".** I rendered it "l'Oint", capitalized for Christ. For Saul and David I kept LSG's lowercase "l'oint de l'Éternel". "Kingmaker" became "celui qui faisait les rois". Fill 10 answer: "l'Oint" (graded as "oint").
12. **MC15 format.** The English options ("anointed / friend / father / king") would read awkwardly as "l'ami/le père de l'Éternel" in French. I rephrased the stem as a missing-word question: "« l'… de l'Éternel ». Quel mot manque ?". The options are now "oint / ami / père / roi". Their order and the answer index are unchanged.

## Problems in the English source

1. **SA9.** The English prompt alone hits 3 of its own keywords ("judges", "kings", "bridge"), so a student could pass just by copying the question. The French groups use other ideas from the lesson, and the French prompt hits 0.
2. **b025 vs MC15.** The quotation in b025 is NKJV 1 Samuel 26:11, but the MC15 `why` cites 24:6 (see Question 9).
3. **b007 / b015.** Both paragraphs carry nearly the same caveat ("a unique prophetic commission… the posture… toward the written Word"). I translated both.
4. **SA4 model.** It says God told Samuel to "give them what they asked" and that "God sometimes lets us have what we insist on". The lesson does not teach either point. I translated them, but neither one is used as a keyword.
5. **MC15.** Option "king" is arguably also true: Saul was the king God had given. In context the key ("anointed") is clear.
