## Questions for Wayne

1. **Genesis 50:20 (b003, b009, b032, b033, b035, MC18, Fill 9, Fill 10, SA8).** The NKJV has "You meant evil... God meant it for good", using the same verb twice. LSG has "Vous aviez médité de me faire du mal: Dieu l'a changé en bien". I quoted LSG and, following convention 1, added one sentence at the start of b033 saying that the Hebrew uses the same verb for the brothers and for God. The proposition ("What men intend for evil, God intends for good") is rendered "Ce que les hommes méditent pour le mal, Dieu le change en bien" so that it matches Segond.
2. **"Pit" (title, b007, b013, b014, b018, b035, SA1, SA2).** Segond has "citerne" in Genesis 37, so the lesson's "pit" motif is "citerne" throughout ("De la citerne au palais"). For Daniel I kept "fosse aux lions".
3. **Genesis 39:21 "showed him mercy" (b022, MC9, SA4).** LSG has "il étendit sur lui sa bonté". I quoted it as written. The SA4 keywords accept both "bonté" and "miséricorde".
4. **Genesis 41:16 "an answer of peace" (b026).** LSG has "une réponse favorable". I quoted it as written.
5. **Genesis 39:2 "a successful man" (b017, MC4).** LSG has "la prospérité l'accompagna". MC4 uses only the first half: "l'Éternel fut avec lui".
6. **"Dreamer" (b011, b013, b014).** I used Segond's "faiseur de songes" for both the heading and the quotation. Throughout the lesson, "dreams" is rendered "songes".
7. **Cupbearer and silver (Fill 2, Fill 6).** Segond's words are "chef des échansons" and "vingt sicles d'argent". Fill 2's answer is "argent". Fill 6's answer is "échansons", and it also accepts "échanson", "chef des échansons" and "grand échanson".
8. **"Vindicated by God" (b023).** I chose "justifié par Dieu". If you prefer a less forensic word, "réhabilité par Dieu" would work.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b029.** "He hid his own silver cup in Benjamin's sack": in Genesis 44:2, Joseph ordered his steward to do it. The French says "il fit cacher", which is accurate and barely changes the sentence.
2. **SA8 keywords.** The groups "mean" and "meant" overlap, because "meant" contains "mean", so the English item really has 7 distinct groups. In French all 8 groups are distinct, following convention 10.
3. **SA9 keywords.** The English stems "all" and "call" are short and generic. They are replaced in French by distinctive groups.
4. **Fill 10.** The definition of providence ("His wise and unseen rule over all events") is never given in the lesson. The word "providence" itself is taught in b007, b009, so the item can still be answered.
