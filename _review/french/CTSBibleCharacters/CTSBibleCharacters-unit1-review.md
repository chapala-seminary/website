## Questions for Wayne

1. **Amos 3:3, "unless they are agreed" (b019, b020, b023, MC5, MC6, Fill 3, SA3).** LSG reads "Deux hommes marchent-ils ensemble, Sans en être convenus?" ("without having arranged to meet"). Section II's heading, "A Walk of Agreement", depends on this verse. I kept the heading "Une marche d'accord", quoted LSG, and added in b020: "Le texte hébreu dit littéralement « sans s'être donné rendez-vous », c'est-à-dire sans s'être mis d'accord." MC5's answer option is "convenus". Fill 3's answer is "convenus", and it also accepts "convenu", "d'accord", "en accord" and "accord".
2. **Hebrews 11:5-6, "pleased God" (b026-b028, MC10-12, Fill 6, SA4, SA8).** LSG uses "agréable à Dieu" / "lui être agréable", not a verb. b028 dwells on "that small word 'pleased'", so I wrote "ce petit mot « agréable »" and added "Le texte grec dit littéralement qu'Hénoc « avait plu à Dieu »". Fill 6's answer is "agréable", and it also accepts "agréé" and "plaisant". Elsewhere the lesson uses "plaire à Dieu" where the English does not quote the verse.
3. **Jude 14, "the Lord comes with ten thousands of His saints" (b031, Fill 8).** LSG reads "le Seigneur est venu avec ses saintes myriades" (past tense, "holy myriads"). I quoted LSG. Fill 8's answer is "saintes", and it also accepts "saints", "sainte" and "saintes myriades". The past tense does not affect the lesson, but please note it.
4. **Names.** Genesis in LSG has "Hénoc" and "Metuschélah", while Hebrews 11:5 and Jude 14 in LSG have "Enoch". I used "Hénoc" in the lesson text, following the Genesis course, and left "Enoch" unchanged inside the Hebrews and Jude quotations. Fill 1's answer is "Metuschélah", and it also accepts "Mathusalem" and "Methuselah". For Elijah, LSG has "Elie", which I wrote "Élie". Fill 10 also accepts "Elijah".
5. **"Took him home" / "taken".** Genesis 5:24 (LSG) has "Dieu le prit", and Hebrews 11:5 has "enlevé" / "enlèvement". I used "prit/pris chez lui" for Genesis and "enlevé" for Hebrews. "Enlèvement" is also a term in eschatology (the Rapture), but here it is only Segond's word for what happened to Enoch.

## Problems in the English source

1. **b007 and MC1: "Adam in the garden" walked with God.** Scripture never says that Adam walked with God. Genesis 3:8 says that God walked in the garden. Only Enoch (Genesis 5:22, 24) and Noah (Genesis 6:9) are said to have walked with God. MC1's `why` cites Genesis 5:24 and 6:9 for all three men, but neither verse speaks of Adam. I translated it as written.
2. **b035 and MC16: "Five times in Genesis 5" the refrain "and he died".** The refrain actually appears eight times (verses 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 27 and 31). MC16's own `why` cites "Gen 5:5-31". I translated "cinq fois" faithfully. It should probably say "eight times" in all three languages.
3. **SA1 model** says "God took him", which does not match its own keyword "taken", and none of the keywords match "against the current". The French model hits 7 of the 8 groups.
4. **SA keywords.** Under convention 10, I replaced weak or repeated English stems (for example "walk" in SA1, SA2, SA4 and SA10, and "God" in SA8, which any answer hits) with distinct ideas from the lesson. No French prompt hits any of its own groups.
