## Questions for Wayne

1. **Ruth 1:16, "Entreat me not to leave you, or to turn back from following after you."** LSG has "Ne me presse pas de te laisser, de retourner loin de toi!" I quoted it verbatim in b019. Fill 4 uses LSG's word "laisser" as the answer and also accepts "quitter", "abandonner" and "délaisser". The masthead verse (b003) quotes the LSG phrases with "..." between them.
2. **Ruth 1:20, "the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me."** LSG has "le Tout-Puissant m'a remplie d'amertume" ("has filled me with bitterness"). The meaning is the same. I used it in b013, MC5 and SA1. Fill 1 ("Mara means ____") has the answer "amère" and accepts "amer", "amertume" and "remplie d'amertume".
3. **Names follow Segond:** Naomi (not Noémi), Elimélec, Machlon, Kiljon, Orpa, Boaz, Obed, Isaï, Bethléhem. Fill 3 also accepts "Orpah"/"Orfa". Please confirm that "Naomi" (LSG) is right for Kinshasa readers, who may know "Noémi" from other Bibles.
4. **Hebrews 11:6, "a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him."** LSG has "le rémunérateur de ceux qui le cherchent", so there is no "diligently". Following convention 1, b022 adds: "Le texte grec dit littéralement « ceux qui le recherchent avec ardeur »." MC14 uses LSG's wording.
5. **"Kinsman-redeemer."** LSG never uses this as a title. It says "a droit de rachat" (Ruth 2:20, 3:9, 4:14). I used "parent rédempteur" throughout ("Parent Rédempteur", capitalized, when it refers to Christ). In b023 I added one sentence: Segond speaks of the one who "a droit de rachat"; the Hebrew word go'el means the near relative who redeems. Fill 7 has the answer "parent rédempteur" and accepts "rédempteur", "goël", "go'el", "proche parent" and "celui qui a droit de rachat".
6. **Ruth 2:3, "she happened to come."** LSG has "il se trouva par hasard que la pièce de terre appartenait à Boaz". I quoted it in b023, and God's "happenings" became God's saints « hasards ». Please confirm that "les saints hasards de Dieu" reads well. It is a deliberate paradox, as in the English.
7. **1 Corinthians 10:11, "for our admonition."** LSG has "pour notre instruction". I quoted LSG in b027. Nothing in the exam depends on this word.
8. **Ruth 4:15, "better to you than seven sons."** LSG has "elle qui vaut mieux pour toi que sept fils". I quoted it in b024. MC19 quotes it as "vaut mieux pour [Naomi] que sept fils".
9. **"Gentile"** became "non juive"/"non-Juive", not "païenne", following the convention used in Acts. The SA9 and SA10 keyword groups also accept "païen".
10. **b015, "some measure of resolution"** became "une certaine paix retrouvée" ("a certain peace regained"). "Résolution" would sound clinical in French.

## Problems in the English source

1. **SA2, SA3 and SA8: the English prompt passes its own keywords.** SA2 hits mara, bitter, naomi and grief (4). SA3 hits change, orpah and ruth (3). SA8 hits kinsman, redeem and boaz (3). Under convention 10, I replaced those groups in French with distinct ideas from the lesson. Each French prompt now reaches at most 2 hits. I also replaced the weak groups "god", "ruth" and "naomi" in the other questions.
2. **MC11** says "What did Wayne call 'the seven last words of the church'". The lesson (b020) says "what someone once called". I translated the item faithfully.
3. **SA6 model** quotes the first half of Hebrews 11:6 ("without faith it is impossible to please God"). The lesson quotes only the second half. The French model keeps it, as the English does, and the keywords only partly depend on it.
4. **b007, the Franklin anecdote** is traditional and poorly documented. The French now follows the English as stated (the checker removed the drafter's added "On raconte que…" / "It is said that…"). If you want it hedged, that one phrase can go back in.

- b020: "five states" became "cinq États différents des États-Unis" ("five different states of the United States"). "We never did it that way before" became the natural French saying « On n'a jamais fait comme ça avant ».
