## Questions for Wayne

1. **1 Cor 15:14, "empty" (b004, b006, b008, MC 1, Fill 1, SA 1).** LSG says "vaine". I quoted LSG everywhere. In MC 1 the option is "Vaines (vides)". Fill 1 answer: "vaine". It also accepts "vain", "vide", "inutile" and "en vain".
2. **1 Cor 15:3, "received… delivered" (b024).** The lesson puts both words in quotation marks. LSG has "comme je l'avais aussi reçu" but renders "delivered" as "Je vous ai enseigné". Following convention 1, I quoted LSG and added: "le grec dit littéralement « je vous ai transmis »".
3. **1 Cor 15:5, "Peter" (b024).** LSG has "Céphas". I wrote "Céphas (c'est-à-dire Pierre)". I also lowercased "les douze", as LSG does.
4. **"I AM" (b026, SA 10).** I wrote « JE SUIS » in capitals, as the English does. LSG has lowercase "je suis" (for example Jean 8:58). Please confirm the capitals.
5. **Thomas's words (b012).** The lesson paraphrases rather than quotes him ("unless I touch the wounds"; LSG Jean 20:25 speaks of "la marque des clous"). I kept the paraphrase without quotation marks, ending with LSG's "je ne croirai point". Fill 5 ("Mon Seigneur et mon ____") matches LSG Jean 20:28 exactly.
6. **Morison's chapter title (b018, MC 14, Fill 9).** I translated it myself as « Le livre qui refusait d'être écrit ». The book is known in French as "Qui a roulé la pierre ?", but I could not check the French edition's chapter title. Fill 9 answer: "écrit". It also accepts "rédigé".
7. **"Reluctant witnesses" (heading b011, SA 3).** I used "témoins malgré eux". The SA 3 keyword group also accepts "réticent".

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC 4 (stem and `why`), SA 2 model and keywords.** These say Jews "began to worship on" Sunday, "switched to" it, and call it a "sociological earthquake". The lesson is more careful: it speaks of a "sociological shift", "special significance" given to the first day, a change over "a generation or two", and many believers still attending synagogue and temple. The words "earthquake" and "Sunday" never appear in the lesson. I translated faithfully and added "(le changement sociologique décrit dans la leçon)" to the MC 4 stem. The SA 2 keyword groups accept "premier jour" and "sociolog".
2. **MC 12, SA 6.** "Destroy Christianity" (MC 12 option and SA 6 keyword "destroy") and "split up to research" go beyond the lesson. The lesson only says the two men set out to examine the claims, each expecting to show them false. In French the SA 6 group accepts "réfut" and "faux/fausse" as well as "détrui".
3. **MC 13, "Both became believers".** The lesson says only that each concluded his event was genuine and published a defence. It never says either man became a believer. Separately, the West–Lyttelton story (that they set out as unbelievers to disprove Christianity) is widely questioned by historians: both appear to have been professing Christians already when they wrote their 1747 books. You may want to soften b018 and MC 12–13.
4. **b012, "fell down and said, My Lord and my God".** John 20 does not say that Thomas fell down. I translated it as "tomba à genoux", which is faithful to the English.
5. **b022, "pierced through the heart".** John 19:34 says the side was pierced. I translated faithfully.
6. All answer keys match the lesson.
