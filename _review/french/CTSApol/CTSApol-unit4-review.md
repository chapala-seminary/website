# CTSApol Unit 4 — French draft review notes

Checker verified every quotation against the offline LSG tool: Ésaïe 53:3, Ésaïe 53:4, Genèse 50:20, Romains 8:28, Matthieu 27:46 (allusion: Romains 8:22). All are verbatim LSG; no corrections needed.

## Questions for Wayne

1. **Isaiah 53:4, "griefs … sorrows" (b004, b006, b024, b028, Fill 1).** LSG says "ce sont nos souffrances qu'il a portées, C'est de nos douleurs qu'il s'est chargé". The lesson does not comment on the word, so I added no Hebrew note. In b024 and b028 I wrote "souffrances et douleurs" to match LSG. Fill 1 answer: "souffrances". It also accepts "maladies", the literal Hebrew sense and the Spanish RVG word.
2. **Genesis 50:20 (b026, MC 15, Fill 9, SA 9).** LSG says "Vous aviez médité de me faire du mal: Dieu l'a changé en bien". This loses the NKJV's repeated "meant … meant", which the heading relies on. Following convention 1, I added one sentence to b026: "le texte hébreu emploie le même verbe … littéralement « vous aviez pensé du mal contre moi ; Dieu l'a pensé en bien »". Heading: "Pensé pour le mal, changé en bien". Fill 9 answer: "bien".
3. **Romans 8:28.** LSG says "toutes choses concourent au bien de ceux qui aiment Dieu". It has no "work together", so the lesson's "woven" image now stands on "concourent". MC 16 uses the LSG wording.
4. **Matthew 27:46, "forsaken" (b024, MC 20, Fill 8).** LSG says "pourquoi m'as-tu abandonné?". Fill 8 answer: "abandonné". It also accepts "délaissé".
5. **Isaiah 53:3 (b029).** LSG says "Homme de douleur et habitué à la souffrance" (in the singular). I quoted only the first two lines, to match the English.
6. **Terms.** "Libertarian freedom" is "liberté libertarienne" (Fill 5). "Free will" is "libre arbitre". "All-good" is "parfaitement bon". "Privation" stays as in English. "The whole creation groans" (b019) uses the verb from LSG Romans 8:22, "soupire". Please confirm "libertarienne" is the term a French theological reader expects. "Liberté libertarienne" is also used, but "libre arbitre libertarien" is common in French philosophy.
7. **"He gave Job Himself" (b019, MC 11).** I wrote "Il s'est donné lui-même à Job".
8. **SA keyword substitutions.** In several places the English stem is a word in the French prompt, or a word the lesson never teaches. There I used a distinctive word from the French lesson instead: SA 1 "arrow" became "atout"; SA 3 "thing" became "substance"; SA 6 "tidy" became "honnête"; SA 8 "unique" became "aucune autre"; SA 10 "presence" became "humilité". All SA prompts hit at most 2 of their own groups, and all French models hit 8 of 8 (re-checked after the checker's keyword changes).

## Problems in the English source

1. **MC 2 (correct option) and SA 2 (keyword "lawgiver").** The word "Lawgiver" never appears in the lesson. b010 speaks of "a good and personal God whose character is the standard". In SA 2 the group also accepts "dieu bon" and "fondement".
2. **MC 3 `why`.** It says the straight standard "must come from God". The lesson is more careful: it says theism "offers a more natural grounding". I translated the `why` faithfully.
3. **MC 18.** The answer "longing for justice has its answer in God" comes from b028 (the closing section), not from the "Boomerang" section (b010), which is about grounding the moral standard. The key is defensible, but the stem mixes the two sections.
4. **SA 1 model.** It says the problem is "the strongest arrow in the skeptic's quiver" (keyword "arrow"). The lesson says "trump card". The French model uses "atout".
5. **SA 8 model.** It says other religions offer a god who "calls suffering an illusion". The lesson does not teach this, though it appears as an MC 12 distractor. The keywords "unique" and "distant" are not lesson words ("far throne").
6. **SA 10 model and keyword "presence".** The lesson's closing section does not mention offering our presence. "Presence" appears only for Job, in b019. I kept the sentence in the model and replaced the keyword.
7. All answer keys match the lesson.

## Adaptations

- "Trump card" became "atout maître". Card games are familiar in Kinshasa, so no further change was needed.
- "The baby who never drew a guilty breath" became "le bébé qui n'a jamais commis la moindre faute". The English idiom does not carry over into French.
- I kept the seagull and raft illustration from Dr Cook's sermon (b021) as it is: "la mouette arrive".
- b031 (track instructions) is identical to the Unit 1 French text.
