## Questions for Wayne

1. **1 Peter 3:15, "give an answer... for the hope".** LSG has "étant toujours prêts à vous défendre, avec douceur et respect, devant quiconque vous demande raison de l'espérance qui est en vous". There is no noun "answer", and LSG's order puts "douceur et respect" in the middle of the verse. The lesson leans on "answer = apologia" (b011, b034, MC2, Fill 1, SA1). I quoted LSG and added in b011: "Le texte grec dit littéralement : toujours prêts à donner une apologia, une réponse." The opening line b004 quotes LSG with ellipses. Note also that LSG starts with "sanctifiez... Christ le Seigneur" where the NKJV has "revere Christ as Lord" (b009).
2. **"Gentleness and respect"** is "douceur et respect" in LSG. That matches the English, so b031, b032, MC13 and Fill 10 use it unchanged.
3. **Romans 1:20.** LSG has "les perfections invisibles de Dieu... se voient comme à l'oeil... quand on les considère dans ses ouvrages. Ils sont donc inexcusables". The NKJV has "clearly seen... understood from what has been made... without excuse". I quoted LSG in b019 and used its wording in MC12 ("se voient comme à l'œil... dans ses ouvrages") and SA8. The SA8 keywords accept both "inexcusable" and "sans excuse".
4. **Romans 1:16 (b035, MC20).** LSG has "*une* puissance de Dieu" where the NKJV has "*the* power of God". I quoted it as LSG, stopping at "quiconque croit", which is where the English quotation stops.
5. **John 20:25-27 (b023, MC8, Fill 6).** Thomas speaks of "la marque des clous", not "wounds", and Jesus says "Avance ici ton doigt", not "Put your finger here". I used LSG's words. Fill 6 accepts "Didyme" (John 20:24).
6. **"Design" is translated "dessein"** (purpose/plan), not "design", which would be an anglicism in theological French. **"Fine-tuning" is "réglage fin".** Please have the French reader confirm both.
7. **"Evidence" is translated "preuves" throughout.** "Évidence" is a false friend in French. Fill 5's answer is "preuves".

## Problems in the English source

1. **b018 is missing the start of the story.** It begins "He had thought of everything but one thing", but no block tells the story itself: the man eating the whole tray, washing it, the wife walking in. The SA2 model and MC5 depend on that missing part (MC5's options "crumbs" and "receipt" make no sense without it). The Spanish edition has the same gap. I translated what is there.
2. **SA8 asks about "Romans 1:18-20".** The lesson quotes and teaches only 1:20.
3. **SA keywords.** Several English prompts hit their own keywords: SA4 (6 hits), SA10 (5), SA3, SA6 and SA9 (2 each). Under convention 10, I chose French groups so that no French prompt gets more than 1 hit.
4. **MC4** says the stones are things that block a person "from Christ". The lesson says they block him from *seeing* Christ. The meaning is close, and I kept "voir" in the French.
5. **MC18** says "the inner witness of the Spirit". The lesson's wording is "the inner seal of the Spirit". The French follows the MC wording.

## Adaptations

- "A neighbor over the fence" became "un voisin, par-dessus le mur de la cour". Kinshasa compounds are walled, not fenced.
- I kept the title "Ice-Box Cookie" as "Les biscuits du réfrigérateur". "Receipt" became "ticket de caisse".
- "Slick as a whistle" became "nette comme un sou neuf".
- In MC14, the option "Because Spanish is required" is a deliberate distractor. I translated it literally ("l'espagnol").
