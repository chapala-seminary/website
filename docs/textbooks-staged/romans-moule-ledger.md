# Romans textbook examination — Moule source-quotation ledger

**Draft 1 · 10 October 2026 · not installed**

Source: H. C. G. Moule, *The Expositor’s Bible: The Epistle of St Paul to the Romans* (1894), Gutenberg ebook #48858.

Every quotation below is an exact sentence in the existing CTS first-textbook batch’s English text. The exam tests Moule’s interpretation rather than repeating the five CTS digest assessments.

## Group 1 — Chapters III, VIII

### Q01 · Chapter III · communion

> "He serves Him," in that great branch of ministry, "in his spirit" with his whole love, will, and mind, working in communion with his Lord.

### Q02 · Chapter III · ministerially

> And this was to be done ministerially, not magisterially.

### Q03 · Chapter III · duty

> But supernaturally, in Christ, he loved both, and scrupulously remembered his duty to both.

### Q04 · Chapter III · correlative

> It is the correlative of the overwhelming, the absolute, prominence of Jesus Christ.

### Q05 · Chapter VIII · inalienable

> "Whose doom is just." What a witness is this to the inalienable truthfulness of the Gospel!

### Q06 · Chapter VIII · truthfulness

> But years moved, and he saw deeper into himself, seeing deeper into the holiness of God; and the truthfulness of that passage grew upon him.

### Q07 · Chapter VIII · silence

> "That every mouth may be stopped." Oh solemn silence, when at last it comes!

### Q08 · Chapter VIII · paradox

> And of course the mighty paradox of Justification lent itself easily to the distortions, as well as to the contradictions, of sinners.

## Group 2 — Chapters IX, XII

### Q09 · Chapter IX · surgery

> It is to be morally diseased, and in need of surgery and medicine.

### Q10 · Chapter IX · guilty

> But first of all it is to be morally guilty, and in urgent need of justification, of a reversal of sentence, of satisfactory settlement with the offended--and eternal--Law of God.

### Q11 · Chapter IX · riddle

> The meaning of Justification is now to you no riddle of the schools.

### Q12 · Chapter IX · self-applause

> And great is the rest and gladness of sitting down at His feet, while the door is shut and the key is turned upon our self-applause.

### Q13 · Chapter XII · magnet

> Not, surely, by implanting a new faculty, but by so opening the soul to God in Christ that the divine magnet effectually draws the man to a willing repose upon such a God.

### Q14 · Chapter XII · lawfulness

> He is reasoning on; "For Christ, when we still were weak." He has set justification before us in its majestic lawfulness.

### Q15 · Chapter XII · suasion

> For them "Christ died"; not "lived," let us remember, but "died." It was a question not of example, nor of suasion, nor even of utterances of divine compassion.

### Q16 · Chapter XII · irradiated

> It is to appear as a state warmed with eternal Love; irradiated with the prospect of glory.

## Group 3 — Chapters XIV, XVII

### Q17 · Chapter XIV · recommendatory

> And he must have meant by justification without works, what those words ought to mean, acceptance irrespective of our recommendatory conduct.

### Q18 · Chapter XIV · isolated

> So that, while the Way of Justification can be isolated for study, as it has been in this Epistle, the justified man cannot be isolated from Christ, who is his life.

### Q19 · Chapter XIV · glorious call

> And thus he can never ultimately be considered apart from his possession, in Christ, of a new possibility, a new power, a new and glorious call to living holiness.

### Q20 · Chapter XIV · fountain

> And it is as justified that he has access to the fountain, and drinks as deep as he will of its life, its power, its purity.

### Q21 · Chapter XVII · Pentecost

> To one it is a crisis of memorable consciousness, a private Pentecost.

### Q22 · Chapter XVII · organically

> All the reasons, methods, and issues of the work of the Holy Ghost are eternally and organically connected with the Son of God.

### Q23 · Chapter XVII · emancipated

> And this "law," unlike the stern "letter" of Sinai, has actually "freed me." It has endowed me not only with place but with power, in which to live emancipated from a rival law, the law of sin and of death.

### Q24 · Chapter XVII · stultify

> That "for" cannot mean to imply that there is no condemnation because the Spirit has enabled us to be holy; this would stultify the whole argument of chapters iii.-v.

## Group 4 — Chapters XX, XXIV

### Q25 · Chapter XX · veil

> He knows that only righteousness and love is behind it; but he knows that it is a veil, and that in front of it man's thought must cease and be silent.

### Q26 · Chapter XX · Promiser

> One thing only cannot be; the promises have not failed; there has been no failure in the Promiser.

### Q27 · Chapter XX · mystery

> And the whole tone of the record (not to speak of this its apostolic interpretation) looks towards mystery, not explanation.

### Q28 · Chapter XX · recipient

> But it could be put only into a recipient hand open and empty.

### Q29 · Chapter XXIV · mystically

> But surely it puts violence on words, and on thought, to explain "Israel" in this whole passage mystically.

### Q30 · Chapter XXIV · critical

> But we may surely gather that the numbers blessed will be at least the vast majority, and that the work will not be chronic but critical.

### Q31 · Chapter XXIV · unmistakably

> All we do now is to express the conviction that the prophetic quotations here cannot be held to predict unmistakably a visible and local Return.

### Q32 · Chapter XXIV · caution

> But it still seems to us that a deep and reverent caution is called for before we attempt to treat of any detail of that prospect, as regards time, season, mode, as if we quite knew.

## Group 5 — Chapters XXV, XXIX

### Q33 · Chapter XXV · moral directory

> Rather, because they are supplied and assured to the believer, it spreads out all the more amply and minutely a moral directory before his eyes.

### Q34 · Chapter XXV · accurately

> Alas for us if we shew ever so convincingly, and even ever so winningly, the bond between salvation and holiness, and do not "walk accurately" (Eph. v. 15) ourselves, in the details of our walk.

### Q35 · Chapter XXV · body

> It is only through the body, practically, that we can "serve our generation by the will of God." Not without the body but through it the spirit must tell on the embodied spirits around us.

### Q36 · Chapter XXV · self-surrender

> This relative aspect of the life of self-surrender is the burthen of this great paragraph of duty.

### Q37 · Chapter XXIX · patience

> Their "strength" in Christ was never to be ungentle; never to be "used like a giant." It was to be shewn, first and most, by patience.

### Q38 · Chapter XXIX · individualism

> The individualism of Rom. xiv., asserted in an argument full of the deepest secrets of cohesion, is the holy and healthful thing it is because it is Christian.

### Q39 · Chapter XXIX · MASTER

> It is a deep reminder that the beloved Saviour is also, and in no figure of speech, but in an eternal earnest, THE MASTER too.

### Q40 · Chapter XXIX · conscience

> It was to appear as reverence for another's conscience, even when the conscience went astray for want of better light.
