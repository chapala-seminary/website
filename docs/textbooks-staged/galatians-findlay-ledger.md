# Galatians textbook examination — Findlay source-quotation ledger

**Draft 1 · 10 October 2026 · not installed**

Source: G. G. Findlay, *The Expositor’s Bible: The Epistle to the Galatians* (1893), Gutenberg ebook #42645.

Every quotation below is taken from the exact Gutenberg text collected in the existing CTS first-textbook batch. This draft tests Findlay’s reading of Paul, not the five CTS digest summaries.

## Group 1 — Chapters IV

### Q01 · Chapter IV · legalism

> On the ruins of confuted legalism he builds up an impregnable fortress for Christian liberty, an immortal vindication of the gospel of the grace of God.

### Q02 · Chapter IV · Gentile

> The cause of Gentile freedom at this crisis was bound up with the person of the Apostle Paul.

### Q03 · Chapter IV · Apostolic character

> In the eyes of his children in the faith, he is bound to vindicate his Apostolic character, defamed by Jewish malice and untruth.

### Q04 · Chapter IV · supernaturally

> Paul's gospel was supernaturally conveyed to him, by a personal intervention of Jesus Christ. This assertion is the Apostle's starting-point.

### Q05 · Chapter IV · inviolable

> The gospel of Paul was inviolable, then, because of its superhuman character.

### Q06 · Chapter IV · second-hand

> He is no Apostle at second-hand, no disciple of Peter's or dependant of the "pillars" at Jerusalem.

### Q07 · Chapter IV · interposition

> The chasm between his Christian and pre-Christian life had only been bridged by a supernatural interposition of the mercy of Christ.

### Q08 · Chapter IV · objective

> The objective vision could only have revealed a "Christ after the flesh," had it not been the means of opening Saul's closed heart to the influence of the Spirit of Christ.

## Group 2 — Chapters X

### Q09 · Chapter X · pride

> In his self-assertion there is no note of pride or personal anxiety.

### Q10 · Chapter X · confession

> Read in the light of the foregoing narrative, this passage is a compendium of the Pauline Gospel, invested with the peculiar interest that belongs to a confession of personal faith, made at a signal crisis in the author's life.

### Q11 · Chapter X · touchstone

> "The grace of God" is the touchstone to which Peter's dissimulation is finally brought.

### Q12 · Chapter X · redeeming

> God's grace is not His love alone; it is redeeming love--love poured out upon the undeserving, love coming to seek and save the lost, "bringing salvation to all men" (Rom. v. 1-8; Tit. ii. 11).

### Q13 · Chapter X · antithesis

> Grace in God is therefore the antithesis of sin in man, counterworking and finally triumphing over it.

### Q14 · Chapter X · response

> While sin is the reply which man's nature makes to the demands of law, faith is the response elicited by grace; it is the door of the heart opening to grace.[66] Grace and Faith go hand in hand, as Law and Transgression.

### Q15 · Chapter X · basis

> But it was, in the first place, the basis of his personal life.

### Q16 · Chapter X · communion

> The office of the Holy Spirit, whose communion holds together the glorified Lord and His members upon earth, is implied in the teaching of ver. 20.

## Group 3 — Chapters XIV, XVI

### Q17 · Chapter XIV · ancillary

> The Legal economy is ancillary thereto; it never professed to accomplish the work of grace, as the Judaists would have it do.

### Q18 · Chapter XIV · preliminary

> They brought in the Law to perfect the unfinished work of faith (ver. 3): he made it preliminary and propædeutic.

### Q19 · Chapter XIV · reveals

> By bringing about repeated transgressions the Law reveals the true nature of sin, so that it "becomes exceeding sinful." It does not make matters worse; but it shows how bad they really are.

### Q20 · Chapter XIV · discipline

> Like the discipline and drill of a strictly governed boyhood, it was calculated to produce a certain effect on the moral nature, after the attainment of which it was no longer needed and its continuance would be injurious.

### Q21 · Chapter XVI · minority

> We see, first, the heir of God in his minority; and again, the same heir in possession of his estate.

### Q22 · Chapter XVI · majority

> In Roman and in Jewish law the date of civil majority was fixed.

### Q23 · Chapter XVI · pupilage

> A grant is made by a father still living, who keeps his son in pupilage till he sees fit to put him in possession of the promised estate.

### Q24 · Chapter XVI · enriched

> The heritage of Abraham came into possession of his heirs enriched by their long minority.

## Group 4 — Chapters XXII, XXV

### Q25 · Chapter XXII · antinomianism

> They vindicate Paul's gospel from the charge of antinomianism, while they guard Christians from giving occasion to the charge.

### Q26 · Chapter XXII · untrained

> Here lies the danger of liberty, especially when conferred on a young, untrained nature, and in a newly emancipated community.

### Q27 · Chapter XXII · responsibility

> Freedom is a priceless boon; but it is a grave responsibility.

### Q28 · Chapter XXII · licence

> Men clamour for liberty, when they mean licence; what they seek is the liberty of the flesh, not of the Spirit, freedom to indulge their lusts and to trample on the rights of others, the freedom of outlaws and brigands.

### Q29 · Chapter XXV · cardinal

> It is interesting to compare the Apostle's definitions with Plato's celebrated scheme of the four cardinal virtues.

### Q30 · Chapter XXV · Platonic

> The difference between the cast of the Platonic and Pauline ethics is most instructive.

### Q31 · Chapter XXV · humility

> Love and humility are the two features whose predominance distinguishes the Christian from the purest classical conceptions of moral worth.

### Q32 · Chapter XXV · vice

> The pride of philosophy regarded it as a vice rather than a virtue.

## Group 5 — Chapters XXVI, XXVII

### Q33 · Chapter XXVI · lenient

> The suddenness, the surprise of his temptation is both a reason for more lenient judgment, and a ground for hope of his restoration.

### Q34 · Chapter XXVI · implacable

> An offence committed in a rash moment, under provocation, must not be visited with implacable severity, nor magnified until it become a fatal barrier between the evil-doer and society.

### Q35 · Chapter XXVI · patient skill

> The work of restoration needs a knowledge of the human heart, a self-restraint and patient skill, quite beyond their capability.

### Q36 · Chapter XXVI · recovery

> The restoration here signified, denotes not only, or not so much, the man's inward, spiritual renewal, as his recovery for the Church, the mending of the rent caused by his removal.

### Q37 · Chapter XXVII · social

> Personal responsibility is the last word of the former paragraph; social responsibility is the first word of this.

### Q38 · Chapter XXVII · fellowship

> But it is spiritual fellowship that the Apostle chiefly desiderates.

### Q39 · Chapter XXVII · pecuniary

> Of that return the pecuniary contribution, the meed of "carnal things" with which so many seem to think their debt discharged, is often the least and easiest part.

### Q40 · Chapter XXVII · immortality

> The use made of our seed-time determines exactly, and with a moral certainty greater even than that which rules in the natural field, what kind of fruitage our immortality will render.
