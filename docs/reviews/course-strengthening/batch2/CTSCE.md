# CTSCE — Christian Education (Andi Cook): Proposed Additions

## Assessment

This is a warm, biblically rich course. Andi's voice is clear, and each method is anchored in a memorable biblical model (Stephen, Emmaus, Nathan, Jerusalem Council, the Twelve sent out). The 2026 editorial notes already cover several gaps: low-resource rooms (U5 b017), measuring learning (U1 b030, U6 b023), confidentiality (U2 b031), and a solid child-protection section (U10 b042). What is still missing is practical help for the situations most CTS students face. The course never explains how to recruit, screen, and train the volunteers who will do the teaching. It names the home as the first classroom (U1 b034, U4 b036–b037) but never shows parents how to teach there. It gives no help for teaching where few people can read. And it does not treat the new believer as a learner of his own kind. The four short additions below fill those gaps, adding about 1,000 words. The trims listed at the end would remove more than that.

## Proposals

### 1. Unit 2, after [b045] — Raising Up Other Teachers

**WHY:** The unit Scripture (2 Timothy 2:2) is about passing teaching on to others, but the unit never explains how a church finds, screens, and trains volunteer teachers. This is also where child-safety screening belongs, since U10 b042 covers conduct in the classroom but not who is allowed to teach.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **CTS Addition (2026): Raising Up Other Teachers**
>
> Paul did not tell Timothy only to teach. He told him to "commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2). Every teacher should be looking for the next teacher. A church that depends on one or two tired teachers will lose its classes the day those teachers move away or fall ill.
>
> Choose carefully. Paul warned, "Do not lay hands on anyone hastily" (1 Timothy 5:22), and of deacons he said, "let these also first be tested; then let them serve" (1 Timothy 3:10). Before anyone teaches children or youth, the church should know that person well. A simple rule helps: a new volunteer should be a known member of the congregation for a period the church sets (many churches use six months), should be approved by the pastor or leaders, and should never be placed alone with minors. This protects the children, and it protects the teacher too.
>
> Then train the way Jesus trained, by apprenticeship. First, the new helper watches an experienced teacher. Second, the helper assists by greeting the children, leading the song, or telling the Bible story. Third, the helper teaches a full lesson while the experienced teacher watches. Fourth, the two talk afterward: What went well? What was the aim? Did the learners reach it? Only then does the new teacher take a class, and even then he should be visited and encouraged.
>
> Meet with your teachers regularly, even for half an hour once a month, to pray, plan, and share what is working. No printed program is required. What is required is a faithful teacher who is looking for another faithful teacher.

(about 290 words)

### 2. Unit 4, after [b029] — The New Believer

**WHY:** Unit 4 sorts learners by age (child, youth, adult). The new convert of any age has needs of his own, and in churches that are growing through evangelism he may be the most common learner in the room. U7 b042 mentions new believers only in passing.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **CTS Addition (2026): The New Believer**
>
> One more kind of learner belongs in every church's thinking: the new believer. He may be sixty years old, but in the faith he is an infant. Peter wrote, "as newborn babes, desire the pure milk of the word, that you may grow thereby" (1 Peter 2:2). A newborn needs to be fed often, simply, and by someone who loves him.
>
> The first church shows the pattern. Three thousand people were converted on one day, and right away "they continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers" (Acts 2:42). Teaching, fellowship, worship, and prayer began at once. Nobody waited a year to start.
>
> A church does not need a printed program to do the same. Pair each new believer with a mature believer of the same sex who will meet with him weekly for the first few months. Walk through a short set of foundations together. Who is Jesus, and what did He do on the cross? What does it mean to be saved and to have assurance? How do I read the Bible and pray? Why do believers gather, give, and obey Christ in baptism and the Lord's Supper? How do I tell my family what has happened to me?
>
> Keep each meeting simple: read one passage, ask what it teaches about God and about us, decide on one step of obedience for the week, and pray. Next week, ask about that step. The new believer who is fed in the first months is far more likely to be standing firm years later. The one who is only welcomed and then left alone often drifts away.

(about 280 words)

### 3. Unit 4, after [b040] — Helping Parents Teach at Home

**WHY:** The course rightly calls the home the first classroom (U1 b034; U4 b036–b040) but never gives parents a practical pattern for it. Many CTS families are first-generation believers, or have parents who cannot read. This addition also covers teaching across generations.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **CTS Addition (2026): Helping Parents Teach at Home**
>
> Deuteronomy 6 places the first classroom in the home, but many parents feel they cannot teach. Some were never taught themselves. Some cannot read well. Some are the first believers in their family. The church's teacher can help them by sending the lesson home.
>
> A simple home pattern needs no book. Once during the week, at a meal or at bedtime, the family does four things. They retell the Bible story from Sunday, with the children telling as much of it as they can. They ask one question: "What does this story show us about God?" They say the memory verse together. And they pray for one another by name. Ten minutes is enough. A parent who cannot read can still retell a story, ask a question, and pray.
>
> The Sunday teacher can make this easier. End each class with one question for the children to ask at home, and the next week ask the children what their family said. This builds a bridge between the church and the home without shaming families who are weak.
>
> Where there is no believing parent, the church becomes the family. The psalmist promised, "We will not hide them from their children, telling to the generation to come the praises of the LORD" (Psalm 78:4). Older believers who have walked with God for many years are a great treasure here. Invite a grandmother to tell the class how the Lord kept her through a hard season. Ask an older man to pray over the boys. Seat young and old together sometimes. "One generation shall praise Your works to another" (Psalm 145:4). The Sunday class lasts an hour. A believing household, or an adopted spiritual family, teaches all week.

(about 300 words)

### 4. Unit 7, after [b033] — Telling the Bible Story Where Few Can Read

**WHY:** Many CTS students teach in oral cultures and low-literacy congregations with no printed curriculum. The story section (Nathan) shows that story is powerful but does not show how to teach the Bible accurately to learners who cannot read it for themselves.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **CTS Addition (2026): Telling the Bible Story Where Few Can Read**
>
> In many congregations few people can read, and fewer still own a Bible. This does not keep them from the Word, for "faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God" (Romans 10:17). For most of history, most believers received Scripture by hearing it. When Ezra taught the returned exiles, "they read distinctly from the book, in the Law of God; and they gave the sense, and helped them to understand the reading" (Nehemiah 8:8).
>
> The teacher can teach Scripture faithfully in an oral setting with a simple pattern. First, prepare by reading the passage many times until you can tell it accurately. Do not add details that are not in the text, and do not leave out the hard parts. Second, tell the story clearly, in the people's own language, with the names and events as Scripture gives them. Third, have the group retell it. Ask one person to tell it back, and let the others correct anything missed. Fourth, ask simple questions: What happened? What does this show us about God? What does it show us about people? What will we do this week because of it? Fifth, if someone in the group can read, have the passage read aloud so everyone hears the Word itself.
>
> Telling the stories in order, from Creation to Christ, helps listeners see how the whole Bible fits together. A learner who can retell twenty Bible stories accurately carries the Bible with him into his village and can teach it to his own family. Jesus "spoke the word to them as they were able to hear it" (Mark 4:33). We follow Him when we do the same.

(about 300 words)

## Errors or problems found

1. **Translation consistency (whole course).** The unit Scriptures are NKJV, but most quotations inside the lessons are KJV. Examples: U1 b036 and U3 b027 ("Suffer the little children…"), U4 b017, U5 b015/b020, U6 b015, U7–U10 throughout. If CTS standardizes on NKJV, these need converting. Check the exam wording first, because some questions may quote the KJV phrasing. Also, U7 b044/b048 and U9 b018/b045 say "pictures of silver" (KJV), while the U7 unit Scripture (b008) says "settings of silver" (NKJV).
2. **U1 b046.** "46 AD when Paul wrote to Timothy" is wrong. 2 Timothy is usually dated about AD 64–67. Suggest "in the first century, when Paul wrote to Timothy."
3. **U1 b059.** "A scroll that the Holy Spirit dictated" contradicts b057, which says God used each writer's own personality and style (inspiration, not dictation). Suggest "a scroll the Holy Spirit breathed out." "Nineteen centuries" should be "twenty centuries" (or "two thousand years").
4. **U1 b049 vs U10 b038.** U1 says Timothy was not "personally saved" until Paul led him to Christ. U10 says the childhood reading "was already the means by which God had been saving him." Scripture does not tell us exactly when Timothy was converted. Softening U1 ("Paul calls him 'my true son in the faith,' 1 Timothy 1:2") would remove the tension.
5. **U1 b018.** "Make Quirks and Female Foibles" looks like a typo, possibly for "Male Quirks…". "Artic MK" may be meant as "Arctic". Please verify both titles. Also, b015/b029 call all ten works "biblical fiction," but some titles (*Cancer, God, and Me*; *Crash Test Dummy*) do not sound like fiction. Consider "ten books, many of them biblical fiction."
6. **U6 b022.** "The text records exactly when each gate was built and by which family." Nehemiah 3 records who built each section but not when. Suggest "The text records which family built each section and gate."
7. **U6 b048.** "The Master Teacher used this form, his apostles used this form" overstates the careful disclaimer in b029 ("not as proof the apostles were consciously following this outline"). Suggest "The pattern can be seen in…".
8. **U10 b022–b023.** b022 describes three motions (arms, hands, blessing), but b023 says "learns from those four motions." Suggest "learns four lessons from those motions."
9. **U10 b025 vs b026–b030.** b025 says the sermon "does five things, each in about a minute" and speaks of "five movements." But only four are named (gather, story [2 minutes], truth, response), and b030 adds "one minute of buffer" even though the five minutes are already used up. Suggest saying "four movements" in b025 and dropping or reworking the buffer phrase in b030.
10. **U10 b028.** The Daniel lions' den lesson cites Daniel 3:17–18, which is the fiery furnace passage spoken by Daniel's three friends. Either cite Daniel 6:22–23 for the lions' den, or label the reference as the three friends' words.
11. **U10 b021.** The claim that the verb for "much displeased" is "the same verb the Gospels use for righteous indignation" is shaky. The same word (*aganakteō*) is used of the disciples' jealousy (Mark 10:41) and of the chief priests (Matthew 21:15). Suggest "a strong word for indignation."
12. **U10 b051.** "On the bench in the seminary courtyard at Chapala" suggests a physical campus. CTS is a free self-study seminary, so students may be misled. Suggest "if she were sitting beside you as you teach."
13. **U10 b052.** "The Lord will save the children he plans to save" leans toward determinism. Given the course's Arminian-leaning but fair stance, consider "the Lord will use whatever five minutes you give him to draw the children to Himself."
14. **U9 b039.** "Folded a paper boat and named it the Mayflower while studying Pilgrim history" is a North American and non-biblical example for a Latin American and African audience. Suggest "a paper boat for Paul's shipwreck (Acts 27)" or "Noah's ark."
15. **U9 b052.** The exam-instruction text differs from every other unit, and the MC/SA/Submit/Reset/Catalog blocks are missing. This may point to a different exam engine on this page and is worth checking.

## Places that could be trimmed

- **U1 b029:** Repeats Andi's biography from b015 and b019 almost word for word. Cut to one sentence (about 120 words saved). b074–b075 repeat the same tribute a third time and could be shortened as well.
- **U1 b058–b059:** Two paragraphs make the same point about the Bible's authority in the classroom (also made in b045–b046). Keep one (about 130 words).
- **U3 b012–b014 and U2 b043–b045:** List the six characteristics again before and after the sections that cover them. Could be compressed (about 100 words).
- **U5 b043–b046 vs b018 and b041:** The "Saturday preparation / Mark 14:15 room" point is made at least four times in the unit, and again in the closing (b048–b049). Section VI could be cut by half (about 200 words).
- **U6 b047–b049:** A three-paragraph closing that restates the unit. One paragraph is enough (about 120 words).
- **U7 b046–b048, U8 b047–b049, U9 b047–b050, U10 b013 and b049–b050:** Each unit ends with a recap of the whole course so far. The U9 and U10 recaps overlap heavily. Shortening these could save 400 or more words.
- **U10 b015:** The history of the children's sermon form (Puritans, Sunday school) is not needed for the lesson and could be one sentence (about 80 words).
