# Apologetics (CTSApol) — Proposed Additions for Dr. Cook's Review

## Overall assessment

This is a warm, pastoral, and well-built course. The "clear the stones / reason brings a man to the door" theme ties all ten units together, and recent revisions have made the hard spots honest and modest (the moral argument, Jupiter, Stoner's odds, the free-will note, the 1 Corinthians 15 creed, terminal lucidity). The biggest gap is the actual questions our students hear in Latin America and Africa. Unit 9 deals with "all roads lead to God" and Western atheism, but it never answers a Muslim neighbor, a Jehovah's Witness at the door, or the fear of witchcraft and the ancestors. Nothing addresses prosperity teaching either, which is the most common false answer to suffering among the poor. A smaller gap: the course uses several apologetic methods but never names them, so students do not learn that faithful Christians argue in different ways. Most units run 1,700–2,200 words, so there is room for these additions without going much past the 2,500-word target.

## Proposals

### 1. Unit 1 — after [b028]
**Title:** Four Ways Christians Have Made the Case

**WHY:** The course already uses the classical method (Unit 2), the evidential method (Units 5 and 7), presuppositional ideas (Unit 1 [b022], Unit 8 [b018]) and the cumulative case (Unit 10 [b016]), but it never names them. A short, even-handed map helps students recognize each one and see that evangelicals hold all of them in good conscience.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **Four Ways Christians Have Made the Case**
>
> Faithful Christians do not all defend the faith in the same way, and it will help you to know the main approaches by name. In this course you will meet all of them.
>
> The *classical* approach works in two steps. First it shows that God exists, using arguments from cause, design, and the moral law. Then it shows that this God has spoken in Christ. Unit 2 follows this path.
>
> The *evidential* approach begins with history: the reliability of the documents, fulfilled prophecy, and above all the resurrection. It asks the honest person to weigh the facts as a jury would. Units 5 and 7 lean this way.
>
> The *presuppositional* approach says there is no neutral ground. Every person reasons from basic beliefs he has already accepted, and only the God of the Bible explains why reason, logic, and morality can be trusted at all. So this approach does not set Scripture to one side while it argues. It shows the unbeliever that his own worldview cannot carry the weight he puts on it. Paul speaks of "casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:5). When we said above that the atheist is not standing on neutral ground, we were borrowing from this tradition.
>
> The *cumulative case* approach does not lean on any single argument. It lays many lines of evidence side by side and asks which explanation best fits the whole picture. Unit 10 returns to this idea.
>
> Thoughtful believers disagree about which approach should come first, and each has its strengths. The classical and evidential methods meet the skeptic on common ground. The presuppositional method exposes the hidden assumptions behind his objections. The cumulative case shows how strong the evidence is when it is taken together. A wise apologist learns from all of them and uses whichever one best fits the person in front of him. Whatever the method, the goal stays the same: to remove the stones so that a person can see Christ.

(About 330 words.)

### 2. Unit 4 — after [b022]
**Title:** Does Suffering Mean Your Faith Is Weak?

**WHY:** Many of our students minister among people living in poverty and violence, and in those places the prosperity message offers a false answer: sickness and poverty prove that a person lacks faith or is under a curse. This addition answers that error from Scripture and fits the unit's theme that God is present in suffering.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **Does Suffering Mean Your Faith Is Weak?**
>
> In many of our communities a different answer to suffering is preached loudly, and it wounds people. Some teachers say that God always wants His children healthy and prosperous, so anyone who stays sick or poor must lack faith, must be hiding sin, or has not given enough money to the preacher. To a mother who cannot feed her children, or a family that has lost a son to violence, this message adds guilt on top of grief.
>
> Scripture does not teach it. Jesus told His disciples plainly, "In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world" (John 16:33). Paul, a man of great faith, prayed three times for his thorn in the flesh to be removed. God did not remove it. He answered, "My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9). Paul and Barnabas strengthened the young churches by telling them, "We must through many tribulations enter the kingdom of God" (Acts 14:22). Hebrews 11 praises those who by faith conquered kingdoms, and in the same breath it praises others who by faith were tortured, imprisoned, and went about destitute, "of whom the world was not worthy" (Hebrews 11:38). Their faith was not weaker. Their road was harder.
>
> Paul even warned against people "who suppose that godliness is a means of gain," and added, "Now godliness with contentment is great gain" (1 Timothy 6:5–6).
>
> God does heal, and He does provide, and we should pray boldly for both. But the gospel is not a contract that guarantees comfort in this life. When the skeptic asks why believers suffer, the honest answer is not "they did not believe enough." We live in a fallen world, and God has promised to walk through it with us and to bring us out at the end into a world without tears. A gospel that only works for the healthy and the rich is no answer to the problem of evil. The crucified Lord is.

(About 360 words.)

### 3. Unit 9 — after [b016]
**Title:** Answering Our Muslim Neighbors

**WHY:** Islam is a major presence in Africa and growing in Latin America. Our students regularly hear three objections: the Bible has been corrupted, Jesus was not crucified, and the Trinity means three gods. Unit 9 never addresses Islam directly, and this short section gives clear, respectful answers that build on Units 5 and 7.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **Answering Our Muslim Neighbors**
>
> Many of you live and work beside Muslim neighbors, and they will often raise three objections. Answer them as a friend, never as an enemy.
>
> *"Your Bible has been corrupted."* Ask kindly when it was changed, and by whom. The Qur'an itself speaks respectfully of the Torah and the Gospel, and it tells Christians to judge by what God revealed in them (Surah 5:47). It also says that no one can change the words of God (Surah 6:115). Yet we possess complete copies of the New Testament from the 300s, such as Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus, and many earlier portions besides. All of these come from centuries before Muhammad was born, and they say what our Bibles say today. If the Gospel was trustworthy in Muhammad's time, it is trustworthy now, because it is the same text. This is the manuscript evidence of Unit 5 put to work.
>
> *"Jesus was not crucified."* The Qur'an says it only "appeared so" (Surah 4:157). But this was written about six hundred years after the event. The crucifixion of Jesus is among the most firmly established facts of ancient history. It is reported by every Gospel, by Paul within about twenty-five years, and by non-Christian writers such as the Roman historian Tacitus and the Jewish historian Josephus. An account written six centuries later cannot overturn the testimony of those who were there.
>
> *"The Trinity means three gods."* Agree warmly that God is one, for so says the Bible: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one!" (Deuteronomy 6:4). Christians do not worship three gods. Nor do we believe in a "trinity" of God, Jesus, and Mary, which some people wrongly suppose. We believe that the one God exists eternally as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. That is why Jesus told us to baptize "in the name" (singular) "of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19). It is a mystery, but it is not a contradiction.
>
> Above all, show your Muslim friend the love of Christ, invite him to read the Gospels for himself, and pray for him.

(About 370 words.)

### 4. Unit 9 — after [b018]
**Title:** Spirits, Curses, and the Ancestors

**WHY:** In much of Africa and Latin America the strongest rival to the gospel is not atheism but folk religion. That means fear of witchcraft and curses, visits to the healer or diviner, and offerings to the ancestors, often mixed with church attendance. The course never mentions this. Here the apologetic task is to show that Christ is strong enough, not to argue that the spirit world doesn't exist.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **Spirits, Curses, and the Ancestors**
>
> In many of our villages and cities the real question is not "Does God exist?" Almost everyone believes He does. The question is "Is Christ strong enough?" People go to church on Sunday, and on Tuesday they visit the healer, wear a charm against the evil eye, or leave an offering for the spirits of the ancestors, just in case. We must not mock these fears. Scripture takes the spiritual world seriously, and people's fear of curses and witchcraft is often very real. But the Bible gives a better answer than the charm.
>
> First, Christ has already defeated the powers. At the cross, "having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them in it" (Colossians 2:15). The believer does not need to buy protection from a lesser power when he belongs to the One who conquered them all: "He who is in you is greater than he who is in the world" (1 John 4:4).
>
> Second, God forbids His people to seek help from diviners, sorcerers, or those who consult the dead (Deuteronomy 18:10–12). He forbids it because He is jealous for our trust and because those powers are not our friends, not because He has nothing better to offer.
>
> Third, the Bible teaches that "it is appointed for men to die once, but after this the judgment" (Hebrews 9:27). Our loved ones who have died are in God's hands. They are not wandering spirits waiting to be fed or appeased. We may honor their memory with gratitude, but we pray to God alone.
>
> When you speak with someone caught between Christ and the old ways, be patient. Fear does not leave in a single conversation. Show from your own life that you sleep in peace without the charm, and tell them the Lord who stilled the storm and cast out demons with a word is the same Lord today.

(About 350 words.)

### 5. Unit 10 — after [b012]
**Title:** When the Knock Comes to Your Door

**WHY:** Jehovah's Witnesses, Latter-day Saints (Mormons), and Oneness Pentecostals are active throughout our students' regions, and church members often feel unprepared when they come to the door. Unit 10 is about apologetics in practice, so a brief, gentle guide to the key passages fits here, and it puts the unit's "meekness and fear" teaching to work.

**PROPOSED TEXT:**

> **When the Knock Comes to Your Door**
>
> Many of you will meet apologetics on your own doorstep, when two neatly dressed visitors come to talk about the Bible. Treat them with kindness. They are usually sincere, and they are seldom treated well. Paul reminds us that "a servant of the Lord must not quarrel but be gentle to all, able to teach, patient, in humility correcting those who are in opposition" (2 Timothy 2:24–25). Stay with a few clear passages, and keep bringing the conversation back to one question: who is Jesus?
>
> *Jehovah's Witnesses* teach that Jesus is a created being, the first and greatest of God's creatures, but not God. Their own translation renders John 1:1 as "the Word was a god." Ask them gently how this fits Isaiah 43:10, where the Lord says, "Before Me there was no God formed, nor shall there be after Me." Then turn to Thomas, who looked at the risen Jesus and said, "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28). Jesus did not correct him.
>
> *Latter-day Saints* add other books to the Bible and teach that God the Father was once a man who became God. Read with them Isaiah 44:8: "Is there a God besides Me? Indeed there is no other Rock; I know not one." Show them Paul's warning: "Even if we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel to you than what we have preached to you, let him be accursed" (Galatians 1:8). Lovingly explain that the gospel of grace needs no new revelation added to it.
>
> *Oneness* teachers rightly insist that God is one, but they deny that the Father, Son, and Spirit are distinct persons. Read Matthew 3:16–17, where the Son is baptized, the Spirit descends, and the Father speaks from heaven, all at the same moment. Read Jesus' prayer: "O Father, glorify Me together with Yourself, with the glory which I had with You before the world was" (John 17:5).
>
> Do not try to win in one visit. Ask good questions, listen, give them a Gospel of John to read, and invite them back. Many who left these groups say it was a patient Christian friend, not a clever argument, who opened the door.

(About 390 words.)

## Errors or problems found

1. **Unit 1 [b018]: The Ice-Box Cookie story is missing its beginning.** The paragraph opens with "He had thought of everything but one thing. He had hidden the tray…" and nothing says who "he" is or what happened. [b017] says "Let me tell you a story," but the story itself seems to have been lost. Please restore the opening (a boy, cookies, a hidden tray, presumably). [b019] depends on it.
2. **Not NKJV:** Unit 1 [b004] and [b009] quote 1 Peter 3:15 in another translation ("revere Christ as Lord… gentleness and respect"). Unit 1 [b019] quotes Romans 1:20 the same way ("God's invisible qualities — his eternal power and divine nature"). Unit 10 [b006] and Unit 2 [b027] already give the NKJV wording. Caution: "gentleness and respect" may appear in the Unit 1 exam. If so, keep that phrase in the teaching text, add the NKJV quotation, and note that "meekness and fear" means the same thing.
3. **Not NKJV (smaller):** Unit 9 [b014] gives Judges 21:25 in KJV wording ("every man did that which was right"). NKJV reads "everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Unit 6 [b019] quotes Isaiah 53:5 as "crushed for our iniquities," but NKJV says "bruised." Unit 6 [b021] has "This is My Son; listen to Him," but NKJV Luke 9:35 says "This is My beloved Son. Hear Him!" Unit 5 [b024] has "holy men of old," but NKJV 2 Peter 1:21 says "holy men of God."
4. **Unit 2 [b008]: "The Greek raises a statue to an unknown god."** Acts 17:23 says an *altar*. In the same sentence, "The African builds an altar to the sun" may read to African students as a stereotype. Consider "Some peoples build altars to the sun."
5. **Unit 6 [b012]: "before the Romans had even invented crucifixion."** The Romans did not invent crucifixion. Persians, Carthaginians, and others practiced it first, and the Romans adopted it later. Suggest: "centuries before crucifixion was known as a method of execution."
6. **Unit 5 [b008]: "the third trail, the reliability of the documents."** Unit 1 [b030] lists the documents as the *fourth* clue. Suggest "another trail" (check the exams first).
7. **Unit 5 [b010]: Caesar and Homer figures are outdated.** Current counts list about 250 manuscripts of the *Gallic Wars*, most of them late (the "handful," about 10, is the old figure), and well over 1,500 manuscripts and papyri of the *Iliad*. The argument still stands, because the New Testament remains far ahead in both number and earliness, but the wording should be softened ("relatively few early copies… a gap of about nine hundred years"; "Homer's Iliad does better") so that a skeptic cannot easily correct it.
8. **Unit 5 [b018]: Greenleaf story overstated.** Simon Greenleaf was already a committed Christian (an Episcopalian) when he wrote *The Testimony of the Evangelists* (1846). The story that a skeptic challenged him and that he expected to expose the Gospels is not well documented. Suggest presenting him as a master of the rules of evidence who applied them to the Gospels, and dropping "expecting, perhaps, to expose them" and "surprised him" (if no exam item depends on it).
9. **Unit 7 [b010]: "facts that even most skeptical historians grant" includes the empty tomb.** Death by crucifixion and the disciples' belief in the appearances are nearly universal among scholars. The empty tomb has majority support but is more disputed. Suggest "facts that most historians, including many skeptics, grant."
10. **Minor:** Unit 7 [b022] says "pierced through the heart," but John 19:34 says "His side." Paley's watch is told twice (Unit 2 [b015] and Unit 8 [b016]). This may be intentional, but Unit 8 could shorten it and refer back to Unit 2.
