# Style guide: CTS Spanish readers for World Religions

These are full, faithful Spanish translations of public-domain historical English texts, prepared for Chapala Theological Seminary (CTS), a free bilingual seminary in Mexico. Students will read them in place of the English and then take a fill-in-the-blank test written in both languages. Fidelity and clarity matter more than elegance.

## Rules

1. **Translate everything in the assigned English file:** every paragraph, heading, quotation and footnote, in order. Do not summarise, skip, merge or add paragraphs. One English paragraph is one Spanish paragraph.
2. **Keep every page marker exactly as written,** e.g. `{24}`, in the same place in the sentence or as close as Spanish word order allows. Students use them to find passages. Keep footnote markers such as `[1]` or `[Footnote 3: ...]` in place, and translate the footnote's prose. Leave bibliographic data (titles of books and articles, volume and page numbers) in the original language.
3. **Register:** clear, educated, neutral Latin American (Mexican) Spanish. Use "ustedes", not "vosotros". Avoid Spain-only vocabulary. Use full Spanish punctuation (¿?, ¡!) and «» or “” for quotations, consistently.
4. **Historical fidelity:** translate the author's own judgments and period terminology faithfully, without softening or modernising, e.g. "savage" → "salvaje", "lower races" → "razas inferiores", "fetichism" → "fetichismo", "Mohammedan" → "mahometano". Do not add commentary. CTS adds a separate note on historical context at the front of each reader.
5. **Names:** use the standard Spanish forms of well-known names (Heródoto, Platón, Eusebio, Clemente de Alejandría, Orígenes, Agustín, Tomás de Aquino, Buda, Confucio, Zoroastro, Mahoma, Moisés, Job). Keep other proper names as the author wrote them. Keep transliterated religious terms (Brahman, karma, nirvana, Avesta, Upanishads) in their usual Spanish spelling.
6. **Scripture:** when the author quotes the Bible, use the Reina-Valera 1960 wording of that verse if it is clearly the same verse. Otherwise translate.
7. **Greek, Latin, French and German phrases:** leave them as the author wrote them, followed by a Spanish rendering in brackets only if the author gave an English one.
8. **Key terms:** comparative religion → "religión comparada"; the science of religion → "la ciencia de la religión"; applied science → "ciencia aplicada"; pure science → "ciencia pura"; historical science / historic sciences → "ciencia histórica / ciencias históricas"; resemblances → "semejanzas"; differences → "diferencias"; missionary → "misionero"; Logos → "Logos"; morality → "moralidad"; retribution → "retribución"; continuance (of existence) → "continuidad de la existencia" / "existencia continuada"; worship → "culto" or "adoración" as fits.
9. **Test sentences:** a list of the test questions on your text is provided. Where a question's answer comes from a sentence in your text, translate that sentence naturally, using the Spanish answer word given (or one of its listed alternatives), so a student reading the Spanish can find the answer. Do not distort the sentence to force this; if it can't be done naturally, add a note in your final message saying so.
10. **Output:** UTF-8 Markdown. Start with a level-1 heading giving the work and chapter in Spanish. Next, one line in italics: the source (author, English title, chapter and the Project Gutenberg number given), and "Traducción de CTS, 2026". Then the translation. Use plain paragraphs separated by blank lines. Turn the text's own headings into `##` headings. Put block quotations in `>` blocks.
