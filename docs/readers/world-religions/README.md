# World Religions: CTS Spanish readers (draft)

These are CTS's own Spanish translations of the five assigned World Religions readings, so a Spanish-speaking student can do the readings that the revised World Religions test (draft PR #38) is built on. The rights review of 10 Oct 2026 cleared CTS translations of the assigned historical text. The economic rights of these works had expired in Mexico before the 1994 term extension, and Mexico does not restore copyright to expired works. Every author and translator must still be credited.

**Status: draft for bilingual review. Not published.** These files are under `docs/`, so the site does not serve them. Before the revised test is switched on, a bilingual reviewer should check them for fidelity and readability. Each test answer should then be checked against the Spanish text.

## How they were made

- Each translation follows `STYLE.md`. They are full and faithful, paragraph for paragraph, with every printed page marker `{n}` kept so students can find a passage. The authors' period terminology is translated as written, without modernising, so a CTS note on historical standpoint belongs at the front of each reader.
- The sentence behind every test answer was rendered so that the Spanish answer (or a listed alternate) appears in the text.
- Jevons and Carpenter were translated chapter by chapter, under a shared style guide, and spot-checked against the English. The Qur'an suras were translated directly from Rodwell's English, not from the Arabic. They are unnumbered, as in Rodwell, and his footnotes are omitted.
- The Qur'an uses "vosotros" in direct address, the register of Spanish scripture. The prose readers use "ustedes".

## Notes for the reviewer

- Clarke §389: the note's text is missing from the Gutenberg edition; the reader says so.
- Farquhar p. 46: the day of the Chinese government's April prayer request is unreadable in the scan («17 (?)»). The p. 443 footnote is also lost in the scan; the reader cites Mark 8:36.
- Farquhar's Bible quotations use the Reina-Valera 1960 wording; Clarke's, Farquhar's and Palgrave's period polemic is translated as written, per `STYLE.md`.

- Carpenter's John 1:1 keeps «el Logos», with his own note that English Bibles say "Word" («Verbo»). The test accepts both.
- The Herodotus test question puts Greece first, while Carpenter's sentence puts Egypt first. The answer, «Egipto», is the same either way.
- Carpenter VIII has an English typo, "tasted only four years". It is translated as «solo duraba cuatro años».
