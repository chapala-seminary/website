# Psalm 23: Personal Trust

Alexander Maclaren, *The Expositor’s Bible: The Psalms* (1892–94), on Psalm 23. Assigned selection: Maclaren’s commentary and the Psalm in his own English rendering. The interpretations are the author’s.

The world could spare many a large book better than this sunny little psalm. It has dried many tears and supplied the mould into which many hearts have poured their peaceful faith. To suppose that the speaker is the personified nation chills the whole. The tone is too intense not to be the outcome of personal experience, however admissible the application to the nation may be as secondary. No doubt Jehovah is the Shepherd of Israel in several Asaphite psalms and in Jeremiah; but, notwithstanding great authorities, I cannot persuade myself that the voice which comes so straight to the heart did not come from the heart of a brother speaking across the centuries his own personal emotions, which are universal just because they are individual. It is the pure utterance of personal trust in Jehovah, darkened by no fears or complaints and so perfectly at rest that it has nothing more to ask. For the time desire is stilled in satisfaction. One tone, and that the most blessed which can sound in a life, is heard through the whole. It is the psalm of quiet trust.

*Psalm text (23:1–6, Maclaren's English rendering)*

Jehovah is my Shepherd; I do not want. In pastures of fresh grass He leads me; by waters of rest He makes me lie. My soul He refreshes; He guides me in paths of righteousness for His name's sake. Even if I walk in a gorge of gloom, I fear not evil, for Thou art with me; Thy rod and Thy staff—they comfort me. Thou spreadest before me a table in presence of my foes; Thou anointest with oil my head: my cup is overfulness. Only good and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life, and my dwelling shall be in the house of Jehovah for length of days.
