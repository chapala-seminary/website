# Without Holiness No One Will See the Lord

J. C. Ryle (1816–1900), “Holiness,” on Hebrews 12:14, in the editors’ abridged and modernized text, in *Holiness Through the Ages: An Historical Reader of Christian Writers on Holiness*, ed. Robert E. Coleman, Robert A. Danielson and Faith E. Parry (First Fruits Press, 2014, Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 3.0 United States), book XII, chapter 2. Assigned excerpt (selected paragraphs). Scripture quotations in the English are as the editors give them (NIV). The interpretations are the author’s.

“…without holiness, no one will see the Lord,” Hebrews 12:14.

This text opens up a subject of deep importance, that of practical holiness. It suggests a question which demands the attention of all professing Christians. Are we holy? Will we see the Lord? …

To find out what true, practical holiness is, let’s look at the people who God calls holy. A person can go to great lengths and never reach holiness. It is not knowledge: Balaam had that. Nor is it a profession: Judas had that. Nor morality and good outward conduct; the young ruler had that. Yet none of these were holy. …

Holiness is the habit of being one mind with God and agreeing with God’s judgment. This includes hating what He hates and loving what He loves. It means measuring everything by the standard of His Word. This includes striving to be like Jesus. It goes beyond depending on Him, to laboring to have His mind and to be conformed to His image.

Holy people strive after a pure heart. They dread all contamination of spirit and seek to avoid it. They know their hearts are like tinder and will diligently keep clear of the sparks of temptation. Who dares to talk of strength when a man as David can fall?

Holy people fear God. I do not mean the fear of a slave, which is work driven by the fear of punishment. This fear is that of a child, who wishes to please his father because he loves him.

Holy people pursue faithfulness in all aspects of life. … Holy people aim at doing everything well and are ashamed of mediocre work. Like Daniel, they seek to give no fault against themselves except their faith. They strive to be good spouses, parents and children. They are good bosses, employees, friends, citizens, and neighbors; in both public and private.

Holy people follow after spiritual mindedness. They strive to set their hearts entirely on things above and to hold things on earth with a loose hand. They don’t neglect this present life, but they aim to live with their treasures in heaven.

True holiness is a great reality. It is something in a person that can be seen, and felt by those around him. Like light, if it exists, it will show.

I do not say that holiness shuts out indwelling sin. The greatest misery of a holy person is that he carries with him “a body of death.” … Holiness does not come to perfection all at once. … The holiest people have many blemishes and defects. Their life is a continual warfare with sin and the world. … A person may be truly holy and not perfect. But after every allowance, I cannot see how anyone can be called “holy” who willfully allows himself to sin and is not humbled and ashamed of it.

Can holiness save us? No. Our purest works are no better than filthy rags when in the light of God’s holy law. The white robe Jesus offers must be our only righteousness and the Lamb’s book of life our only ticket to heaven. … Then why is holiness so important?

We must be holy because this is the purpose for which Christ came into the world. … So to talk of people being saved from the guilt of sin without being saved from its dominion is to contradict the witness of all scripture. Jesus is a complete Savior. He does not merely take away the guilt of a believer’s sin. He breaks its power.

We must be holy because this is the only sound evidence that we have a saving faith in our Lord Jesus Christ. James warns us about dead faith: a faith which goes no further than a profession and has no impact on a person’s character (James 2:17). But true faith always shows itself by its fruits.

We must be holy, because this is the way to help others. … Our lives … are a silent sermon which all can read. … You may talk to people about the doctrines of the Gospel and few will listen, still fewer will understand. But your life is an argument that none can escape.

I believe there is much harm done by unholy and inconsistent Christians. Such people are among Satan’s best allies. They pull down with their lives what ministers build with their lips. …

Lastly, we must be holy because holiness on earth prepares us to enjoy heaven. … To reach the holiday of glory, we must pass through the training school of grace.

You may object to the holy life because you would stand out. So it should. Christ’s true servants are always unlike the world around them. … He always said that people must take up their cross and be ready to sacrifice everything if they would be His disciples. Like all other areas of life, that which costs nothing is worth nothing.

It is sometimes forgotten that God married justification and sanctification. They are distinct and different but one is never without the other. Don’t tell me you’re justified, unless you have some marks of sanctification. Don’t boast of Christ’s work for you, unless you can show the Spirit’s work in you.

A word of advice to all who desire to be holy, begin with Christ. You will make no progress until you feel your sin and run to Him. He is the root and beginning of holiness. … Do you want to attain holiness? Then go to Christ and wait for nothing or nobody. Don’t get ready. Just go and cling to Him. … Would you remain holy? Then abide in Christ. He says, “If a man remains in me and I in him, he will bear much fruit; apart from me you can do nothing,” (John 15:5).
